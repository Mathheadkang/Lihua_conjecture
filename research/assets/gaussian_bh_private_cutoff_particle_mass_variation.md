## Statement

Use the approved [private-smoothed directing population](../definitions/gaussian_bh_private_smoothed_directing_population.md), [signed near-active conventions](../definitions/gaussian_bh_signed_near_active_gradient_cones.md) and [marked-array conventions](../definitions/gaussian_bh_marked_exchangeable_parameter_array.md). Here $S=\overline\Phi$, $\phi$ is the standard normal density, $m=S/\phi$, and $s$ denotes private smoothing, with all tests one-sided. The approved [complete signed variation](gaussian_bh_private_noise_cutoff_signed_transport_variation.md) and [marked-array compactification](gaussian_bh_exchangeable_array_variational_compactification.md) are prerequisites, including their actual largest non-strict rank, full FDP and endpoint recovery. All laws and perturbations are chosen before Gaussian innovations.

Let $\pi,\nu$ be any lawful deterministic directing laws of finite second mean moment, embedded if necessary into a common larger separable Hilbert space. Thus $\ell=1$ implies $\theta\le0$ and $\ell=0$ implies $\theta\ge0$. For fixed $\rho\ge0$ and finite $\lambda$, put
$$
\pi_\epsilon=(1-\epsilon\rho)\pi+\epsilon\rho\nu,
\qquad q_\epsilon=q(1+\epsilon\lambda).
$$
Use sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$ such that $\epsilon\rho<\min(a/2,1)$, $\epsilon|\lambda|\le1/2$ and $q_\epsilon\le q_{\max}$; at $q=q_{\max}$ this requires $\lambda\le0$. A dot denotes the right derivative at zero along this fixed joint perturbation. Subscripts $\epsilon$ refer to the perturbed population and level; a subscript $\nu$ denotes the conditional kernel generated by $\nu$ at the same background $X$.

$$
\sup_z|H_{\epsilon,X}(z)-H_X(z)|\le\epsilon\rho,
\qquad \sup_z|V_{\epsilon,X}(z)-V_X(z)|\le\epsilon\rho.
$$
For $a\le r<1$, prove

|V_{\epsilon,X}(Q_{\epsilon,X}(r))-V_X(Q_X(r))|\le2\epsilon\rho.

At $r=1$ use the continuous true-mass endpoint convention, which also satisfies this estimate. For $0<r<1$, prove the derivatives, locally uniformly on compact rank intervals,

\dot Q_X(r)=\rho\frac{H_{\nu,X}(Q_X(r))-H_X(Q_X(r))}{h_X(Q_X(r))},
$$

\dot f_X(r)=\frac\rho r\left[
V_{\nu,X}(Q_X(r))-V_X(Q_X(r))
-\frac{h_{0,X}(Q_X(r))}{h_X(Q_X(r))}

At $r=1$ set $\dot f_X(1)=\rho(\int\ell d\nu-\int\ell d\pi)$; rank one has conditional probability zero. All kernels and chosen versions must be jointly measurable and representative invariant for the stated endpoint family.

2. **Full signed actual-rank derivative.** Use $u_q(r)=S^{-1}(qr)$,

$$
with $B_X(1)=M_X(1)=+\infty$, and use the actual rank $R$ of the approved population. For $0<r<1$, prove
$$
\dot M_X(r)=\frac1\sigma\min_{t\in\mathcal A_X(r)}
\{-\lambda m(u_q(t))-\dot Q_X(t)\}.
$$
With $g_X=\chi_af_X$ and $w_X=g_X'$, prove the complete right derivative
$$
\dot{\mathcal F}^{a}
=\mathbb E_{X,Z}[\chi_a(R)\dot f_X(R)]
-\mathbb E_X\int_a^1w_X(r)\phi(M_X(r))\dot M_X(r)\,dr.

The first integrand is zero on $R\le a$ and on the rank-one null exceptional set. The formula retains both signed bulk and rank terms and every competing suffix minimum. Prove the envelope limit and all interchanges; value continuity alone is insufficient.

## Proof

We use the actual-rank and deterministic endpoint-recovery conclusions of the approved [complete signed variation](gaussian_bh_private_noise_cutoff_signed_transport_variation.md), and the directing maximum selection in the approved [marked-array compactification](gaussian_bh_exchangeable_array_variational_compactification.md). The compact minimum argument and all mass-variation interchanges are proved below. Write $d=\epsilon\rho$, suppress $X$ in conditional calculations, and set $g(0)=0$ and $g(r)=\chi_a(r)f(r)$ for positive ranks.

### 1. Simultaneous versions and actual ranks

Embed the two Hilbert spaces into their separable orthogonal sum, preserving norms and inner products, and use one isonormal series there. Its partial sums, convergence set and zero extension are jointly measurable. For each deterministic loading the series converges almost surely; Fubini for each of $\pi,\nu$ supplies one background set of probability one on which their centers are finite almost everywhere under both laws. Work on this set. Because $s\le c_s(v)\le\sqrt{1-\sigma^2}$, every score derivative of a Gaussian kernel is bounded by a constant depending only on its order and $s$. Differentiation under each parameter integral is therefore valid. Both total tails are smooth and strictly decreasing from one to zero, with positive densities. Their true densities satisfy $0\le h_0\le h$. Define every perturbed kernel, for all allowed $\epsilon$ at once, by the affine combinations of these two kernels. This avoids parameter-dependent exceptional sets.

A strictly decreasing continuous tail has a jointly measurable inverse: for example $\{Q_\epsilon(r)<z\}=\{H_\epsilon(z)<r\}$. The kernels and inverses are jointly measurable in background, perturbation and rank. Suffix infima can be taken over rational $t>r$ below one, with $B_\epsilon(r)$ also included; continuity gives the same value as the full infimum. These descriptions give joint measurability of $M_\epsilon$. The endpoint exclusion in part 3 below gives finite attained minima. Equivalently a positive qualifier is $B_\epsilon(t)\le Z$. The largest qualifying rank is attained whenever positive: on any interval bounded away from zero the qualifier set is closed, and near one it is empty because $B_\epsilon(t)\to+\infty$. Thus, simultaneously for every allowed $\epsilon$, every $0<r<1$ and finite $Z$,
$$
\{R_\epsilon(Z)\ge r\}=\{Z\ge M_\epsilon(r)\},\qquad
P_\epsilon(r):=\mathbb P_Z(R_\epsilon\ge r)=S(M_\epsilon(r)).
$$
In particular these are the actual globally largest non-strict ranks. Their full fractional payoffs are $f_\epsilon(R_\epsilon)$ by the cited prerequisite. No finite $Z$ gives $R_\epsilon=1$: the total tail at the finite score $u_{q_\epsilon}(1)-\sigma Z$ is strictly below one, and continuity excludes qualifiers sufficiently close to one as well. We set $P_\epsilon(1)=0$.

Two representatives of the same isonormal evaluations agree almost surely at each deterministic loading. Another application of Fubini makes their kernels agree for both laws, simultaneously at every score by continuity; hence all affine mixtures, quantiles, ranks and payoffs agree. The identical argument works for any stated countable collection of deterministic endpoint and recovery laws, by intersecting the resulting probability-one sets. Isometric changes of Hilbert presentation are coupled by the corresponding isonormal process. Parameter choices and all recovery laws remain independent of the innovations.

### 2. Fixed-rank mass calculus

Affineness gives $H_\epsilon-H=d(H_\nu-H)$ and $V_\epsilon-V=d(V_\nu-V)$, so both uniform tail errors are at most $d$. If $z_\epsilon=Q_\epsilon(r)$ and $z=Q(r)$, then $H_\epsilon(z_\epsilon)=H(z)=r$ and $|H(z_\epsilon)-H(z)|\le d$. Since $0\le h_0\le h$, integration between these two scores gives
$$
|V(z_\epsilon)-V(z)|\le |H(z_\epsilon)-H(z)|\le d,
\qquad |v_\epsilon(r)-v(r)|\le2d.
$$
This holds for all interior ranks. At rank one $v_\epsilon(1)$ is the affine true mass and its error is at most $d$.

On a compact rank interval $I\subset(0,1)$, the uniform tail convergence traps all $Q_\epsilon(I)$ in a fixed compact score interval and implies uniform convergence to $Q(I)$. On that score interval $h$ has a positive minimum; $h_\epsilon$ converges uniformly to $h$. Implicit differentiation, first at small positive perturbation parameter and then continuously at zero, gives
$$
\dot Q(r)=\rho\frac{H_\nu(Q(r))-H(Q(r))}{h(Q(r))}.
$$
Indeed the derivative at parameter $v$ has the same numerator evaluated at $Q_v(r)$ and denominator $h_v(Q_v(r))$. Its uniform continuity as $v\downarrow0$ on $I$ proves uniform convergence of the quantile difference quotients. Differentiating the affine true tail at this quantile, whose score derivative is $-h_0$, now yields locally uniformly
$$
\dot f(r)=\frac\rho r\left[V_\nu(Q(r))-V(Q(r))-
\frac{h_0(Q(r))}{h(Q(r))}\{H_\nu(Q(r))-H(Q(r))\}\right].
$$
At rank one differentiation of the true mass gives the asserted $\rho(\int\ell\,d\nu-\int\ell\,d\pi)$.

Also $v'(r)=h_0(Q(r))/h(Q(r))\in[0,1]$, while $0\le v(r)\le r$. Consequently $0\le f\le1$ and $|f'(r)|=|v'(r)-f(r)|/r\le1/r$. The true-mass limit as $r\uparrow1$ shows that $g$ has its stated continuous endpoint. Its locally continuous derivative extends integrably to one, so the fundamental theorem on compact intervals, followed by this endpoint limit, gives absolute continuity on $[a,1]$. Writing $w=g'$, we obtain
$$
|w|\le W_a,\qquad \int_a^1|w(r)|\,dr
\le\int_a^1\chi_a'(r)\,dr+\int_a^1\frac{dr}{r}\le T_a.
$$
The same assertions hold for each perturbed payoff. The fixed-rank error proves the global bound
$$
\left|\frac{g_\epsilon(r)-g(r)}\epsilon\right|\le\frac{2\rho}{a}
\quad(0\le r\le1),\qquad |\dot f(r)|\le\frac{2\rho}{r}\quad(0<r<1).
$$
On $r\le a$ the first difference is identically zero. More precisely its bound for $r>a$ is $2\rho\chi_a(r)/r$, which will handle the cutoff boundary.

### 3. Uniform exclusion of the terminal endpoint

For every fixed background and finite $K>0$, put $t_K=\max\{H(-K),H_\nu(-K)\}<1$. For $t>t_K$, every affine mixture satisfies $H_\epsilon(-K)<t$, hence $Q_\epsilon(t)<-K$. Equivalently $Q_\epsilon(t)\le\max\{Q(t),Q_\nu(t)\}$. Thus $Q_\epsilon(t)\to-\infty$ as $t\uparrow1$, uniformly even over mixture weights in $[0,1]$. All intermediate allowed levels have $u_{q_\epsilon}(t)\ge1$, so $B_\epsilon(t)\to+\infty$ uniformly in the small perturbation interval.

Fix $0<c\le b<1$ and choose $t_0\in(b,1)$. Part 2 bounds $B_\epsilon(t_0)$ uniformly for small $\epsilon$. The preceding divergence therefore excludes all ranks sufficiently close to one from every suffix minimizer with lower endpoint in $[c,b]$. All such minimizers lie in a common compact interval $[c,k]$, $k<1$, and exist by continuity. On this interval part 2 and ordinary differentiation of the inverse Gaussian tail give the uniform expansion
$$
B_\epsilon(t)=B(t)+\epsilon b_*(t)+o(\epsilon),\qquad
b_*(t)=\frac{-\lambda m(u_q(t))-\dot Q(t)}\sigma.
$$
Here $b_*$ is continuous. Constants in this localization can depend on the fixed background; none will be used for outer domination.

### 4. Every active minimum in the directional envelope

Fix $0<r<1$ and use the preceding compact localization for its suffix. Let $A=\mathcal A(r)$, a nonempty compact set, and choose $t_*\in A$ minimizing $b_*$ on $A$. Evaluation at $t_*$ gives
$$
\limsup_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\frac{M_\epsilon(r)-M(r)}\epsilon\le\min_{t\in A}b_*(t).
$$
For any perturbed minimizer $t_\epsilon$ in the same compact interval, uniform value convergence implies $B(t_\epsilon)\to M(r)$. Every convergent subsequence therefore has its limit in $A$. Moreover
$$
\frac{M_\epsilon(r)-M(r)}\epsilon
=\frac{B(t_\epsilon)-M(r)}\epsilon+b_*(t_\epsilon)+o(1)
\ge b_*(t_\epsilon)+o(1).
$$
If the lower limit were smaller than $\min_A b_*$, choose a sequence realizing that strict inequality and then a convergent subsequence of its minimizers. Continuity of $b_*$ and membership of the limit in $A$ give a contradiction. Thus the lower limit has the opposite bound and
$$
\dot M(r)=\min_{t\in\mathcal A(r)}b_*(t)
=\frac1\sigma\min_{t\in\mathcal A(r)}\{-\lambda m(u_q(t))-\dot Q(t)\}.
$$
This is an elementary compact minimizer sandwich, written here without using the compact Danskin analogue as a black box. It keeps all ties and does not assert linearity in a reversed direction. Measurability of the resulting minimum follows from its difference-quotient limit. The argument holds at every interior rank on the common good background set. In particular
$$
\frac{P_\epsilon(r)-P(r)}\epsilon\longrightarrow-\phi(M(r))\dot M(r).
$$

### 5. Qualifier transfer for the actual globally largest ranks

The continuous derivative $u_q'(t)=-q/\phi(u_q(t))$ is bounded in magnitude by the finite $L_{q,a}$ on $[a/2,1]$. Along $q_v=q(1+v\lambda)$,
$$
\frac{d}{dv}u_{q_v}(t)=-\frac{\lambda}{1+v\lambda}m(u_{q_v}(t)).
$$
For $u\ge1$, $m(u)=\int_0^\infty e^{-ux-x^2/2}\,dx$ is decreasing. Every intermediate $u_{q_v}(t)\ge1$ and $1+v\lambda\ge1/2$, giving
$$
|u_q(t\pm d)-u_q(t)|\le dL_{q,a},\qquad
|u_{q_\epsilon}(t)-u_q(t)|\le2\epsilon|\lambda|m(1)
$$
whenever the ranks in the first bound lie in $[a/2,1]$. Set $C=\rho L_{q,a}+2|\lambda|m(1)$, so $\sigma\Delta_z=\epsilon C$.

For the left bracket assume $r+d<1$ and $R_0(Z-\Delta_z)\ge r+d$. Its attained largest qualifier is some $t\ge r+d$, $t<1$. Put $y=t-d\ge r$. Then
$$
u_{q_\epsilon}(y)-\sigma Z\le u_q(t)-\sigma(Z-\Delta_z).
$$
Since the tails decrease and $H_\epsilon\ge H-d$, the perturbed tail at the left score is at least $t-d=y$. Thus $y$ qualifies and $R_\epsilon(Z)\ge r$. If $r+d=1$ the left event is empty by the rank-one exclusion; if $r+d>1$ it is defined to be empty.

For the right bracket an attained perturbed qualifier $t=R_\epsilon(Z)\ge r$ has $t<1$ and $y=t-d\ge a-d>a/2$. Now
$$
u_q(y)-\sigma(Z+\Delta_z)\le u_{q_\epsilon}(t)-\sigma Z.
$$
Using $H\ge H_\epsilon-d$ makes $y$ a base qualifier at $Z+\Delta_z$. Therefore $R_0(Z+\Delta_z)\ge r-d$. These transfers use non-strict inequalities throughout and do not select a local crossing. When $\rho=0$ the same score comparison transfers the qualifier without a rank shift; when $C=0$ it is the identity. This proves both asserted indicator brackets for every finite $Z$.

### 6. Closed-interval absolute continuity of survival

Fix a compact interval $[a/2,b]$ below one. Part 3 confines all its base suffix minimizers to $[a/2,k]$, $k<1$, where $B$ is continuously differentiable with finite Lipschitz constant $L$. If $r_1<r_2\le b$, then $M(r_1)\le M(r_2)$. If a minimizer at $r_1$ is at least $r_2$, equality holds. Otherwise such a minimizer $t$ lies in $[r_1,r_2)$ and
$$
0\le M(r_2)-M(r_1)\le B(r_2)-B(t)\le L(r_2-r_1).
$$
Hence $M$, and therefore $P=S\circ M$, are locally Lipschitz below one. The divergence of $B(t)$ at one implies $M(r)\to+\infty$ and $P(r)\to0$. Since $P$ is nonincreasing, its compact-interval fundamental-theorem identities imply
$$
\int_{a/2}^1|P'(r)|\,dr
=\lim_{b\uparrow1}\{P(a/2)-P(b)\}=P(a/2)\le1.
$$
Letting $b\uparrow1$ in those identities proves the integral representation through the endpoint; thus $P$ is absolutely continuous on $[a/2,1]$, not merely continuous there. Extend it constantly below $a/2$ and by zero above one. This extension is locally absolutely continuous on the real line and has derivative in $L^1(\mathbb R)$, with total variation at most one. There is no terminal atom or singular remainder.

### 7. Translation domination, uniform integrability and conditional L1 convergence

Integrating the brackets in $Z$ and using the Lipschitz constant $1/\sqrt{2\pi}$ of $S$ gives
$$
P_\epsilon(r)\le P(r-d)+\frac{\epsilon C}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}},\qquad
P_\epsilon(r)\ge P(r+d)-\frac{\epsilon C}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}.
$$
The lower bound remains valid for $r+d\ge1$ by the zero extension. Consequently, with $J_\epsilon(r)=(P_\epsilon(r)-P(r))/\epsilon$,
$$
|J_\epsilon(r)|\le
\frac{P(r-d)-P(r)}\epsilon+
\frac{P(r)-P(r+d)}\epsilon+
\frac{2C}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
\quad(a\le r<1).
$$
When $\rho>0$, writing $F=|P'|\in L^1(\mathbb R)$, the two horizontal terms are
$$
\frac\rho d\int_0^d F(r-v)\,dv,\qquad
\frac\rho d\int_0^d F(r+v)\,dv.
$$
To establish their uniform integrability, choose a bounded integrable truncation $F_N=\min(F,N)$ with $\|F-F_N\|_1$ arbitrarily small. Each translation average of $F_N$ is bounded by $N$. For any measurable rank set $E$, Tonelli bounds the integral over $E$ of a translation average of $F-F_N$ by $\|F-F_N\|_1$. Thus each horizontal term has integral over $E$ bounded by
$$
\rho N|E|+\rho\|F-F_N\|_1.
$$
The bound holds uniformly over all $d>0$ in question and proves uniform absolute continuity of these integrals and their bounded total integrals. Together with the constant term this gives uniform integrability of $J_\epsilon$ on the finite interval $[a,1]$. If $\rho=0$, the horizontal terms vanish and the constant bound suffices.

Part 4 gives pointwise convergence at every interior rank to $J=-\phi(M)\dot M$. Fatou makes $J$ integrable. For completeness, the just-proved uniform integrability also gives uniformly small tails $\int_{|J_\epsilon|>K}|J_\epsilon|$ as $K\to\infty$: their sets have uniformly small measure by the uniform L1 bound, and uniform absolute continuity applies. The same holds for $J$ by its integrability. Clip $J_\epsilon$ and $J$ to $[-K,K]$; the clipped functions converge pointwise and bounded convergence gives convergence in L1. The two tail estimates then yield
$$
\|J_\epsilon+\phi(M)\dot M\|_{L^1(a,1)}\longrightarrow0.
$$
Only after this convergence do we multiply by the bounded signed function $w$ and integrate. In particular the resulting conditional rank derivative is $-\int_a^1w\phi(M)\dot M\,dr$, with an absolutely integrable conditional integrand.

### 8. Moving payoff and moving rank law

For every rank $t$, absolute continuity and $g(a)=0$ give $g(t)=\int_a^1w(r)\mathbf1_{\{t\ge r\}}dr$, with zero when $t\le a$. Signed Fubini is valid because $\int|w|\le T_a$. Thus the exact conditional difference separates as
$$
\mathbb E_Z[g_\epsilon(R_\epsilon)-g(R)]
=\mathbb E_Z[(g_\epsilon-g)(R_\epsilon)]
+\int_a^1w(r)[P_\epsilon(r)-P(r)]\,dr.
$$
To handle the first term, $Z\mapsto R_0(Z)$ is bounded and nondecreasing and therefore has at most countably many discontinuities. At a continuity point $Z$, the right bracket implies $R_\epsilon(Z)\le R_0(Z+\Delta_z)+d$ whenever $R_\epsilon\ge a$. If $R_0(Z)>a$, the left bracket applied to any fixed $a\le r<R_0(Z)$ gives eventually $R_\epsilon\ge r$. The right bracket and continuity give the matching upper limit. Hence $R_\epsilon\to R_0$ in this case. If $R_0(Z)<a$, the right bracket makes $R_\epsilon\ge a$ impossible eventually, so its cutoff vanishes. If $R_0(Z)=a$, the same upper bound gives $\limsup R_\epsilon\le a$; the refined bound $2\rho\chi_a(R_\epsilon)/R_\epsilon$ above $a$ tends to zero. Rank one is absent for finite $Z$ by part 1.

At the remaining $Z$, with $a<R_0(Z)<1$, local uniform fixed-rank difference-quotient convergence from part 2 gives
$$
\frac{(g_\epsilon-g)(R_\epsilon)}\epsilon
\longrightarrow\chi_a(R_0)\dot f(R_0).
$$
The global bound $2\rho/a$ permits conditional dominated convergence. Excluding the countable discontinuity set costs zero Gaussian probability. Combining this bulk limit with part 7 proves the complete conditional signed derivative.

### 9. Background-independent domination and outer expectation

The translation formulas and $\|P'\|_1\le1$ give, for each horizontal term in part 7, integral over $[a,1]$ at most $\rho$. Therefore
$$
\int_a^1 |w(r)|\,|J_\epsilon(r)|\,dr
\le 2\rho W_a+\frac{2T_aC}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}.
$$
Together with the bulk estimate, the exact decomposition in part 8 proves
$$
\left|\frac{\mathbb E_Z g_\epsilon(R_\epsilon)-\mathbb E_Zg(R)}\epsilon\right|
\le\frac{2\rho}{a}+2\rho W_a+
\frac{2T_a}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\{\rho L_{q,a}+2|\lambda|m(1)\}.
$$
This constant is independent of $X,\pi,\nu$. Dominated convergence of the entire conditional quotient in $X$ now proves the derivative of $\mathcal F^a$. The two terms are separately integrable: the bulk has absolute value bounded by $2\rho/a$, and Fatou, or the conditional L1 limit above, gives
$$
\int_a^1|w(r)\phi(M(r))\dot M(r)|\,dr
\le2\rho W_a+\frac{2T_aC}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
$$
for each good background. Joint measurability was established in part 1 and by the derivative limits. Thus Fubini and the outer expectation are legitimate, giving exactly
$$
\dot{\mathcal F}^a
=\mathbb E_{X,Z}[\chi_a(R)\dot f_X(R)]
-\mathbb E_X\int_a^1w_X(r)\phi(M_X(r))\dot M_X(r)\,dr.
$$
Assign zero to the bulk on $R\le a$ and on the rank-one null set. No signed component or active minimum has been discarded.

### 10. Diagnostic special cases

If $H_\nu=H$ and $\lambda=0$, the total kernel and every actual rank remain fixed. The true kernel is affine, so the derivative is exactly the affine bulk response and $\dot M=0$. If both laws are entirely true, $V_\epsilon=H_\epsilon$, $f_\epsilon=1$, and both terms in the bracket for $\dot f$ cancel; the derivative has only its cutoff rank-distribution term. If the base true sector is empty, $V=0$, $g=w=0$ and $V_\epsilon=\epsilon\rho V_\nu$. Its fixed-rank response is $\dot f(r)=\rho V_\nu(Q(r))/r$, and part 8 gives the bulk-only derivative at the unperturbed actual rank. These are direct algebraic consequences, independent of any counterexample-search observations.

### 11. Actual extrema and normalization
