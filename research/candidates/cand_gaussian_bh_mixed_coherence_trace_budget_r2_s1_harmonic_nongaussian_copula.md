## Object

For every $0<q\le\overline\Phi(3)$, set $N=2$ and $\theta=(0,0)$. Let $J$ take values $A,B,C,E$ with probabilities $q/2,q/2,q/2,1-3q/2$, respectively. Conditional on $J$, draw $P_1,P_2$ independently with the following uniform laws on open intervals:

| $J$ | $P_1$ | $P_2$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $A$ | $\operatorname{Unif}(0,q/2)$ | $\operatorname{Unif}(q,1)$ |
| $B$ | $\operatorname{Unif}(q,1)$ | $\operatorname{Unif}(0,q/2)$ |
| $C$ | $\operatorname{Unif}(q/2,q)$ | $\operatorname{Unif}(q/2,q)$ |
| $E$ | $\operatorname{Unif}(q,1)$ | $\operatorname{Unif}(q,1)$ |

Put $T_i=\overline\Phi^{-1}(P_i)$. This is an attempted import of the arbitrary-dependence harmonic-extremal mechanism, with normal marginal observations supplied by the inverse survival transform. It is not an antipodal Gaussian model, nor a parameter change of a family on the board: its dependence is generated by a discrete mixture whose support has forbidden open rectangles.

The literature motivation is the sharpness of the arbitrary-dependence BH harmonic correction in Guo and Rao, *On control of the false discovery rate under no assumption of dependency*, Journal of Statistical Planning and Inference 138 (2008), 3176–3188, DOI 10.1016/j.jspi.2008.01.003. Their [author abstract](https://people.stat.sc.edu/Hitchcock/NPCposters/Guo.Wenge.pdf) states attainment of the arbitrary-dependence bound. The explicit continuous-marginal object above is specified independently here; no claim is made that it is their exact published construction.

## Checks

### H1

Pass: finite dimension/count and allowed level/means. The object has $N=2$, a real mean vector $\theta=(0,0)$, and can be chosen at every arbitrarily small $q>0$. Since $q\le\overline\Phi(3)<1/2$, every displayed mixture weight is strictly positive. Thus it is not excluded by any sufficiently small universal $q_0$ on grounds of level alone. Whether a finite Gaussian latent representation exists is checked next.

### H2

Fail: there is no jointly Gaussian representation $T_i=\theta_i+v_i\cdot G$ with unit vectors $v_i$ in any finite dimension.

First each $P_i$ is uniform on $(0,1)$. For $P_1$, the masses on $(0,q/2)$ and $(q/2,q)$ are $q/2$ each, and the mass on $(q,1)$ is

$$
q/2+(1-3q/2)=1-q.
$$

The conditional densities on their intervals are constant, so the marginal density equals one on all three intervals. The argument for $P_2$ is symmetric. Therefore each $T_i$ is individually $N(0,1)$, and failure is specifically joint Gaussianity.

Suppose the pair were jointly Gaussian and let its correlation be $\rho\in[-1,1]$. If $|\rho|<1$, its bivariate normal density is strictly positive throughout $\mathbb R^2$. Consequently the open event

$$
\{0<P_1<q/2,\quad q/2<P_2<q\}
$$

would have strictly positive probability: its inverse-normal image is a nonempty open rectangle in $\mathbb R^2$. But the displayed mixture gives this event probability zero. Only state $A$ can have $P_1<q/2$, and there $P_2>q$.

If $\rho=1$, zero means and unit variances give $T_2=T_1$ almost surely, hence $P_2=P_1$ almost surely. State $A$, of positive probability, contradicts this since its two intervals are disjoint.

If $\rho=-1$, then $T_2=-T_1$ almost surely, hence $P_2=1-P_1$ almost surely. State $C$, of probability $q/2>0$, contradicts this: both $P_i<q<1/2$, so $P_1+P_2<1$.

These exhaust all possible bivariate Gaussian correlations, including singular covariances. No allowed unit-vector Gaussian model realizes this copula.
