## Object

This is a family for every integer N >= 2, every integer 1 <= k < N, and every 0 < q <= S(1). Set d=1. The first k coordinates have mean zero and direction +1. For j=1,...,N-k, give coordinate k+j direction -1 and mean 2u_{k+j}, where u_r=S^{-1}(qr/N). Thus the true-null set is precisely {1,...,k}. Use all N physical p-values, ordinary non-strict BH, and the full forced-rank mask in the target. All model parameters may depend on q.

The proposed failure mechanism was simultaneous translated rank cells at the common boundary y=0: every rank r>=k occurs there for each of the k null coordinates.

## Checks

### H1

N and d=1 are finite positive integers. The selected k is an integer in the prescribed range.

### H2

Every direction is a deterministic unit vector in R. The covariance is its Gram matrix and is positive semidefinite with unit diagonal, although singular. The first k means are zero. All remaining means are strictly positive because u_r>=u_N>=1. Consequently the null labels are exactly the first k coordinates, and alternatives are admissible. The scores are generated by the same scalar standard Gaussian.

### H3

The family uses 0<q<=S(1), all physical multiplicities, and the target's non-strict ordinary BH thresholds. No p-value is removed or renormalized.

### H4

For each null coordinate i, its translation is z=u_r+y, and only its own p-value is forced to zero. Consider y>=0.

If r<k, all k null coordinates meet the rank-k threshold because their original score u_r+y exceeds u_k; the forced coordinate does also. Thus R_i(z)>=k>r.

If r>k and y>0, the alternative in position r-k in the decreasing-mean alternative list has score 2u_r-z=u_r-y<u_r. At most r-k-1 alternatives meet the rank-r threshold. Including all k null coordinates, at most r-1 p-values meet it. Hence BH cannot have rank exactly r. This uses the necessary fact that rank r requires at least r p-values below its threshold; a possible larger BH rank does not change that conclusion.

If r=k, the k null coordinates meet the rank-k threshold. For any s>k, the alternative in position s-k has score 2u_s-u_k-y<u_s. At most s-k-1 alternatives and k nulls meet the rank-s threshold, so rank s cannot qualify. Therefore R_i(u_k+y)=k for every y>=0.

Finally, at y=0 and r>k, rank r qualifies: all k nulls and the first r-k alternatives meet its threshold. For each s>r, the alternative in position s-k has score 2u_s-u_r<u_s, so s does not qualify. Thus R_i(u_r)=r. The other ranks are confined to this single translated boundary point.

It follows that, for Lebesgue-almost every y,
$$
H_q(y)=\mathbf 1\{y\ge0\}\exp(-u_k y-u_k^2/2).
$$
The k physical null copies contribute exactly k/k=1 to the surviving rank-k term. No mask has been optimized or selected independently.

### H5

Use precisely tau=1/L, L=log(1/q), and s=sqrt(1-exp(-2/L)) from the target. For q<=S(1), L>=1. The Mehler variable has an everywhere positive smooth density for every finite x, so its expectation is unchanged by the extra singleton-boundary values found above.

### NOT

Fail: neither conclusion is violated by this family, even when N and k vary arbitrarily with q.

First, the translated mass is S(u_k)=qk/N. In the original model, a true rejection at any final rank r requires z>=u_r. For r<k this entails rank k qualifying, contradicting final rank r. For r>k the alternative in position r-k must meet the rank-r threshold, so z<=u_r; true rejection then forces the equality z=u_r, a probability-zero event. For r=k, true rejections occur precisely when z>=u_k, all k nulls are rejected, and no larger rank qualifies by the argument in H4. Therefore the actual FDR is S(u_k)=qk/N, agreeing with the mass identity.

Second, writing m=exp(-tau)x and u=u_k>=1, the normal density bound gives uniformly over m
$$
\mathbb E[H_q(m+sG)]
\le \frac{\exp(-u^2/2)}{\sqrt{2\pi}s}
\int_0^\infty e^{-uy}\,dy
=\frac{\phi(u)}{su}.
$$
The elementary Mills bound S(u)>=phi(u)u/(u^2+1) yields
$$
\frac{\phi(u)}{su}
\le\frac{S(u)}s(1+u^{-2})
\le\frac{2qk}{Ns}.
$$
Concavity of t -> 1-exp(-2t) on [0,1] implies
$$
s^2=1-e^{-2/L}\ge(1-e^{-2})/L.
$$
Consequently the full universally varying family satisfies
$$
\sup_x\mathbb E[H_q(e^{-\tau}x+sG)]
\le\frac{2}{\sqrt{1-e^{-2}}}\,q\sqrt L.
$$
This supplies a single universal constant for all N,k,q in this family and therefore cannot violate the existential universal-constant statement in part (b).

## Failure

NOT fails: simultaneous opposite-direction ladder cells occur only on the translated equality boundary, which Mehler smoothing ignores; the surviving rank-k kernel obeys a uniform constant times q sqrt(log(1/q)).
