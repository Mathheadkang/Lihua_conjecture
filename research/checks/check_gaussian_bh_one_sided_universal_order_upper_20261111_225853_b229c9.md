- Obstruction: Every admissible original one-sided true null has a superuniform marginal p-value, including negative null means and singular joint covariance. Writing $z_s=\overline\Phi^{-1}(s)$, for $\theta_i\le0$ one has $\mathbb P(P_i\le s)=\overline\Phi(z_s-\theta_i)\le s$. Consequently the original null count satisfies $\mathbb E[C(s)]\le ms$, where $m=|I_0|$; no sign or independence assumption on covariance is used.
- Obstruction: This marginal statement gives the finite-model harmonic bound $\operatorname{FDR}\le q(m/N)H_N$, with $H_N=\sum_{r=1}^N1/r$, for the same actual BH rank and original null labels. Indeed, the deterministic BH contribution inequality is $\operatorname{FDP}\le C(q)/N+\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}C(qr/N)(1/r-1/(r+1))$; substitution of $\mathbb E[C(s)]\le ms$ gives the bound. This is the marginal Benjamini-Yekutieli scale, not the desired dimension-uniform improvement.
- Obstruction: A refutation of the stated existence of an arbitrary finite universal constant requires an admissible sequence $q_n\downarrow0$ and finite Gaussian models for which $\operatorname{FDR}_n/[q_n\sqrt{\log(1/q_n)}]\to\infty$ after taking a subsequence. The harmonic bound forces $(m_n/N_n)H_{N_n}/\sqrt{\log(1/q_n)}\to\infty$ along that subsequence. In particular $\log N_n/\sqrt{\log(1/q_n)}$ must be unbounded; families with $\log N=O(\sqrt{\log(1/q)})$ cannot refute the order assertion. An isolated example with FDR exceeding $q$, or exceeding a chosen fixed multiple of the proposed scale, does not refute existence of some larger universal constant.
- Obstruction: Fixed dimension and a uniformly bounded number of original true nulls are excluded even if the remaining model parameters vary with $q$. For $m\ge1$, monotonicity of $H_N/N$ gives $(m/N)H_N\le H_m$, so $\operatorname{FDR}\le qH_m$. A bounded $m$ therefore makes the normalized ratio tend to zero as $q\downarrow0$. If $m=0$, FDP is identically zero.
- Obstruction: Suppose the original null covariance block has $\Sigma_{ij}\le0$ for distinct $i,j\in I_0$, and the entire alternative block is independent of the null vector. Conditioning on all alternative p-values leaves the Gaussian null mean vector and covariance block unchanged. Because every original null mean is nonpositive and every original marginal variance is one, $F_{i,\mathcal H}(s)\le s$ and the approved conditional sub-Poisson helper has $K=1$. Thus the original ordinary BH procedure satisfies $\operatorname{FDR}\le\min\{1,4eq\}$, uniformly in all dimensions, null counts, negative null means, singularities, and parameters within this subclass. Its normalized ratio is at most $4e/\sqrt{\log(1/q)}$, so this subclass cannot refute the main order assertion. The application is already saved as draft idea_52b05be9e378; no duplicate idea is created.
- Obstruction: A saturation comparison removes the independence requirement at an explicit probability cost, while retaining nonpositive covariance within the original null block. Write $a=|I_0^c|$. For $a\ge1$ define the sufficient good event $A=\{\max_{j\notin I_0}P_j\le qa/N\}$ and the exact bad-event cost $\delta=\mathbb P(A^c)$. Keep the original null p-values and labels in an auxiliary vector whose alternative p-values are all zero. Its BH rank $R_0$ is at least $a$. Coordinatewise monotonicity of the BH rank gives $R\le R_0$; on $A$, all original alternatives are already below $qa/N\le qR_0/N$, so rank $R_0$ also qualifies in the original procedure, yielding $R=R_0$. The null rejection set and FDP therefore agree on $A$. Since FDP is at most one off $A$, $\operatorname{FDR}_{\rm original}\le\operatorname{FDR}_{\rm auxiliary}+\delta\le4eq+\delta$; the separate bound FDR at most one gives $\operatorname{FDR}_{\rm original}\le\min\{1,4eq+\delta\}$. The auxiliary bound follows from the approved helper with deterministic alternative zeros and $K=1$. This comparison uses no independence between alternatives and nulls and asserts no global monotonicity of FDR under replacing alternatives by zeros.
- Obstruction: The saturation error is the exact union probability $\delta=\mathbb P\{\exists j\notin I_0:T_j<\overline\Phi^{-1}(qa/N)\}$, with the finite-model union bound $\delta\le\sum_{j\notin I_0}\Phi(\overline\Phi^{-1}(qa/N)-\theta_j)$. Here $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF; the unit-variance Gaussian marginals have no atoms at the threshold, even if the joint law is singular. Thus $\delta=O(q)$ gives an $O(q)$ subclass bound; more generally $\delta=O(q\sqrt{\log(1/q)})$ prevents an unbounded normalized ratio. When $a=0$, no saturation event is needed and the all-null nonpositive-covariance case already has the $4eq$ bound. This application is already saved as draft idea_7a957628fee8; no duplicate idea is created.
- Obstruction: For an original null block with nonpositive off-diagonal covariance, the unconditional Gaussian null-count mgf holds with rate $Ns$, regardless of its dependence on the alternatives. With $a=|I_0^c|$, grouping original outcomes by actual false and alternative rejection counts gives the draft bound $\operatorname{FDR}\le\min\{1,2e(a+2)q\}$ in [Bounded correlated alternative counts preserve O(q) FDR under nonpositive null covariance](../ideas/idea_fe556cf8f196.md). Both observations retain arbitrary correlations and unsaturated alternatives; neither assumes that revealing alternatives preserves the null covariance signs. Both saved records remain unchanged.
- Obstruction: The independent geometric argument yields the sharper consequence $\operatorname{FDR}\le\min\{1,2e(a+1)q\}$. For $1\le v\le|I_0|$, the original event $\{V=v\}$ implies $C(q(v+a)/N)\ge v$, since $R\le V+a$. The threshold is in $(0,q]$ because $v+a\le N$. Put $b=eq(a+1)$. When $b<1/2$, Chernoff gives $\mathbb P(V=v)\le b^v$, hence $\operatorname{FDR}\le\mathbb P(V>0)\le b/(1-b)\le2b$. When $b\ge1/2$, the trivial cap is at most $2b$. Thus bounded alternative counts cannot refute the main order assertion even with arbitrary unsaturated correlated alternative gates. Within this nonpositive-null-covariance subclass, a diverging normalized ratio requires $(a+1)/\sqrt{\log(1/q)}$ to be unbounded.
- Obstruction: Final conditioning is a real limitation of the conditional tool, not a main counterexample. The precise saved diagnostic [A correlated alternative flips independent null residuals positive and defeats marginal-only sub-Poisson calibration](../ideas/idea_1256293060f9.md) uses independent $X,Y\sim N(0,1)$ and $(T_1,T_2,T_3)=(X,Y,1+(X-Y)/\sqrt2)$. The original null covariance is zero, but conditioning on the complete alternative leaves null residual covariance $\frac12\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with positive off-diagonal entry. At $q=\overline\Phi(20)$ the prescribed conditional marginal calibration factor is $K=1$ near $T_3=1$, yet the count mgf fails at $e^\lambda=2/(3q)$ on a positive-probability interval of conditioning values. This rules out replacing the final conditional count assumption by marginal calibration alone. It does not refute the approved helper, whose sign requirement is imposed after final conditioning, or the main theorem: the same three-test model has $\operatorname{FDR}\le11q/9$ by the marginal harmonic bound.
