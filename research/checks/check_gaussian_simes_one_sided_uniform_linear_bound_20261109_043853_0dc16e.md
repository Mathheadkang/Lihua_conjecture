- Obstruction: Every signed rank-one Gaussian family, including arbitrary multiplicities, sign imbalance, and dimension growing with the testing level, has Simes rejection probability exactly $q$ for $0<q<1/2$, according to the three rank-one failures in the round pack. These are one family, not three independent obstructions. Writing $T_i=s_i Z$, with $m_+$ positive signs and $m_-$ negative signs, rejection occurs in the disjoint tails $\overline\Phi(Z)\le qm_+/N$ or $\overline\Phi(Z)\ge1-qm_-/N$ (omit an absent sign class). Their probabilities sum to $q$. Replication or sign imbalance in this family therefore cannot produce an unbounded rejection-probability-to-level ratio.
- Obstruction: Every model with $N\le3$ obeys $\mathbb P(E_q)/q\le11/6$, as reported by the bounded-three-coordinate candidate. More generally, a bounded dimension cannot refute a conjecture allowing an unspecified finite universal constant: the harmonic Simes bound gives $\mathbb P(E_q)\le qH_N$ for arbitrary uniform marginals, where $H_N=\sum_{k=1}^N1/k$. A refuting sequence must have unbounded dimension.

- Obstruction: A normal-marginal construction in which a coordinate difference has both an atom at zero and positive probability of being nonzero cannot be jointly Gaussian: every Gaussian difference is either deterministic or has an atom-free normal law. - Obstruction: Singular zero-sum regular-simplex Gaussian families, including their growing-dimensional instances, satisfy the reported bound $\mathbb P(E_q)\le 2eq$ for $0<q\le 1/(2e)$, where $E_q=\{\exists k:p_{(k)}^{(1)}\le qk/N\}$. - Obstruction: Arbitrarily dependent pairs of standard Gaussian carriers with deterministic signed multiplicities produce at most four distinct coordinate p-value formulas. This family satisfies $\mathbb P(E_q)\le4q$ by the candidate failure line, irrespective of multiplicities and correlation. More generally, if coordinates consist of at most $L$ distinct standard-uniform p-value formulas, then rejection implies that at least one of those formulas is at most $q$: every BH threshold $qk/N$ is at most $q$. A union bound gives $\mathbb P(E_q)\le Lq$ without assuming independence. Thus every fixed number $m$ of Gaussian carriers used through signed repetitions has the bound $2mq$ and cannot refute the conjecture merely by increasing dimension or altering multiplicities.
- Obstruction: A deterministic partition into a bounded number of classes with uniformly controlled classwise Simes probabilities prevents an unbounded rejection-probability-to-level ratio. Precisely, let nonempty classes have sizes $n_j$ summing to $N$, and let $E_{q,j}$ be the Simes crossing event within class $j$ at level $q$. For a full crossing at rank $k$, count $r_j$ class-$j$ p-values at most $qk/N$. Since $\sum_j r_j\ge k$, some class satisfies $r_j/n_j\ge k/N$. Its $r_j$-th order statistic is at most $qk/N\le qr_j/n_j$, so $E_q\subseteq\bigcup_j E_{q,j}$. Consequently, classwise bounds $\mathbb P(E_{q,j})\le C_jq$ give $\mathbb P(E_q)\le q\sum_j C_j$, with no independence assumption between classes. This deterministic implication is checked here directly; the classwise probability inputs below are the supplied candidate reports.
- Obstruction: Growing-dimensional adjacent Gaussian difference chains fail to refute the target because their odd and even coordinates separately have independent uniform p-values. The supplied round-4 candidate therefore has two classes with $C_j=1$, yielding $\mathbb P(E_q)\le2q$. Growth in the chain length does not escape this fixed-partition obstruction.
