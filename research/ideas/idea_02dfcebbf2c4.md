Focused primary-source tool pass after the signed-fibre obstruction. Barthe-Roberto, Sobolev inequalities for probability measures on the real line, Studia Math159(3)(2003)481-497, DOI10.4064/sm159-3-9, publisher PDF https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/89465, p483 and Proposition2 pp484-485: a nonnegative two-weight Hardy inequality is controlled by the supremum of tail mass times an inverse derivative-weight density integral, with square-form constants between B and 4B. Any proposed BH use must specify both actual weights and show that this supremum is finite; signed jumps themselves are not nonnegative measures. The disconnected actual FDP fibre example is preserved separately as idea_3db5febfc68b.

A distinct verified tool family is Dario Cordero-Erausquin and Michel Ledoux, Hypercontractive measures, Talagrand's inequality, and influences, official arXiv:1105.4533v1 (23 May 2011), https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.4533v1. Read the Gaussian/product setup, Corollary5 on p.12, Theorem6 and proof on pp.13-14. Theorem6 bounds variance of bounded smooth functions using L1 directional influences with a square-root logarithmic improvement; Gaussian hypotheses provide dimension-independent functional-inequality constants. Corollary5's nonorthogonal directions require an actual positive projector decomposition of the identity. Arbitrary Gaussian test-coordinate directions do not automatically form such a frame, and the correlated T coordinates are not a product Gaussian space.

Potential future check: whether an appropriate heat-regularized, actual-null-tagged function admits controlled L1 influences and a valid frame/energy decomposition, or whether a layer-cake decomposition of FDP can use this logarithmic gain without losing rank information. No bound for those influences, no control of the inverse heat normalizer, and no mean-from-variance conversion for the general FDP have been established. Hard BH jumps require justified BV/heat approximation. These are research obligations, not permissions to import a generic variance inequality as the main FDR bound.
