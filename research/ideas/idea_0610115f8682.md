Exact deterministic observation for finite two-sided arrays, NOT a global FDR bound or a proof plan. After row-sign conjugation one may take every alternative mean mu_j>=0 (nulls mu0 unchanged), retaining unit vectors v_j. Write GaussianZ=r*w, r>0, a_j=v_j·w. At a fixed BH threshold z_k>=u, null rejection |r a_i|>=z_k is nondecreasing in r. An alternative can deactivate (outsidefoldedthreshold→inside) as r increases ONLY at its positive boundary mu_j+r a_j=z_k with a_j<0 and mu_j>z_k. Its globally reflected counterpart at the same r is mu_j-r a_j=2mu_j-z_k>z_k, and in fact for all r'>=0 the counterpart mu_j+r'|a_j|>=mu_j>z_k rejects at that level. A decrease of the complete largest BH rank along a ray therefore requires at least one such strong-mean deactivation. Coincident events may be treated by retaining all simultaneous activations/deactivations, not a chosen rank. For genuinely singular rays a_j=0 there is no crossing; coefficient blow-up is irrelevant to this count identity.

This actual deactivation/witness bookkeeping is distinct from assuming independent original face fluxes positive. It may clarify the two-sided half-factor via coupling actual opposite-ray ranks, but no charge bound follows yet. In a general finite array, conditional-on-orientation null projections are atomic, unlike the proved-candidate spherical population. Averaging radial conditional null calibration over orientations while keeping the actual paired rank history is still missing; it cannot be replaced by the isotropic cap density or by discarding favorable faces. Previous reflection-capacity/antipodal-rotation failures remain in force. Any future plan must derive a genuinely new noncircular signed ledger inequality and pass the existing Gaussian staircase and singular-correlation tests before claiming a leading constant.
