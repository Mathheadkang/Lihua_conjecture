The researcher shared screenshots of a conversation with the Stanford faculty member who proposed the conjecture. Attribute the following to that faculty member; it is not independently checked mathematical evidence:
“前两个结果我之前也证出来了，但条件比较 artificial，第四个看上去很有意思，具体是什么意思？”
“能证出来 q\sqrt{\log(1/q)} 的 bound 么？”

Numbering ambiguity:
In the screenshot's first section there are four bullets. The fourth is a loosely worded claim that general-covariance nonsharp bounds exist, with Lei's conditional leave-one-out bound as an example; the third is the isotropic spherical population theorem. Do not assume the faculty member meant the spherical population theorem by “第四个,” and do not identify the fourth with a finished dimension-free order result.

Scope checked in the teaching chat against approved teach packs:
- lei2026_conditional_fdp_bound assumes conditional independence given Z and gives a conditional envelope involving sup_t F_{i,z}(t)/t. This is not by itself an unrestricted dimension-uniform FDR <= C q sqrt(log(1/q)) theorem. Conditioning on a sufficiently large Gaussian vector can make the estimate vacuous.
- multifactor_radial_fdr_bounds retains Delta_s(d,q), a dimension-dependent tail-strip term. Its validity for a multifactor representation does not eliminate this cost for arbitrary covariance.
- Restricted factor/directional/population results must not be presented as the requested unrestricted bound.

Agent interpretation and recommendation, distinguished from the faculty's words:
A useful possible milestone is a theorem with one finite universal C and q0 such that, for every q<=q0 and EVERY finite N, mean vector theta and PSD unit-diagonal Sigma (including singular and q-dependent choices),
FDR_BH^(s)(q;N,theta,Sigma) <= C q sqrt(log(1/q)).
This removes the need to determine the sharp leading coefficient while retaining the full model scope. The faculty did not explicitly specify one-sided versus two-sided in the displayed question, did not explicitly waive sharpness, and did not authorize replacing the existing sharp goals. Treat the weaker uniform-order statement only as a possible supporting target to assess through the native project workflow.
Do not spend more effort polishing already-known restricted cases as if they answer this question. When communicating, say explicitly whether a bound's C depends on N, rank, factor count, directional entropy, or covariance. Currently the reviewed verified tools do not establish the unrestricted uniform-order milestone.
