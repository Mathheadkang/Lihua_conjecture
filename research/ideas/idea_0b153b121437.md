## Purpose and status

This is the exact actual-BH bookkeeping step following the fractional-frame surface bound idea_ac8371a287d5 and centered velocity kernel idea_f018d1d6e808. It derives the real coefficient of the signed Mills drift, rather than assuming it matches a Gram barycenter potential. The root agent suggested testing a barycenter energy; the finite-cell divergence identity below was worked out in this delegation. It is draft agent mathematics and has not been independently verified in a native checking stage.

## A symmetric full-covariance path

Fix the original N, means theta, covariance Sigma and null set I0={i:theta_i<=0}. On standard G in R^N, put
$$
\Sigma_s=(1-s)I+s\Sigma,\qquad
v_i(s)=\Sigma_s^{1/2}e_i,\qquad
T_i(s)=\theta_i+v_i(s)\cdot G,\quad 0<=s<1.
$$
Each v_i has unit norm. The eigenbasis commutes along this path, and
$$
v_i'=K_s v_i,\qquad
K_s=\tfrac12(\Sigma-I)\Sigma_s^{-1}=K_s^{\mathsf T}.
$$
In particular v_i dot v_i'=0. The physical coordinate velocity D_i=v_i' dot G is independent of its own centered score X_i=v_i dot G. The path is positive definite for s<1, with no tie ambiguity in finite-cell calculations; the singular endpoint is not assumed away.

## Exact cell coefficients retaining all null labels

Write t_r=qr/N and u_r=barPhi^{-1}(t_r). In an open Gaussian cell where actual largest nonstrict BH has rank r>0 and selected true-null set S, let v=|S| and f=v/r. If v>0, choose the always-admissible fractional frame c_i=1/v on S. Its total weight is one because
$$
\frac1v\sum_{i\in S}v_i v_i^{\mathsf T}\preceq I.
$$
Define
$$
a_{r,S}=\frac1v\sum_{i\in S}v_i,\qquad
b_{r,S}=f a_{r,S}=\frac1r\sum_{i\in S}v_i,\qquad
c_{r,S}=h(u_r)b_{r,S}.
$$
Set f,b,c to zero if r=0 or v=0. Here h=phi/barPhi. Thus c is a piecewise constant vector field in G at each fixed path time, with all rank changes and all entry/dilution effects encoded by its cell values.

## Sidewise centering at a real BH face

Consider any oriented interface where the score T_j increases through a physical threshold u_k, with minus and plus BH states (r_-,S_-) and (r_+,S_+). Let Delta f=f_+-f_- and Delta c=c_+-c_-. For a generic point on the interface the exact chain-rule contribution to FDR' is D_j Delta f. Sidewise application of the centered kernel uses centers h(u_{r_+})v_j' dot a_+ and h(u_{r_-})v_j' dot a_-. Algebraically,
$$
D_j\Delta f=
f_+\{D_j-h(u_{r_+})v_j'\cdot a_+\}
-f_-\{D_j-h(u_{r_-})v_j'\cdot a_-\}
+v_j'\cdot\Delta c. \tag{DC}
$$
Terms whose side has f=0 vanish. This identity includes null-coordinate entry faces, alternative entry faces, cascades to a larger physical rank, and dilution of old rejections. It also includes the quantile increment h(u_{r_+})-h(u_{r_-}); replacing it by one h(u_k) is generally incorrect.

For negative null means, each selected null satisfies v_i dot G>=u_r-theta_i>=u_r, so the nonnegative residual-side bounds in idea_f018d1d6e808 still apply. The exact coefficients above do not alter theta, labels, ties or BH rank definitions.

## Collate the signed drift before taking any absolute value

At fixed s<1 the coordinate hyperplanes T_j=u_k form a finite arrangement. Define the drift sum using their actual BH interfaces:
$$
\mathcal D_s=\sum_{\text{faces }F\subset\{T_j=u_k\}}
\int_F(v_j'\cdot\Delta c)\phi_N(G)\,dH^{N-1}(G).
$$
The normals v_j have unit length, so this is also the delta-function convention delta(T_j-u_k). Because K_s is symmetric,
$$
v_j'\cdot\Delta c=v_j\cdot K_s\Delta c.
$$
The distributional divergence of the piecewise constant field K_s c(G) is exactly the face sum with these coefficients. Gaussian integration by parts therefore gives the finite identity
$$
\boxed{\quad\mathcal D_s=E[G\cdot K_s c(G)].\quad} \tag{DIV}
$$
No absolute row sum, positive-face sum, independently optimized covariance, or inverse conditional variance has been introduced. For fixed N and s<1, all coefficients are finite and Gaussian tails justify the boundary at infinity.

The root agent additionally observed the useful own-score cancellation:
$$
E[(G\cdot K_s v_i)\,B(T_i)]=0
$$
for every integrable own-score function B, since Cov(G dot K_s v_i,T_i-theta_i)=v_i dot K_s v_i=0. Hence (DIV) can be written as a sum of centered own-score residuals, but arbitrary unsigned estimates of those residuals are not justified.

## What the barycenter energy does and does not give

Within a fixed cell, c'=K_s c and therefore
$$
\partial_s\frac12\|c\|^2=c\cdot K_s c.
$$
This is not the drift in (DIV), which is G dot K_s c. Replacing one by the other leaves the bulk residual (G-c) dot K_s c. Moreover the full derivative of E||c||^2/2 contains the real interface jump of that potential:
$$
\frac d{ds}E\frac{\|c\|^2}{2}
=E[c\cdot K_s c]
+\sum_F\int_F D_j\,\Delta(\|c\|^2/2)\phi_N\,dH^{N-1}.
$$
Thus a proposed energy allocation must retain both this boundary correction and the bulk residual. The simple constant-coefficient Gram energy identity alone does not pay the actual drift.

There is a decisive endpoint calibration. With N iid centered nulls at s=0, on R=1 the selected vector is an orthonormal basis vector and ||c||^2=h(u(q/N))^2. For small fixed q, the binomial union bound gives P(R=1)>=q/2 uniformly for sufficiently large N: P(at least one p<=q/N)>=q-q^2/2 and P(any rank>=2 qualifies)<=sum_{r>=2}(eq)^r. Consequently
$$
E\|c\|^2\ge(q/2)h(u(q/N))^2\asymp q\log N.
$$
An unrenormalized energy endpoint is therefore not uniformly finite at the target scale, already at the independent starting model. This rejects that particular simple energy payment, not the signed drift identity or all renormalized potentials.

## Exact remaining problem

The finite FDR derivative is the centered sidewise marked-face remainder from (DC), plus (DIV). Both now have explicit coefficients. The surface/velocity lemmas control individual fractional-tail marks but do not supply a summable actual-rank cover, and (DIV) still needs a signed residual or renormalized allocation. No new plan is written by merely asserting those sums have the target order. The original theorem and all native state remain unchanged.
