Elementary event comparison to check formally before reuse. For centered Gaussian T with every null true, p_i^(2)=2S(|T_i|)<=2S(T_i)=2p_i^(1), hence ordered p-values satisfy the same componentwise order after scaling. A one-sided Simes rejection at q therefore implies a two-sided Simes rejection at level2q for the SAME covariance, so A1_N(q,Sigma)<=A2_N(2q,Sigma) for q<1/2. Conversely, apply one-sided Simes at q<1/2 to the exactly Gaussian signed duplication (T,-T): only the smaller member of each pair can be rejected, and its ordered p-value is half the original two-sided p-value, with total test count2N. Its largest qualifying rank equals the original two-sided rank. Thus a universal linear global-null bound for either side would imply one for the other, with at most factor2 loss. This statement concerns rejection probabilities under the global null; it does NOT give a mixed-FDR comparison from componentwise p-value order.

Application candidate: the primary preprint Xianyang Zhang, Exact Simes inequalities for two-sided Gaussian tests, arXiv:2611.02513v1 (Nov2 2026), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02513 , has fixed-m covariance-uniform small-level validity (Theorem2.1) and restricted growing-m validity (introduction points to Theorems5.2,6.1). After exact source extraction/verification, the one-sided event comparison can transfer these known versions at cost2. It does not remove their dependence restrictions or the fixed-m cutoff, and therefore does not prove the unrestricted linear conjecture. This is a useful adaptation avenue in the researcher's permitted finite-constant-loss phase; no source theorem is imported or approved by this idea.
