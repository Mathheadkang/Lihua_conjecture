Supporting counterexample search only; no asymptotic refutation and no theorem proof. Scope is the one-sided source construction in Sections 3.3--3.4 of lei2026_gaussian_bh (PDF pp.44--51), not the unrestricted two-sided model.

## Concrete equal-block model and exact BH condition

Fix q in (0,1), an integer B>=2, and alpha=q/B. Take B equal-size independent copies of the source population design nu^+_{alpha,r}, driven by independent standard-normal factors Z_1,...,Z_B and independent residual null errors. In each block the null mass is pi_{0,r}=1-r-tau_r, null loading is r, primary alternative mass is r with mean M_r=c_{alpha,r}+b_r and loading -1, and secondary mass tau_r is the source law supported on means a/r+a with loading -1. Thus every Gaussian coordinate has unit variance, all null means are zero, all alternative means positive, and blocks are exactly independent. The original construction already permits r tending to zero.

Write p(c)=barPhi(c), and G_{r,z}(c) for one block's conditional rejected proportion above statistic cutoff c. With equal block weights 1/B, the global population BH crossing is exactly
$$
C_r(Z)=\inf\left\{c\ge p^{-1}(q):\frac{\alpha}{p(c)}\sum_{b=1}^B G_{r,Z_b}(c)\ge1\right\}.
$$
In particular it is a sum of block crossing ratios; it is not B separate BH procedures at level alpha.

## Explicit primary-signal obstruction

For arbitrary block weights w_j and primary masses r_j within their blocks, put k_j=N w_j r_j (an integer in a finite design), alpha_j=q w_j, and C_{P,j}=p^{-1}(alpha_j r_j). If the deterministic primary statistic in block j satisfies M_j-Z_j>=C_{P,j}, then its k_j identical p-values are at most q k_j/N. Hence the exact finite BH rank condition holds at k_j, and the global cutoff satisfies C_BH<=C_{P,j}, regardless of all other blocks. This statement uses the largest qualifying rank and is valid even if later crossing ratios fall again.

For synchronized equal blocks, fixed q,B and r tending to zero, c_{alpha,r}->infinity and r c_{alpha,r}->0, while tau_r/r->0. If min_b Z_b<0, at least one block's primary signals force the first crossing at c_{alpha,r}+O(1/c_{alpha,r}); the aggregate conditional null ratio there tends to q. Thus the global limiting FDP is q on this event, rather than a sum of inflated local FDPs.

## All-positive population limit and its integrable envelope

For fixed z_1,...,z_B>0, let m=min_b z_b and
$$
H_0(x;z)=\frac qB\sum_{b=1}^B\exp(xz_b-x^2/2).
$$
Before x=m, all secondary crossing contributions vanish as r->0 at c=x/r, and H_0 is strictly increasing. If H_0(m)<1, then necessarily m<y_alpha, and the first secondary contribution turns on at r C_r->m; the FDP tends to H_0(m). If H_0(m)>=1, the first crossing is a null-only root before m and the limiting FDP is 1. (If m>=y_alpha, H_0(m)>=q exp(m^2/2)>=B, so the null-only case applies.) Therefore the candidate derived population limit is
$$
D(Z)=q\,1\{\min_b Z_b<0\}
+\min\{1,H_0(\min_b Z_b;Z)\}\,1\{\min_b Z_b>0\}.
$$
This uses source tail-ratio and secondary-transition estimates extended to a finite sum. The extension has an explicit crossing argument above, but has not received independent verification or a finite-dimensional transfer proof.

Dropping the cap gives a directly integrable bound. By symmetry and splitting whether Z_1 attains the minimum,
$$
\mathbb E\left[e^{mZ_1-m^2/2}1\{m>0\}\right]
=2^{-B}+\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_0^\infty\bar\Phi(t)^{B-1}\,dt.
$$
The term with Z_1 minimum uses e^{t^2/2}phi(t)=1/sqrt(2pi); each other possible minimizer uses the exact tilted-tail integral
$$
\int_t^\infty e^{tz-t^2/2}\phi(z)\,dz=\frac12.
$$
Consequently
$$
\mathbb ED\le q\left[1+\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_0^\infty\bar\Phi(t)^{B-1}\,dt\right]
\le q\left(1+\frac1{2\pi}\right),\qquad B\ge2.
$$
The final equality at B=2 uses int_0^infinity barPhi(t)dt=1/sqrt(2pi). This removes the proposed q sqrt(log(B/q)) gain for this synchronized, iterated population construction.

## Limits and unfinished obligation

The calculation fixes q,B, takes block sizes N/B->infinity (conditional empirical-to-population transfer still to be checked for the combined model), then r->0. It is compatible with B(q)=ceil(q^{-c}) by choosing a separate sufficiently small r(q) and sufficiently large finite N(q) at each q; it does not establish a uniform bound for every simultaneous choice of B(q), r(q), and block sizes. Such a diagonal version yields O(q), not a counterexample.

Unequal weights or loadings require a new uniform lemma: order the deterministic primary cap scales C_{P,j}; prove that a block can contribute leading local-envelope inflation only when every earlier cap with C_{P,j} below its proposed deep cutoff is inactive. For small source r_j these inactivity events have probability approximately 1/2 and are independent across factors, giving a potential geometric suppression instead of an unweighted sum over B. Turning that observation into a uniform FDP estimate, including blocks near their primary transition, is unresolved. Hierarchical scales are an incomplete candidate route, not a refutation or proof of either full target.
