Finner, Dickhaus and Roters, Dependency and false discovery rate: Asymptotics, Annals of Statistics 35(4) (2007), 1432–1455, DOI 10.1214/009053607000000046, https://arxiv.org/pdf/0710.3171 (v1 electronic reprint): Section 2, Remark 2.2, PDF p.7 defines a strict sign-changing largest crossing and assumes its occurrence with probability one, excluding tangent contacts. Theorem 2.1, PDF p.8 gives limiting rejection and FDP formulas for their D-EX model with a positive proportion of alternative p-values exactly zero. Section 2, PDF p.5 additionally imposes a continuous conditional null cdf. These hypotheses do not cover our nonzero finite background atoms or root births. The zero-crossing subsection starts at PDF p.9 and treats additional local-cdf assumptions, rather than allowing one to infer FDP convergence solely from a vanishing rejection fraction.

Dey, On limiting behaviors of stepwise multiple testing procedures, Statistical Papers 65 (2024), 5691–5717, DOI 10.1007/s00362-024-01613-6, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00362-024-01613-6 : Theorem 4.3 is restricted to the equicorrelated Gaussian global null. Its proof passes from empirical-quantile convergence on compact subintervals to a global minimum over all order statistics. The displayed argument does not explicitly supply the needed empirical endpoint control; that passage remains unverified here. This is not a claim that the theorem is false. It supplies neither mixed atomic-background FDP convergence nor compensated noise asymptotics.

Neuvial, Asymptotic properties of false discovery rate controlling procedures under independence, Electronic Journal of Statistics 2 (2008), 1065–1110, DOI 10.1214/08-EJS207, https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.2111 (v2): model assumptions p.1068 require independent p-values with a uniform null and a concave C1 alternative cdf; Condition C.1 and Theorem 3.2, p.1072 require a positive Hadamard-differentiable threshold. Conditions C.2–C.3 and Definition 3.7, p.1074 concern unique interior regular crossings. Our conditional mixture has atoms and possible crossing switches, so these are guidance, not an applicable black box.

Suggested project-specific obligations, not proved results: conditional on the original Gaussian vector, apply empirical-cdf convergence only to the private group, retain background atoms exactly, exclude exceptional tangent/boundary coincidences with an explicit Gaussian-null-set argument, and prove stability of the actual largest crossing. Positive-crossing FDP must retain the fractional chosen-group numerator. When the largest population crossing is zero, separately rule out nonvanishing FDP from microscopic private-copy rejections; convergence of R/(NK) to zero alone is insufficient. Only after this fixed-noise limit is established should the noise limit be studied at scale s/t_s, including root births/switches and signed rank cascades.
