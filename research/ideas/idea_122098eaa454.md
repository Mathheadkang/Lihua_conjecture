For q down to zero let L=log(1/q), s=ceil(exp(sqrt(L))), m=2 ceil(exp(L^2))+2s+1, and N=m+s. Let Z_i, 0<=i<m, be independent standard normals; the m true-null statistics are T_{i+1}=Z_i. For 1<=j<=s set A_{ji}=sqrt(2/m) cos(2 pi j i/m), mu_j=(-1)^j(sqrt(2L)+j/(s+1)), and T_{m+j}=mu_j+sum_i A_{ji}Z_i. The identity A A^T=I_s gives an exactly jointly Gaussian unit-diagonal covariance [[I_m,A^T],[A,I_s]], of rank m. Null-alternative correlations individually vanish but the gates depend on all null coordinates, and the number of alternatives is exp(sqrt(L)) while their proportion tends to zero.

For any independent-null model with at most s alternatives, the elementary binomial order-statistic argument yields FDR <= e q [1+log(s+1)]/[1-eq(s+1)] when the denominator is positive. At the present schedule this upper bound has normalized limiting constant e, above 1/(2 sqrt(pi)); hence it neither excludes nor refutes the target. No asymptotic lower bound or completed global-BH crossing calculation is available for the displayed object. The smaller schedule log(s)~a sqrt(L), a<1/(2e sqrt(pi)), is excluded by that envelope, and this observation must not be extended to the present a=1 construction. A useful next test is whether the dense orthogonal gates can create global adaptive crossings despite their tiny individual correlations, or whether their Gaussian geometry forces a sharper O(q) bound.
