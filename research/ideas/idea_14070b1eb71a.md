At fixed positive private noise and fixed common Gaussian vector G, the privately cloned coordinate has a finite conditional mean m and conditional variance s^2<1. Its conditional survival mass at small rank has the form b(r)=S((S^{-1}(c r)-m)/s), for a fixed positive rank-normalization constant c. Gaussian tail asymptotics give log(b(r)/r)=-(s^{-2}-1)u(r)^2/2+O(u(r)+log u(r)), where u(r)=S^{-1}(c r) tends to infinity. Consequently b(r)/r tends to zero. Each exact block has a fixed positive p-value conditional on G, so it contributes no mass at sufficiently small ranks. This excludes positive population crossings accumulating at rank zero for that fixed G and fixed noise.

Possible proof step: first isolate this deterministic population zero-rank gap; then establish a separate conditional finite-copy lower-tail estimate that rules out empirical BH crossings escaping into vanishing ranks, and combine it with a valid comparison of actual largest crossings away from zero. The population ratio limit alone gives no empirical endpoint convergence. General finite-copy tail control, largest-root comparison through tangencies, births and switches, and the compensated target-scale multirank expansion remain unproved. This is an approach, not a proof of the target and not a new tested candidate family.
