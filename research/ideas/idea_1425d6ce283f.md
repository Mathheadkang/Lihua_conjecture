Supporting computational question for the one-sided proof: with unit-diagonal joint-Gaussian covariance and all alternative means fixed, does reducing one true-null mean from 0 to a negative value ever INCREASE total BH FDR? Pointwise FDP is not monotone, but that alone does not answer Gaussian expectation. A general least-favorable-zero-mean reduction must not be assumed. Run a bounded remote paired/common-random-number screen at moderate finite q across small higher-rank covariances (including signed factors/residuals, PSD singular and interior) and mixed null/non-null sets; fixed I0 remains all theta<=0 before/after. Screen outputs only suggest an auxiliary monotonicity counterexample; use independent heldout seed for the best apparent increase, report paired MCSE, no asymptotic/main-conjecture claim. If nothing survives validation, record no counterexample found, not a monotonicity theorem. Heavy scientific work only UC Davis isolated new directory, <=2 CPU processes, no overwrites of previous experiments. Archive exact script/config/seeds/environment/aggregates. Prefer at most a couple minutes remote work rather than a broad unbounded optimization.
