Agent-origin approach, not a verified proof or a request to skip the native checks. This specifically addresses the current search-summary concern about an additional, higher local barrier well.

The current target already allows two Gaussian directions: one zero-mean true atom and one diffuse alternative quantile. This plan uses that exact model. It does not generalize to two independently diffuse direction groups or to arbitrary parameter laws.

## Proposed next plan: compare the complete sublevel sets

Use the frozen target's g(c), b, c_*, vartheta, r(c)=p+(1-p)c, and its approved bump chi, with 0<=chi<=1, chi=1 on [-1,1], chi=0 outside [-2,2]. Let
g_epsilon^delta(c)=g(c)+delta epsilon chi((c-c_*)/(K sqrt(epsilon))).

1. Extract fixed local and global margins BEFORE choosing epsilon.

Since g'(c_*)=0 and g''(c_*)>0, choose eta>0 and 0<alpha<=beta such that [c_*-eta,c_*+eta] lies in (0,1), and
alpha(c-c_*)^2 <= g(c)-b <= beta(c-c_*)^2
throughout that interval.

Since c_* is the unique global minimizer on the compact interval [0,1],
d_eta=min_{|c-c_*|>=eta}[g(c)-b]>0.
This single gap handles all other wells, including infinitely many or very shallow higher wells outside the fixed neighborhood. Its size may depend on the fixed model, as the target allows.

A useful detail for admissibility: strict decrease of a C3 function does not imply its derivative is everywhere strictly negative. Do not assume that. At c_*, however,
vartheta'(c_*)=(1-p)u'(r_*)<0.
Shrink eta so vartheta'<=-m<0 in this neighborhood.

2. Choose K and the common small-epsilon range.

Take alpha K^2>1, for example alpha K^2>=2. Put C0=4 beta K^2+1.
Require 2K sqrt(epsilon)<eta,
(C0+2)epsilon<d_eta,
epsilon<vartheta(1)/2,
and sqrt(epsilon)||chi'||_infinity/K<m/2.

Both perturbed quantiles stay positive. On the bump support, their derivative differs from vartheta' by at most sqrt(epsilon)||chi'||/K and hence remains negative; outside it they agree with the original strictly decreasing quantile. Check the global strict-decrease conclusion across the support endpoints.

For delta=-1,
g_epsilon^- >=g-epsilon>=b-epsilon,
with equality only at c_*.

For delta=+1, on the plateau |c-c_*|<=K sqrt(epsilon), the perturbation is exactly +epsilon and the unique minimum is b+epsilon. Outside that plateau but inside the eta-neighborhood,
g_epsilon^+>=g>=b+alpha K^2 epsilon>b+epsilon.
Outside the eta-neighborhood use d_eta>epsilon. Thus both claimed minima are exact and unique. The parameter cost follows directly by integrating a displacement at most epsilon over support length 4K sqrt(epsilon).

3. Prove equality of ALL qualifying sets at high levels, rather than following a chosen local root.

On the entire bump support,
g(c)<=b+4 beta K^2 epsilon
and g_epsilon^delta(c)<=b+C0 epsilon.
Therefore, for every y>=b+C0 epsilon, every point of the bump support belongs to BOTH sublevel sets. Outside the support g_epsilon^delta=g. Hence, pointwise on all of [0,1],
{c:g(c)<=y}={c:g_epsilon^delta(c)<=y}.
This is exact equality, including non-strict thresholds and all distant competing wells. Consequently the globally largest auxiliary ranks are exactly equal at such y.

For y<b-epsilon, neither barrier has a qualifying c, so both auxiliary ranks equal p. The two ranks can therefore differ only in
[b-epsilon,b+C0 epsilon].

This is the reason a fixed additional higher local well need not be handled by a new case-by-case branch enumeration: above this narrow window both complete qualifying sets are the same.

4. Within the affected window, control EVERY qualifying root using the compact gap.

If either g(c)<=y or g_epsilon^delta(c)<=y for y<=b+C0 epsilon, then
g(c)<=b+(C0+1)epsilon<b+d_eta.
Thus every qualifying c lies within eta of c_*.
The quadratic lower bound then gives
|c-c_*|<=sqrt((C0+1)/alpha) sqrt(epsilon).
This bounds the largest qualifying c as well as every other one. Whenever a fold is active, c_* itself qualifies, so its auxiliary rank lies near r_* with an O(sqrt(epsilon)) error.

For delta=+1, the birth/death interval is [b,b+epsilon): original active, perturbed inactive.
For delta=-1, it is [b-epsilon,b): perturbed active, original inactive.
At the upper endpoints the non-strict rule makes both folds active; these single endpoints do not affect the Gaussian integral. Keep these endpoint conventions explicit.

5. Recover the full SIGNED variation with quantitative error.

First independently establish the target's actual-FDR representation using forced true rejection: forcing the true atom in can only increase rank; restoring it preserves the forced largest rank exactly when X_0>=u(r_vartheta(Y)). When that condition fails the true atom cannot be rejected at any smaller actual rank, and FDP is zero even if an alternative-only crossing remains. Do not assert equality of auxiliary and actual ranks on that complementary event.

For fixed p>0 and |rho|<1, the conditional kernel k(y,r) and its derivatives are bounded on a fixed compact neighborhood of y=b, r in [p,1]. In the common-active part of the affected window, both ranks are r_*+O(sqrt(epsilon)), so the kernel difference is O(sqrt(epsilon)); the window has length O(epsilon). Its entire contribution is O(epsilon^(3/2)).

In the birth/death interval of length epsilon, the inactive rank is exactly p and the active rank is r_*+O(sqrt(epsilon)). Expanding k(y,r)phi(y) there gives the proposed estimate
F(pi_epsilon^delta)-F(pi)
 = -delta epsilon phi(b)[k(b,r_*)-k(b,p)] + O(epsilon^(3/2)).
Retain the sign of the bracket. Positive and negative jumps are both covered. Constants are fixed-model constants; no uniform rho-to-endpoint or dimension assertion is being made.

6. Finish the remaining target clauses in their correct order.

Only AFTER proving the population finite difference, use the approved continuity theorem at each fixed epsilon to choose canonical finite competitors with simultaneous W1 and full-FDR errors at most epsilon^2. No finite clone derivative or jitter limit needs to be interchanged.

If the stated finite-comparison inequality with finite lambda holds, divide it for each recovered sign by epsilon. The transport penalty tends to zero; the two signs force the signed jump to vanish. This is conditional on the comparison inequality, not proof that such a point exists.

Check the linear-quantile nonempty-hypothesis clause separately from this argument, keeping the frozen statement.
