For q in (0,1/4), let L=log(1/q), d=ceil(8L), and index N=2^d coordinates by all sign vectors s in {-1,1}^d. With independent standard Gaussian factors Z_1,...,Z_d, set theta_s=0 and T_s=d^{-1/2} sum_j s_j Z_j. Then Sigma_st=d^{-1} sum_j s_j t_j is an exactly admissible unit-diagonal Gram covariance, with joint Gaussianity and covariance rank d; all coordinates are true nulls. Ordinary one-sided BH uses p_s=barPhi(T_s). This is a proposed new high-complexity boundary test rather than a candidate with a completed NOT check. The number of directions is exponential and rank is of order log(1/q), so the earlier fixed-rank, rank-one, planar-grid, and displayed regular-simplex calculations do not analyze it. No inference that it lies outside every approved entropy or radial bound has been established. A useful analytic starting point is the exact conditional log moment generating function of the sign projections: for v>0 and fixed factor realization Z, d^{-1}log E_s[exp(v sum_j s_j Z_j)]=d^{-1}sum_j log cosh(v Z_j). The BH crossing requires controlling the random sign-tail count simultaneously over rank thresholds. No rigorous strict asymptotic excess over 1/sqrt(pi) has been derived, and finite-q numerical excess would not establish such an excess. A negative-mean variant theta_s=-q(1+s_1)/2 remains admissible, but because it is all-null its rejection probability is at most that of the zero-mean orbit under coordinatewise coupling; it cannot be used as an unsupported general mean-reduction argument for mixed alternatives. Preserve as an unresolved object for a separate high-complexity asymptotic analysis, not as a surviving counterexample.
