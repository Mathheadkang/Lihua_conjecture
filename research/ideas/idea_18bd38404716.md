Suggested by the teaching chat during the researcher's requested cross-field brainstorm. This is an exploratory approach with a small algebraic first test. It is not a proved mixed trace budget or a reduction of arbitrary Gaussian covariance to one factor.

## Start with the exact whole-avalanche FDP jump
For a nested change of the actual BH rejection set from A to A union B, put r=|A|>0, b=|B|, v=sum_{i in A} ell_i, a=sum_{j in B} ell_j. Then
$$
\Delta\operatorname{FDP}
=\frac{v+a}{r+b}-\frac vr
=\frac{ar-vb}{r(r+b)}
=\frac{1}{r(r+b)}
\sum_{i\in A,\ j\in B}(\ell_j-\ell_i).
$$
This pairs added false-discovery mass with dilution of the previous numerator before taking any bounds. Same-label pairs vanish. In particular every positive-rank-to-positive-rank jump in an all-null model vanishes, including unbalanced antipodal clones. Keep the boundary r=0 separately, where the jump is a/b; no O(q) bound for its integrated contribution is assumed.

Use only the actual nested sets from a single-coordinate upward crossing or a common upward shift. A useful exact way of identifying the finite BH largest fixed point is to start k_0=N and iterate k_{m+1}=#{i:p_i<=q k_m/N}; this is a decreasing integer sequence ending at the largest fixed point. Do not substitute independently optimized block ranks.

## Proposed cross-field step
Insert the pairing identity into the already verified signed face-jump formulas while keeping each trigger-coordinate index and its Gaussian normal. Ask whether a legitimate integration-by-parts or aggregation produces an explicit zero-rank boundary term plus a rectangular null-alternative coefficient matrix B contracted with inner products of Gaussian directions.

Only if that structure is established, test a classical Grothendieck bound:
$$
\sup_{\|u_i\|,\|w_j\|\le1}
\left|\sum_{ij}B_{ij}\langle u_i,w_j\rangle\right|
\le K_G
\max_{s_i,t_j\in\{-1,1\}}\left|\sum_{ij}B_{ij}s_i t_j\right|,
$$
with a universal constant K_G. A finite constant loss is compatible with the current order-only main target, although it would not settle the deferred sharp constant.

## The first possible fatal obstruction
The triggering face coordinate and the two label-pair indices are initially THREE indices. The pairing identity alone therefore gives a tensor, not automatically a bilinear matrix. Write all three indices in the finite-model face formula first. If the third index cannot be legally contracted with controlled cost, stop the Grothendieck branch. Do not hide a rank/dimension-dependent tensor norm in B.

Even if B exists, its required sign norm may grow like log N. Freeze B from the actual original model before maximizing signs. The coefficients depend on the original multivariate law, actual largest crossing and labels; sign rounding is not a change of that Gaussian model. Do not move a maximum outside a time integral without justification.

## Cheapest decisive tests
1. All-null unbalanced antipodal clones: every paired internal contribution must be exactly zero; keep and inspect the zero-rank term.
2. One true-null clump and one alternative clump with a large rank jump: verify the original jump equals the paired sum with the actual denominator.
3. Three non-collinear loading directions: check whether the trigger index survives and prevents a rectangular reduction.
4. On any resulting frozen matrix, compare its sign norm with the desired q sqrt(log(1/q)) scale before claiming a uniform matrix certificate. Divergence kills this strengthened route without refuting the main conjecture.

## Verified external source and limits
Shmuel Friedland and Lek-Heng Lim, Symmetric Grothendieck inequality, arXiv:2003.07345v1, 16 March 2020: https://arxiv.org/html/2003.07345v1 . Section 1, equations (1)-(3), states the rectangular Grothendieck comparison with dimension-independent constant. This is the precise functional-analysis / combinatorial-optimization tool proposed here. It supplies no BH coefficient estimate.
The newer author manuscript https://www.stat.uchicago.edu/~lekheng/work/sgi.pdf has different numbering and discusses symmetric norm versions and nonconvex quadratic programming. Do not apply a one-sided symmetric sign bound as if it implied a universal-factor SDP bound; do not replace the original signed matrix by its positive or entrywise absolute parts. The value of this proposal is exact label cancellation BEFORE attempting a geometric relaxation.
