## First failure in the literal statement
Consequence (ii) says 'for any (random or deterministic) threshold map' but gives neither u(y)>=U nor a measurability requirement, and does not give u_f a domain. Dropping the nonnegative baseline only reduces this claim to an integral of rho_r(u(y),y); the established envelope applies only when u(y)>=U. Thus the claimed unrestricted quantifier is not justified and is false if arbitrary real thresholds are allowed. A deterministic, measurable counterexample already suffices: q=0.1, r=0.99, u(y)=-10 for every y, and u_f=10. Let X=rY+sqrt(1-r^2)epsilon with independent standard normals. Then the first integral is P(Y<=U,X>=-10)/barPhi(-10) >= (0.9-barPhi(10))/barPhi(-10) >0.899. Since U<2 and sqrt(1-r^2)<0.142, the baseline integral is at most 0.9 barPhi(56)/barPhi(10)<exp(-1500). In contrast the claimed bound is 1/2+sqrt(0.01/1.99) U/sqrt(2pi)<0.557. Hence the left side exceeds the right side. The numerical inequalities follow directly from normal tail integral/Mills bounds, not a numerical search. Nonmeasurable maps would additionally leave the integral undefined; a random-map assertion also needs a specified measurable/pathwise reading.
