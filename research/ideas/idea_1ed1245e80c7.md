## Provenance and role

The root agent proposed the determinant-scaled centered scalar kernel below while checking the all-score cover's cofactor residual. I verified it by a direct Gaussian integral. This is an agent derivation in the same native delegation, not independent native verification. It supplies an explicit marked pair kernel; its hypotheses must be checked before applying it to any physical residual.

## Precise kernel

Let X,Y,D be jointly standard Gaussian, with Cov(Y,D)=0. Put
$$
\rho=Cov(X,Y),\quad \sigma=\sqrt{1-\rho^2},
\quad c=Cov(X,D),\quad h(u)=\phi(u)/\barPhi(u).
$$
For every u>=1 and every real y,
$$
\sigma\,\phi(y)
E[|D-h(u)c|1_{\{X>=u\}}M\mid Y=y]
\le C\barPhi(u), \tag{DCV}
$$
for every additional mark M in [0,1]. No independence of M is required. Higher actual null thresholds u-theta, theta<=0, only shrink the nonnegative left side, so all negative null means are covered.

The prefactor sigma is the square root of the two-score Gram determinant. It is not an inverse conditional variance. When sigma=0 the displayed product is interpreted directly as zero; a use involving division by sigma needs a separate limiting argument.

## Direct derivation

For sigma>0 write X=rho Y+sigma Z, with Z independent standard normal relative to Y. Since D is also independent of Y, write D=k Z+sqrt(1-k^2)H, H independent of (Y,Z). Then c=sigma k. At Y=y, the threshold is z=(u-rho y)/sigma. Triangle inequality reduces the claim to bounds for
$$
\sigma\phi(y)\barPhi(z)
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
\sigma\phi(y)E[|Z-\sigma h(u)|1_{\{Z>=z\}}].
$$
The first has the exact integral representation
$$
\sigma\phi(y)\barPhi(z)
=\int_u^\infty\phi(x)
\phi\left(\frac{y-\rho x}{\sigma}\right)dx
\le\phi(0)\barPhi(u).
$$

For the second, change variables from Z to X in the conditional density:
$$
\sigma\phi(y)E[|Z-\sigma h(u)|1_{\{Z>=z\}}]
$$
$$
=\int_u^\infty
|x-\rho y-\sigma^2h(u)|\,
\phi(x)\,
\phi\left(\frac{y-\rho x}{\sigma}\right)\frac{dx}{\sigma}.
$$
With a=(y-rho x)/sigma,
$$
x-\rho y-\sigma^2h(u)
=\sigma^2(x-h(u))-\rho\sigma a.
$$
Consequently this integral is at most
$$
\sigma\phi(0)\int_u^\infty|x-h(u)|\phi(x)dx
+|\rho|\sup_a|a|\phi(a)\,\barPhi(u).
$$
Finally h(u) is the mean of the Gaussian upper tail and
$$
\int_u^\infty|x-h(u)|\phi(x)dx
\le2\{\phi(u)-u\barPhi(u)\}
\le2\barPhi(u)/u.
$$
This proves (DCV), with a universal constant and without dimension, rank, direction-count or conditional-variance assumptions.

## Exact cofactor residual factor and its caveat

The all-score Parseval cover in idea_8303a1aba8de has f_i=sqrt(c_i)v_i and horizontal velocities g_i=f_i'-A f_i, A=F'F^T skew. Its exact cofactor identity gives
$$
E_B[1_{\{i\in B\}}(f_j'-L_B^Tf_j)]
=c_i g_j-\langle f_i,f_j\rangle g_i.
$$
For equal physical weights and a common physical generator g_i=M f_i this is
$$
c_i M(f_j-\rho_{ij}f_i).
$$
The vector f_j-rho_ij f_i has norm sqrt(c_i)sqrt(1-rho_ij^2). This is a genuine determinant factor arising before any inverse-basis estimate.

However M(f_j-rho f_i) is generally not orthogonal to the exposure f_j. It must first be split into its exposure-orthogonal part and its deterministic conditional mean at the face. Those mean terms, the explicit physical-weight derivatives and the Gaussian source in idea_8303a1aba8de remain in the signed identity. One cannot apply (DCV) directly to an arbitrary cofactor residual and silently discard those terms.

Even after the split, bounding M by its operator norm can produce a nonintegrable endpoint factor on a singular covariance interpolation. The actual direction velocity, physical rank increments and all mean/drift corrections must be paired before taking absolute values. Thus (DCV) is a concrete new scalar first gate for that allocation, not an asserted final budget or proof plan.
