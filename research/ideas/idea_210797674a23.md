The round-3 initially empty true-sector family suggests a bounded proof step, not a proof of the general target. If the base directing law has no true-tagged mass, V_X vanishes identically, so V_epsilon,X(z)=epsilon rho V_nu,X(z) exactly. The perturbed cutoff payoff therefore factors as epsilon rho times chi_a(R_epsilon) V_nu,X(Q_epsilon,X(R_epsilon))/R_epsilon, with the factor taken as zero on the cutoff-zero region. On its support R_epsilon>a, this remaining factor is bounded by 1/a independently of the background and perturbation size. Thus, whenever convergence of that remaining factor is established, its bounded convergence identifies the first-order payoff directly; no derivative of that factor is needed. At the base population f_X and w_X both vanish, and the asserted first-order contribution reduces to the bulk response from the inserted true mass. The reported analytic family meets the claimed signed derivative despite simultaneous inward level motion and actual-rank births. A general proof must still establish the needed convergence with actual-largest-rank conventions; this observation does not settle arbitrary mixtures, competing minima, endpoint regularity or the general expectation interchange. Use the exact factorization as a boundary check and preserve payoff change separately from rank-distribution change.
