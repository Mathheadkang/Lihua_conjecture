## Proposed mechanism

Following the three-test squared-correlation cusp test, a constructive replacement is a linear-speed Gaussian transport. With independent null block X in R^m and arbitrary alternative covariance, represent alternative loadings along
$$
v_j(t)=t b_j+\sqrt{1-t^2}\,b'_j+c_j,\qquad 0<=t<=1,
$$
where b_j is its loading on the original null subspace, b'_j the same loading in an independent copy, and c_j its independent residual. The null block and alternative block covariances stay fixed, while the cross block changes from zero to its original value. Each speed is ||b_j||/sqrt(1-t^2), with integrable time integral (pi/2)||b_j||. Bessel gives ||b_j||<=1.

A tempting certificate would bound each alternative's signed FDR directional variation by C q[1+u(q)]/N times its Euclidean speed. Summing alternatives would pay the target order in the independent-null case. The following actual-Gaussian first-derivative test disproves this particular per-alternative certificate. It does not disprove a collective transport, a certificate with additional first-order payment, or the main theorem.

## Legal one-alternative family

Fix 0<q<=1/100, let m>=2 and N=m+1, and take independent standard normals X_1,...,X_m,H. All X_i are centered null scores. There is one alternative, with
$$
T_A(s)=\mu-\frac{s}{\sqrt m}\sum_{i=1}^m X_i+\sqrt{1-s^2}\,H,
\qquad
\mu=u(2q/N)>0,\qquad 0<=s<1.
$$
Its marginal variance is one; its covariances with the nulls are -s/sqrt(m). The full covariance is PSD and positive definite for s<1. At s=0 all nulls are independent of the alternative, so FDR=q m/N. The single alternative's loading speed has norm one at s=0. Its number is one, so there is no sum over many alternative directions hidden in the test.

## Exact independent-endpoint derivative

Let R_i be largest-rank BH after forcing null i's p-value to zero. At s=0 it depends only on X_{-i},H, and the own-null integration gives
$$
E\left[X_i\,\frac{1_{\{X_i>=u(qR_i/N)\}}}{R_i}\,\middle|\,X_{-i},H\right]
=h_N(R_i),\qquad
h_N(r)=\frac{\phi(u(qr/N))}{r}.
$$
The covariance score derivative at independence, or differentiation of the finite Gaussian density, now yields
$$
\frac{d}{ds}FDR(s)\bigg|_{s=0}
=-\sqrt m\,E[H h_N(R_i)].
$$
Terms for a null contribution different from i vanish: integrating its own independent null first gives the constant q/N, whose remaining Gaussian score has mean zero. The identical nulls make all i coefficients equal.

The function h_N(r) is decreasing in r, because
$$
h_N(r)=\frac qN \frac{\phi(u(qr/N))}{barPhi(u(qr/N))}
$$
and the normal hazard is increasing in its argument while u decreases in r. Also R_i is nondecreasing in H. Conditional on every X_{-i}, it follows that E_H[H h_N(R_i)]<=0. Thus contributions outside any event in X_{-i} cannot cancel the positive derivative lower bound below.

## A positive-probability exact two-rank configuration

Let E be the event that BH has largest rank two when both p_i and the alternative p-value are forced to zero, with X_{-i} otherwise unchanged. On E no rank r>=3 can qualify even with both forced zeros; in particular every remaining null p-value exceeds 3q/N. With only p_i forced zero, exactly
$$
R_i=\begin{cases}2,&H>=0,\\1,&H<0.\end{cases}
$$
The threshold H=0 follows from the chosen mean mu=u(2q/N).

A finite binomial union bound gives a universal positive lower bound for P(E). For r=s+2>=3, an offending crossing requires at least s of the m-1 remaining nulls below qr/N. Its probability is bounded by
$$
\left(\frac{e q(s+2)}s\right)^s.
$$
Handle s=1 directly by 3q, and use (s+2)/s<=2 for s>=2. Hence
$$
P(E)\ge1-3q-\sum_{s=2}^\infty(2e q)^s
=1-3q-\frac{(2e q)^2}{1-2e q},
$$
which exceeds a positive universal constant on the stated q interval.

Therefore, using the same sign for all complementary configurations,
$$
FDR'(0)\ge
\sqrt m\,P(E)\,\phi(0)\{h_N(1)-h_N(2)\}>0.
$$

## Dimension calibration

For fixed q and m->infinity, write c_1=u(q/N), c_2=u(2q/N). Mills asymptotics give
$$
h_N(1)-h_N(2)
=\frac qN\{h(c_1)-h(c_2)\}
\sim\frac qN\frac{\log2}{c_1},
\qquad h=\phi/barPhi.
$$
Thus the derivative lower bound is of order
$$
\frac{q}{\sqrt m\sqrt{\log m}}.
$$
For the proposed per-alternative payment, with speed one, the allowed derivative would be C q[1+u(q)]/N. Their ratio diverges at least as sqrt(m)/sqrt(log m), for every fixed q and every finite q-only coefficient. Consequently that per-alternative directional inequality is false even at the independent-null endpoint.

The actual derivative tends to zero, so this calculation supplies no FDR inflation contradicting the universal main target. It shows that Gaussian nulls can share a linear response to one weakly correlated alternative; paying separately at scale 1/N loses their collective sqrt(m) amplification.

## Status and constructive remaining requirement

The path, own-null derivative, exact two-rank event, uniform event probability and asymptotic comparison are new agent calculations, not an independently verified node. A possible continuation must first pay a collective null-block linear response, or transport the entire block's FDR share rather than claiming every alternative has a qU/N budget. The negative-mean and general-correlated-null transfer remain separate obligations. No main asset, completion root, approval, or loop state is changed.
