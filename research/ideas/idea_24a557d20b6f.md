Assume ordinary BH with N original labels and q in (0,1). Fix a subset M of m original true-null labels such that, under the final sigma-field H, every p-value outside M is H-measurable, including all alternatives. Assume H-measurable finite K and the aggregate first-moment calibration E[C(qr/N)|H] <= qKr for r=1,...,N, on a common full-measure set. This follows from the target's count-mgf hypothesis and is the only probabilistic inequality used here.

For k=0,...,m, form a deterministic conditional BH vector by replacing the M coordinates by k zeros and m-k ones; its rejection count r_k depends only on k and the H-measurable remaining vector, because BH counts are invariant to permutations inside M. Let L be the number of rejected M coordinates in the actual original BH procedure. On {L=k}, the actual rejection count R equals r_k: at threshold qR/N the replaced and original vectors have the same count; for any larger BH threshold, the k originally rejected coordinates still contribute k in both vectors, and replacing the remaining coordinates by ones can only decrease the count. Thus no larger rank is created. For k>=1, r_k>=k.

Let D(s) count the H-measurable original null coordinates outside M. Since D(s)<=E[C(s)|H]<=NKs at the finite grid thresholds, the deterministic-null FDP contribution is at most qK pathwise. The conditional expected random-null contribution is sum_{k=1}^m (k/r_k) P(L=k|H). On {L=k}, the M-count C_M(qr_k/N) equals k, so each summand is at most E[C_M(qr_k/N)|H]/r_k <= qK. The H-measurable ranks take finitely many possible values, so the fixed-grid first-moment bound applies. Hence E[FDP|H] <= min{1,(m+1)qK}; there is no conditional-independence assumption.

A distinct boundary application: if the conditional Gaussian covariance Q has rank at most one and off-diagonal entries <=0, writing Q=vv^T shows there is at most one positive and at most one negative nonzero v_i. Thus at most two null coordinates have positive conditional variance; all remaining nulls are H-measurable. The above argument yields E[FDP|H] <= min{1,3qK}. More generally m<=9 is excluded as a counterexample to the target's 4e bound independently of N. This is a limited-random-coordinate obstruction, not the dimension-uniform count-mgf proof for unbounded m.
