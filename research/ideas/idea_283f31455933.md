## Mechanism

This first test asks whether ordinary dimension-free Gaussian concentration of a Lipschitz rank-crossing field can control the target. It uses the exact largest-rank rejection event. It is distinct from taking the expected rank-count envelope: the proposed resource is the Gaussian distance from a typical point to an activation threshold.

Let T_i=theta_i+v_i dot G with ||v_i||=1, allowing singular covariance. Order T decreasingly as T_[1]>=...>=T_[N]. Define
$$
W_q(G)=max_{1<=r<=N}\{T_[r]-u(qr/N)\}.
$$
Every coordinate is 1-Lipschitz in G, each order statistic is 1-Lipschitz for the coordinatewise sup norm, and the maximum preserves this bound. Thus W_q is 1-Lipschitz in the Euclidean norm of G. The event W_q>=0 is exactly BH having at least one rejection, with the original nonstrict comparisons. This only controls FDR through FDR<=P(R>0), so even a successful activation estimate would need testing against alternative-induced activation.

## Concrete first test in the iid all-null submodel

Fix 0<q<1/(4e). Take N independent standard normal nulls. For every rank r, the number exceeding u(qr/N) is Binomial(N,qr/N). The elementary binomial Chernoff bound gives
$$
P(W_q>=0)<=sum_{r=1}^N(e q)^r<=e q/(1-e q)<1/3.
$$
No Simes theorem is needed for this inequality.

On the other hand, for every fixed delta>0,
$$
P(W_q>=-delta)>=P(max_i Z_i>=u(q/N)-delta)->1.
$$
Indeed, write x_N=u(q/N)->infinity. Gaussian tail comparison gives
$$
N barPhi(x_N-delta)
=q\frac{barPhi(x_N-delta)}{barPhi(x_N)}
\sim q\,\frac{x_N}{x_N-delta}
e^{delta x_N-delta^2/2}->infinity,
$$
and independence bounds the probability of no such exceedance by exp[-N barPhi(x_N-delta)].

Consequently every median m_N can be selected with m_N<0 and m_N->0. The concentration estimate supplied merely by the 1-Lipschitz property at the activation level has exponent of order m_N^2 and therefore tends to the trivial bound one. The actual activation probability remains at most O(q) in the same example.

## Exact limitation and next requirement

This diagnoses the loss in a global Lipschitz/median-slack argument, rather than a failure of Gaussian isoperimetry itself. The set {W_q>=-delta} is a threshold-slack set; it is not being identified with the Euclidean delta-neighborhood of {W_q>=0}. Such an identification would be false in general.

The proposed geometry route therefore needs a local rank-dependent metric or a variance-sensitive concentration statement that becomes sharper at extreme ranks. Merely computing the 1-Lipschitz field and applying Gaussian concentration supplies no uniform small-q estimate. General means and alternatives make P(R>0) an even looser FDR surrogate. No main-target counterexample, proof, or new dependency is claimed.
