Diagnosis of the B4 obstruction in plan_two_sided_exogenous_pivotal_blocks_20261105 (heuristic, not proved).

1. Envelope reduction (likely routine). In the independent one-factor block class, condition on all factors Z. Nulls are then conditionally independent, so exactly E[FDP_i | Z] = (q/N) E[rho_i(R^(i)) | Z], with rho_i(k) = F_i(qk/N; Z_g)/(qk/N) and R^(i) the BH rank with p_i set to 0. For one-sided p-values the conditional likelihood ratio in x = PhiBar^{-1}(t) is log-concave, maximized at x* = z/r. For two-sided p-values, log LR in |x| is x^2(1-1/s^2)/2 + log cosh(x r z/s^2) + const, whose second derivative decreases on [0, inf). Hence in both cases rho_i(t) is unimodal in t: increasing up to t_max(z), then decreasing. Therefore rho_i <= rho~_i, the nonincreasing envelope sup_{k' >= k} rho_i(k'), and any stochastic lower bound on R^(i) can be inserted. The BH rank is monotone in adding p-values at fixed N, so R^(i) >= the rank from any sub-collection.

2. Why the sup/envelope form with a frozen background loses a factor 2 in the two-sided case. Two-sided null counts are symmetric in z, so the sup bound pays the peak e^{z^2/2}/(2s) at both signs of z. Real adaptation needs the block's own alternatives. Class monotonicity (pointwise in t): for an alternative with mu*a >= 0 the rejection count at |z| dominates the count at -|z|; for mu*a < 0 the reverse. Nulls and the background B are the same at z and -z. The class that adapts the crossing to t_max(z) for one sign therefore floods at the other sign: its full count stays >= eta*line up to the top of its adaptation range. Conjectured sign-splitting lemma: the threshold ranges on which the two signs can sit at the peak of rho are essentially disjoint in x-space. The total window length in z-space is then r(x_c - x_a), the one-sign length, which gives U/(2 sqrt(2 pi)) instead of twice it. A frozen B, independent of Z_g and identical at both signs, does not affect this flooding argument. That suggests a B4 proof pairing z with -z rather than bounding each sign by the sup.

3. Background-pivotal regime as a sandwich. If block g supplies less than an eta fraction of the line at the crossing, then R^(i) lies in [R_bg, R_bg^(1-eta)], where both are BH ranks of the background alone (the second with line scaled by 1-eta), and both are independent of Z_g. E_Z sup over a threshold interval [a, b] of rho(t; Z) is at most C + r log(b/a)/(s x sqrt(2 pi)) (one-factor computation). So the cost is governed by the hugging length log(R_bg^(1-eta)/R_bg). A deterministic hugging background is realizable: by the law of large numbers, many independent alternatives can have count close to (1-eta) N t/q on a range. In that example a nulls-only block cannot exploit hugging: its crossing lands on the decreasing branch where rho = eta/q, costing O(q). Exploitation again needs the block's own alternatives, so item 2 applies there too. Hugging alone does not beat the constant in the examples checked by hand.

4. One-sided consequence. In the one-sided problem only z > 0 is enhanced, so item 2's factor-2 loss cannot occur. The sup/envelope form with a frozen background may already be sharp there (the B4-b numeric ratio of about 2.02 against U/(2 sqrt(2 pi)) is about 1.01 against U/sqrt(2 pi)). The one-sided independent-blocks gate may therefore be closer than the two-sided one; the remaining issue is the per-block cost q*(number of null blocks), i.e. B6.
