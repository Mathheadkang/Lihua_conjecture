Side-chat proposal of 11 November 2026. No theorem is asserted. These are two restricted classes for testing mechanisms, not completion roots.

## Two cores of the unrestricted target

(a) Necessary. The unrestricted order bound implies a linear one-sided Gaussian Simes bound (idea_8699fa13f1f6; [draft Simes bound](../assets/gaussian_simes_one_sided_uniform_linear_bound.md)).

(b) Almost sufficient. A linear Simes bound for centered Gaussian vectors, applied to the null sub-vector, together with the FDR-linking inequality whose source statement is recorded in idea_e6e1f4c16f22, gives $\mathrm{FDR}\le\pi_0q\{1+C\log(1/(\pi_0q))\}$. Composite nulls are covered because the null-only rejection event is monotone in the null means.

So the target splits into Core I, a null-side weak-type maximal inequality with no alternatives, and Core II, the improvement from $\log(1/q)$ to $\sqrt{\log(1/q)}$. Core II is about how well the alternatives can place the BH threshold at the level where the nulls are enriched. Call this steering. Most failed plans attacked both cores at once. The two classes below remove Core I.

## Class E: mutually independent nulls

Model: $\Sigma_{ii'}=0$ for distinct $i,i'\in I_0$. The alternatives are arbitrary, $T_j=\theta_j+\langle b_j,G_0\rangle+s_j\eta_j$ with $\|b_j\|^2+s_j^2=1$ and $\eta$ any Gaussian vector independent of the null vector $G_0$. Dimension, means and all loadings are free.

Conjecture (E): $\mathrm{FDR}\le Cq$ with a universal $C$.

Why this is the right test. If the BH threshold could follow the smallest null p-value, that is $R\approx Np_{\min}/q$, then $\mathrm{FDP}\approx\min\{1,q/(Np_{\min})\}$ and $\mathrm{FDR}\approx\pi_0q\log(1/(\pi_0q))$. The Daniels loss would be attained and the main target would be false. The main target therefore contains the statement that Gaussian alternatives cannot track the extreme of independent nulls. Class E states exactly that, without any collective null structure.

Reasons to expect $O(q)$:

1. Budget. Each alternative satisfies $\sum_ib_{ji}^2\le1$, so it can respond at unit rate to one null only.
2. Decorrelation. For every unit vector $c$ and $n$ independent standard normal nulls, Gaussian integration by parts gives $\mathrm{Cov}(\langle c,G_0\rangle,\max_iG_i)=\sum_ic_i\,\mathbb P(i=\arg\max)\le n^{-1/2}$. Alternatives see linear functionals only.
3. Self-averaging. The aggregate count of many independent ladders is deterministic by the law of large numbers and cannot adapt to which null is extreme. A single perfectly correlated ladder carries one standard normal shift that is independent of the extreme, and a direct computation gives excess $O(q)$ because the logarithmic singularity in the shift is integrable.
4. Forced rejections. A ladder that is not being cut is rejected in full and raises the rank floor (idea_420827cf1b7e, idea_121635a0e4a9).

What a proof needs beyond the capped lemma: after conditioning on linear functionals of independent nulls the residual nulls are weakly negatively correlated, so the defect lemma (NM) of idea_3e0a9b1ec5d2 is required, and the carrier has to be restricted to the directions that actually steer.

First tests, exact or by short simulation:

- $n$ nulls, each with its own ladder of $N/n$ perfectly anti-correlated alternatives. Prediction: total excess of order $(q/N)\log N$ for $n=1$ and of order $q/N$ for $n\ge2$ equal ladders.
- One ladder loaded on the normalized sum of the nulls. Prediction: excess of order $q\sqrt{\log N/n}$.
- Adversarial search over the loadings $b_j$ and means for $N\le10$ at $q\in\{0.05,0.2\}$.

A family in class E with $\mathrm{FDR}/q$ growing like $\log(1/q)$ would refute the main target. A proof of (E) would be the first statement with unrestricted dependence between nulls and alternatives and unrestricted dimension.

## Class C+: nonnegatively correlated nulls

Model: $\Sigma_{ii'}\ge0$ for all $i,i'\in I_0$; alternatives arbitrary. Core I is free here, since the null p-values are PRDS among themselves and linking gives $q\{1+\log(1/q)\}$. The one-sided family behind the [lower bound](../assets/lei2026_one_sided_fdr_lower.md) lies in this class: the source states on p. 4 that its null pairs are positively correlated and its null and non-null pairs negatively correlated. On C+ the open gap is therefore exactly $\sqrt{\log}$ against $\log$, and a bound $Cq\sqrt{\log(1/q)}$ on C+ would be a sharp-order theorem on a natural class.

Reachable now: carriers with one direction on the nulls, item (B1) of idea_121635a0e4a9.

Next sub-class: models in which the conditional covariance of the nulls given all alternatives is entrywise nonnegative. Then the carrier is the alternative vector $G_1$, the rank floor is the alternatives-only BH rank $R_1$, and with $\rho_i(x;G_1)$ the conditional tail ratio of null $i$ the remaining object is the capped maximal game
$$
\mathbb E\min\Big\{\frac1q,\ \frac1N\sum_{i\in I_0}\ \sup_{u_q\le x\le c_{R_1(G_1)}}\rho_i(x;G_1)\Big\}\ \overset{?}{\le}\ C\sqrt{\log(1/q)} ,
$$
in which the cap and the conditional null means are functions of the same vector $G_1$. Warning: conditioning on alternatives over-reveals when some alternatives are positively correlated copies of nulls; those must stay outside the carrier.
