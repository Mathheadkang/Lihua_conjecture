## Proposed constructive mechanism

Assume temporarily that the centered null scores X_1,...,X_m are independent. Every alternative j can be written
$$
T_j=mu_j+\sum_{i=1}^m a_{ij}X_i+b_j\cdot H,
\qquad \sum_i a_{ij}^2+||b_j||^2=1.
$$
Thus each alternative satisfies the genuine Bessel budget sum_i a_ij^2<=1, and sum_i |a_ij|^l<=1 for l>=2. This motivates cancelling the own-null BH boundaries exactly, paying a first-order term separately, and charging the remaining alternative rank faces by squared correlations while retaining the whole signed scalar generating function.

The next calculation supplies the exact own-null cancellation, then a concrete obstruction to a uniform raw-squared-correlation remainder certificate. It does not rule out a covariance-conditioned or otherwise nonlinear allocation.

## Exact first equation: own-null antiderivative

Force null i's p-value to zero, retaining all other Gaussian scores and largest BH rank. Let r_i(x,w) be that leave-zero rank when X_i=x and w denotes the remaining independent Gaussian coordinates. For a fixed rank r, write t_r=qr/N, u_r=u(t_r), and define
$$
F_r(x)=\int_{-\infty}^x phi(z)
\left\{\frac{1_{\{z>=u_r\}}}{r}-\frac qN\right\}dz
=\begin{cases}
-(q/N)Phi(x),&x<u_r,\\
(q/N-1/r)barPhi(x),&x>=u_r.
\end{cases}
$$
Both endpoint limits are zero and the two branches match at u_r. On every finite piecewise-constant trajectory of r_i, the own-null leave-zero identity and telescoping give
$$
E\left[\frac{1_{\{X_i>=u(qr_i(X_i,w)/N)\}}}{r_i(X_i,w)}-\frac qN
\,\middle|\,w\right]
=\sum_{\text{rank jumps }x=b}
\{F_{r_i(b^-)}(b)-F_{r_i(b^+)}(b)\}.
$$
This is an exact signed alternative-face formula; it does not charge the null's own rejection threshold as a separate face. For fixed x, F_r(x) is nondecreasing in r. Decreasing leave-zero ranks therefore produce positive flux. Under independent nulls, x changes other scores only through alternative coefficients a_ij, so the face coarea coefficient is initially linear in a_ij. Bessel only controls its square. A hoped-for strategy was to subtract a regular linear response and show the remainder has quadratic payment.

## Concrete first adversarial test: two nearly duplicate alternatives

Fix q with U=u(q)>=1. Let N=3, X,H independent standard normals, and take
$$
T_0=X,\qquad
T_+=U+\frac{H+\epsilon X}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^2}},
\qquad
T_-=U+\frac{H-\epsilon X}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^2}}.
$$
There is exactly one true null. Both alternative means U are positive. All variances are one, and the joint covariance is a legitimate singular Gaussian Gram matrix. No non-Gaussian selector or modified BH procedure is introduced. Swapping epsilon to -epsilon exchanges the two alternatives, so FDR is even in epsilon.

Write c_1=u(q/3), c_2=u(2q/3), c_3=U, s=sqrt(1+epsilon^2), and d_2=s(c_2-U)>0. Take epsilon>0.

For X>=U, all three tests reject exactly when H>=epsilon X, because the smaller alternative score then reaches U. If that fails, the null can belong to a rank-two rejection precisely when
$$
X>=c_2,\qquad d_2-\epsilon X<=H<\epsilon X.
$$
This interval exists only for X>d_2/(2epsilon). If both ranks three and two fail, the null is rejected exactly when X>=c_1, at rank one. For X<U it is never rejected. These are the actual largest-rank comparisons, including multiplicities; equality endpoints have zero Gaussian measure.

Consequently the following proxy differs from actual FDP only on that rank-two strip:
$$
g_\epsilon(X,H)=
\tfrac13 1_{\{X>=U,H>=\epsilon X\}}
+1_{\{X>=c_1,H<\epsilon X\}}.
$$
For all sufficiently small epsilon, d_2/(2epsilon)>c_1, and the exact correction is minus one half times the strip probability. In particular its absolute size is at most
$$
\tfrac12\,barPhi(d_2/(2epsilon))=o(\epsilon).
$$
The proxy expectation is
$$
E g_\epsilon=
\frac13\int_U^\infty phi(x)barPhi(\epsilon x)dx
+\int_{c_1}^\infty phi(x)Phi(\epsilon x)dx.
$$
Dominated differentiation is justified by x phi(x). At epsilon=0 this equals q/3, and its right derivative is
$$
c(q)=phi(0)\{\phi(c_1)-\tfrac13\phi(U)\}
=\frac q3 phi(0)\{h(c_1)-h(U)\}>0,
\qquad h=phi/barPhi.
$$
Therefore the exact actual FDR satisfies
$$
FDR_\epsilon=\frac q3+c(q)|\epsilon|+o(|\epsilon|).
$$
For small q, c(q) is asymptotic to q phi(0)log(3)/(3U). This remains compatible with the main qU target; it is not a main-target counterexample.

## What the test falsifies

The total raw null-alternative squared correlation is
$$
\sum_{j\in\{+,-\}} Corr(T_0,T_j)^2
=\frac{2\epsilon^2}{1+\epsilon^2}.
$$
Hence there is no finite coefficient depending only on fixed q that bounds the FDR increase over the independent-null endpoint by this raw Bessel mass. A regular linear Taylor subtraction cannot repair this symmetric singular example: FDR is even but has a nonzero cusp at epsilon=0, and thus is not differentiable there. This rejects that specific uniform quadratic remainder strategy, not every use of Bessel or every signed-face kernel.

The exact reason a conditional rather than raw budget differs is visible algebraically. The alternative covariance C has eigenvalues 2/(1+epsilon^2) and 2epsilon^2/(1+epsilon^2). Its cross vector with the null is a=(epsilon,-epsilon)/sqrt(1+epsilon^2), and
$$
a C^{-1}a^{\mathsf T}=1
$$
for every epsilon>0: the alternative difference reveals X exactly. Arbitrarily small marginal correlations need not mean weak collective information near singular covariance. Paying inverse conditional variance without retaining this geometry would introduce the familiar singularity rather than solve it.

## Status and possible continuation

The own-null signed face identity and the three-test rank/cusp calculation are new analytic first tests, agent-produced and not independently verified by a native checking stage. The contemplated raw-squared Bessel allocation fails already in this finite legal model. A continuation would need a leverage-sensitive signed resummation with a proved way to pay near-singular alternative differences, or a separate exact treatment of those differences. Neither has been assumed as a master budget, and no pending main-target plan is asserted on this record.
