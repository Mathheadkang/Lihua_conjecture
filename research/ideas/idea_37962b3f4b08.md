Round-eight candidate checks suggest a reusable restricted-family step, not a proof of the unrestricted target. Use the target's isotropic product population, zero residual variance, radius R, spherical tail H_r, total rejection mass F_r, null mass pi_0, q=2Q(u), and actual smallest finite contact x_r.

Suppose a deterministic a>=u satisfies F_r(a)>=2Q(a)/q for every r>0. Then a is admissible, so u<=x_r<=a, without differentiating or selecting a different contact. Monotonicity in the cutoff, rather than in radius, gives

$$
\operatorname{FDP}_{\mathrm{pop}}(r)\le q\pi_0\frac{H_r(u)}{Q(a)}.
$$

For an independent uniform direction V, V^T Z is N(0,1), hence E H_R(u)=Q(u). Averaging gives

$$
\operatorname{FDR}_{\mathrm{pop}}\le q\pi_0\frac{Q(u)}{Q(a)}.
$$

One sufficient strong-mass premise is m=W([a,infinity))>0 and 2Q(a)/q<=m/2. Symmetry gives H_r(a-mu)>=1/2 for mu>=a, so F_r(a)>=m/2 and F_r(x_r)>=m/2. The denominator form of FDP then also gives the bound

$$
\operatorname{FDR}_{\mathrm{pop}}\le\frac{2q\pi_0}{m}.
$$

A localized saturation variant is useful: if L>0, a>=u, m=W([a+L,infinity))>0, and 2Q(a)/q<=m, then for 0<r<=L this entire mass is rejected at a and at x_r. Consequently

$$
\operatorname{FDR}_{\mathrm{pop}}\le\frac{q\pi_0}{m}+\mathbb P(R>L).
$$

This follows from FDP(r)<=2pi_0 H_r(u)/m below L and FDP<=1 outside; no moment assumption on W is used. These lemmas apply only when the displayed deterministic mass/cutoff premises have been established. They do not provide those premises for arbitrary W, nor control unrestricted contact births, switches or positive variation. The round-eight candidates with half the mass at means at least 2u, their infinite-mean strong-tail variants, and the dominant-mean growing-dimensional family provide explicit applications; their recorded parameters and tail comparisons remain in the round-eight search record.
