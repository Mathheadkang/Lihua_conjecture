## Scope
This is an analytic obstruction for the round-2 boundary search, not a proof of the general target. Assume every null loading norm belongs to {0,1}; alternative means and loadings remain arbitrary, null means may be any theta_i<=0, and the grouping is any admissible oriented null grouping, including any assignments of zero loadings. No positive lower bound on pi0 or any group weight is used. No numerical computation was required.

## Endpoint decomposition
Let beta be the number of zero-loading nulls divided by all m tests. For each existing group h, let alpha_h be its number of full-loading nulls divided by m, and let beta_h count its zero-loading nulls divided by m, so pi_h=alpha_h+beta_h and beta=sum_h beta_h. Set 0 log(1/0)=0. Every zero-loading null p-value is independent of all other p-values and superuniform, so the ordinary BH leave-one-out identity bounds the sum of their FDR contributions by q beta.

For a full-loading null in group h, write U_h=barPhi(v_h^T Z). Its p-value is at least U_h because its mean is nonpositive. On any event with a rejected full-loading null in this group, U_h<=qR/m<=q. If V_h is the number of rejected full-loading nulls in group h, then V_h<=m alpha_h and therefore pointwise

$$
\frac{V_h}{R\vee1}\leq \mathbf1\{U_h\leq q\}\min\left\{1,\frac{q\alpha_h}{U_h}\right\}.
$$

The marginal U_h is uniform, regardless of dependence between projections. Integration yields

$$
\mathbb E\frac{V_h}{R\vee1}\leq q\alpha_h\left(1+\log\frac1{\alpha_h}\right).
$$

For alpha_h=0 both sides are zero. Thus

$$
\operatorname{FDR}\leq q\left[\pi_0+\sum_h\alpha_h\log\frac1{\alpha_h}\right].
$$

## Entropy comparison for arbitrary assignments of zero loadings
For pi_h>0 put t_h=alpha_h/pi_h in [0,1]. Then

$$
\alpha_h\log\frac1{\alpha_h}-\pi_h\log\frac1{\pi_h}
=\pi_h t_h\log\frac1{t_h}-\pi_h(1-t_h)\log\frac1{\pi_h}
\leq\frac{\pi_h}{e}.
$$

Here pi_h<=1 and t log(1/t)<=1/e. Summing gives the uniform mixed-endpoint bound

$$
\operatorname{FDR}\leq\min\left\{1,\ q\left[(1+e^{-1})\pi_0+\mathcal E_0\right]\right\}.
$$

Since 1+e^{-1}<3/2 and the target's loading-coefficient term is nonnegative, every such mixed-endpoint model satisfies the proposed finite-q bound. This remains true for vanishing pi0 and arbitrarily small group masses. No claim is made here for null norms strictly between 0 and 1.
