## Why this is a different tool

The two approved conditional tools need conditional independence. The [Lei conditional leave-one-out FDP bound](../assets/lei2026_conditional_fdp_bound.md) keeps the level supremum inside the null sum; the [finite-level conditional compound-p calibration](../assets/gaussian_bh_conditional_compound_calibration.md) moves it outside. Both stop at singular or strongly positively dependent residuals (duplicates, tight clusters, clones), and neither uses the fact that alternatives which are rejected anyway push the BH rank up. The lemma below replaces conditional independence by positive regression dependence on the nulls, allows arbitrary non-uniform null marginals, and adds a rank floor.

## Lemma (finite, no Gaussian assumption)

Let $p_1,\dots,p_N\in[0,1]$, a fixed null set $I_0$, $t_k=qk/N$, and $R$ the ordinary largest BH rank. Assume PRDS on $I_0$ in the form: for every $i\in I_0$ and every coordinatewise increasing measurable $D\subseteq[0,1]^N$, the map $t\mapsto\mathbb P(p\in D\mid p_i\le t)$ is nondecreasing where $\mathbb P(p_i\le t)>0$. No marginal assumption is made. Put
$$
a_{i,k}=\frac{\mathbb P(p_i\le t_k)}{k},\qquad b_{i,k}=\mathbb P(R\ge k\mid p_i\le t_k),\qquad a_{i,0}=0,\quad b_{i,N+1}=0 .
$$
Then for every $i\in I_0$ and every integer $1\le r_0\le N$,
$$
\mathbb E\Big[\frac{\mathbf 1\{i\in\mathcal R,\ R\ge r_0\}}{R}\Big]\le a_{i,r_0}b_{i,r_0}+\sum_{k>r_0}(a_{i,k}-a_{i,k-1})\,b_{i,k}\le a_{i,r_0}+\sum_{k>r_0}(a_{i,k}-a_{i,k-1})_+ .
$$
If $k\mapsto a_{i,k}$ is unimodal on $\{r_0,\dots,N\}$, the right side equals $\max_{k\ge r_0}a_{i,k}$, and therefore
$$
\mathbb E[\mathrm{FDP};R\ge r_0]\le\sum_{i\in I_0}\max_{k\ge r_0}a_{i,k}.
$$

Proof. On $\{R=k\}$ the null $i$ is rejected exactly when $p_i\le t_k$, so the left side is $\sum_{k\ge r_0}k^{-1}\mathbb P(p_i\le t_k,R=k)$. With $\beta_k(t)=\mathbb P(R\ge k\mid p_i\le t)$ one has $k^{-1}\mathbb P(p_i\le t_k,R=k)=a_{i,k}[\beta_k(t_k)-\beta_{k+1}(t_k)]$. The set $\{R\le k\}$ is increasing in $p$, so PRDS makes $\beta_{k+1}$ nonincreasing, and $\beta_{k+1}(t_k)\ge\beta_{k+1}(t_{k+1})=b_{i,k+1}$. Hence the left side is at most $\sum_{k\ge r_0}a_{i,k}(b_{i,k}-b_{i,k+1})$; summation by parts gives the middle expression, and $0\le b_{i,k}\le1$ gives the last one. Terms with $\mathbb P(p_i\le t_k)=0$ vanish. For uniform nulls and $r_0=1$ this is the classical PRDS proof, where $a_{i,k}\equiv q/N$.

## Gaussian instance

Let $T\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\Gamma)$ with arbitrary $\mu$, $\Gamma_{jj}\le1$, and $\Gamma_{ij}\ge0$ for every $i\in I_0$ and every $j$; let $p_j=\overline\Phi(T_j)$. The matrix $\Gamma$ may be singular.

(i) PRDS on $I_0$ holds: given $T_i=x$ the remaining coordinates are Gaussian with a mean that is coordinatewise nondecreasing in $x$ and a fixed covariance. A degenerate coordinate $\Gamma_{ii}=0$ is trivial.

(ii) With $c_k=\overline\Phi^{-1}(t_k)$ and $\sigma_i=\Gamma_{ii}^{1/2}$, $a_{i,k}=(q/N)\rho_i(c_k)$, where
$$
\rho_i(x)=\frac{\overline\Phi((x-\mu_i)/\sigma_i)}{\overline\Phi(x)} .
$$

(iii) $\rho_i$ is unimodal. Let $\ell_i$ be the density ratio of $\mathcal N(\mu_i,\sigma_i^2)$ to $\mathcal N(0,1)$. Then $\rho_i(x)$ is the average of $\ell_i$ over $[x,\infty)$ under the standard normal tail and $\rho_i'(x)$ has the sign of $\rho_i(x)-\ell_i(x)$. Since $\log\ell_i$ is a concave quadratic (affine when $\sigma_i=1$), $\ell_i$ is unimodal; to the right of its mode $\rho_i<\ell_i$, and at a zero of $\rho_i-\ell_i$ to the left of the mode the derivative of $\rho_i-\ell_i$ equals $-\ell_i'<0$. So there is at most one sign change of $\rho_i'$, from plus to minus.

Consequently, with $u_q=\overline\Phi^{-1}(q)$,
$$
\mathbb E[\mathrm{FDP};R\ge r_0]\le\frac qN\sum_{i\in I_0}\ \sup_{u_q\le x\le c_{r_0}}\rho_i(x).
$$

## Conditional (carrier) form

Suppose $T_j=\theta_j+\langle A_j,W'\rangle+\varepsilon_j$ with $W'\sim\mathcal N(0,I_K)$ independent of $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\Gamma)$, $\|A_j\|^2+\Gamma_{jj}=1$, and $\Gamma_{ij}\ge0$ for $i\in I_0$ and all $j$. The dimension $K$ is arbitrary. For every measurable $r_0(W')\in\{1,\dots,N\}$,
$$
\mathrm{FDR}\le\mathbb E\min\Big\{1,\ \mathbb P(1\le R<r_0\mid W')+\frac qN\sum_{i\in I_0}\ \sup_{u_q\le x\le c_{r_0(W')}}\frac{\overline\Phi\big((x-\theta_i-\langle A_i,W'\rangle)/\sigma_i\big)}{\overline\Phi(x)}\Big\}.
$$
For $\sigma_i=0$ read the ratio as $\mathbf 1\{\theta_i+\langle A_i,W'\rangle\ge x\}/\overline\Phi(x)$. If the alternatives are $W'$-measurable one may take $r_0=\max\{R_1(W'),1\}$, where $R_1$ is the BH rank of the alternatives alone against the same line; the probability term then vanishes. This turns the non-null cap of the earlier family-specific notes idea_61a3f931c742, idea_716df438c4ed and idea_11028fd850f5 into one general inequality.

## Size of the capped envelope

For a boundary null with carrier loading $r=\|A_i\|$, $s=\sqrt{1-r^2}$ and a deterministic cap $X\ge u_q$, put $G(r,q,X)=\mathbb E\sup_{u_q\le x\le X}\rho_i(x)$.

- Rigorous for every $r$: $G\le1+\log\{q/\overline\Phi(X)\}$ (conditional Jensen, then the one-coordinate computation $\int_0^q dp/\max\{p,\overline\Phi(X)\}$).
- Supported by quadrature and by a short argument not yet written in full (it needs the location of the mode of $\rho_i$): $G\le2+r(X-u_q)/(s\sqrt{2\pi})$, using the pointwise density bound in [Lei integrated conditional Gaussian tail-ratio bounds](../assets/lei2026_integrated_conditional_tail_ratios.md).
- Quadrature: for $r\le0.5$, $G=1+r(X-u_q)/\sqrt{2\pi}$ within a few percent; as $r\to1$ it rises to the first bound.

## Checks done

400 random PRDS Gaussian configurations with arbitrary means, $N\le12$, $q\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2,0.3\}$: with $r_0=1$ the bound never fails beyond Monte Carlo error and is often tight (largest ratio 1.004). 2115 tests of the capped form over all $r_0$: no violation. Unimodality of $\rho_i$: no violation on a grid of $(\mu_i,\sigma_i)$.

## Limits

The supremum stays inside the null sum. Under conditional PRDS it cannot be moved outside in general (idea_b0ad85164bdf). The lemma is sharp when the BH rank is essentially fixed by the carrier, and lossy when the rank is small and driven by the nulls themselves.
