At fixed baseline u, geometry, exposure w,y and feasible weights, replacing every threshold a_i>=u by u enlarges the canonical conditional event and leaves the proposed bound unchanged. Thus any violating object also violates at equal thresholds. Removing zero-weight constraints likewise enlarges the event without changing beta or feasibility. If all weights vanish, K<=phi(0)<sqrt(e)h(u) for u>=1, since h(u)>u; that case is excluded. At fixed event and geometry, coordinatewise weight reduction preserves frame feasibility and raises sqrt(e)h(u)S(u)^beta, because 0<S(u)<1. Therefore weight reductions cannot turn a known nonviolation into a violation; a violation at smaller weights remains a violation at a feasible dominating weight vector, if one is independently available. These are exact proof-step reductions, including degenerate canonical slices. They neither construct a dominating or saturated frame nor prove the equal-threshold inequality.
