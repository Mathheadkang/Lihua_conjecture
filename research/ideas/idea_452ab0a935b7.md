## New cutoff: average over a logarithmically wide band

Choose an admissible scaled smooth cutoff chi_a(r)=psi(r/a), where psi is nondecreasing, zero below 1 and one above 2. Fix epsilon,c>0 BEFORE optimizing in q. Let
$$
L=\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+c^{-1}),\quad t_1-t_0=L,\quad
\omega(t)=\frac{\epsilon e^{\epsilon(t-t_0)}}{e^{\epsilon L}-1},
$$
and define
$$
\bar\chi(r)=\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\omega(t)\psi(r/e^t)\,dt.
$$
The weights integrate to one; bar-chi is zero below exp(t0), one above 2 exp(t1), and smooth whenever psi is. Integration by parts gives the exact deterministic identity
$$
r\bar\chi'(r)=\epsilon\bar\chi(r)
+\epsilon c\,\psi(r/e^{t_0})
-\epsilon(1+c)\psi(r/e^{t_1}),
$$
hence
$$
r\bar\chi'(r)\le\epsilon\bar\chi(r)+\epsilon c.
$$
For EVERY actual rank and FDP, therefore,
$$
\mathbb E[R\bar\chi'(R)D]\le
\epsilon F^{\bar\chi}+\epsilon c.
$$
This inequality is independent of dimension, means, covariance, common loading, private smoothing, and rank distribution. In particular it remains useful when rank mass concentrates inside the transition band.

Freeze c and the entire cutoff during level variation. If a fixed-regularizer coercive interval [q_min,q_max] has already been justified, choose c<=q_min S^{-1}(q_min). Since q S^{-1}(q) is increasing on q<=S(1), this yields c<=qU at every optimizing level. Thus the explicit surcharge is at most epsilon F^{bar-chi}+epsilon qU. Do NOT set c equal to the varying qU and silently omit the derivative of the cutoff.

Translate both t0,t1 toward minus infinity to remove the cutoff in value at each fixed model, while retaining the same surcharge estimate. Any required uniform value recovery must still use the verified order of limits; no sigma-uniform microscopic-rank convergence is inferred.
