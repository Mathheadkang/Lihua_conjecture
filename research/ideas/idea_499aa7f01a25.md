For a two-sided model T~N(theta,Sigma), use the exactly Gaussian signed vector (T,-T) with covariance [[Sigma,-Sigma],[-Sigma,Sigma]], unit diagonal and PSD even when singular. At q<1/2 each signed pair has at most one one-sided p-value below q; ranks above N cannot qualify. The remaining small signed p-values equal half the original two-sided p-values, so the largest qualifying BH ranks agree at the same q. Original theta=0 rejected nulls correspond to one signed rejected null apiece; extra signed negative means create additional one-sided true nulls. Thus a candidate pointwise FDP comparison yields F_2(q)<=F_1(q). This is a proposed route, not yet a native independently verified result; prove and cold-audit it as part of the two-sided target after the one-sided bound. The one-sided universal order bound remains unresolved.
