## Restricted family

Consider any finite admissible one-sided Gaussian BH model, with original null set I_0={i:theta_i<=0}, at level q in (0,1). Suppose Sigma_ij<=0 for distinct i,j in I_0 and Sigma_ij=0 whenever i is an original null and j is an alternative. Alternative scores may have arbitrary internal covariance. Dimensions, means, null proportion, and covariance rank may vary arbitrarily with q; singular covariance is allowed. Keep all coordinates, original labels, and the same actual largest BH rejection rank.

## Calculation

Let H be the sigma-field generated by the complete alternative score vector. It contains the complete alternative p-value vector. Joint Gaussianity and zero cross covariance make the null score block independent of this entire alternative block, including singular Gaussian cases. Consequently, under H, the null score law retains mean theta_{I_0} and covariance Sigma_{I_0,I_0}, whose off-diagonal entries remain nonpositive. For every original null and every s in (0,q], its conditional CDF is

$$
F_{i,H}(s)=\overline\Phi(\overline\Phi^{-1}(s)-\theta_i)\le s.
$$

Writing m=|I_0|, we have sum_i F_{i,H}(s)<=m s<=N s simultaneously over s, so the helper's prescribed calibration factor is exactly K=1. Its Gaussian sufficient condition therefore applies under the full final conditioning, and its actual-rank conclusion gives

$$
\mathbb E[\operatorname{FDP}\mid H]\le\min\{1,4e q\},\qquad \operatorname{FDR}\le\min\{1,4e q\}.
$$

This excludes the entire restricted family as a counterexample to the q sqrt(log(1/q)) order assertion, even when the null block has arbitrarily growing rank or dimension: the normalized ratio is at most 4e/sqrt(log(1/q)), which tends to zero.

## Scope

The null-alternative independence is essential to this application, not asserted for arbitrary Gaussian geometry. If cross covariances are nonzero, conditioning on the entire alternative vector can change the null covariance signs, so the above unchanged-law step cannot be used. This is a restricted-family obstruction and does not prove the main universal statement.
