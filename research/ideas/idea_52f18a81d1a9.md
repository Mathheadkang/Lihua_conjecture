Elementary candidate reduction to check in a future main plan, not a uniform bound or monotonicity claim. For fixed q let W_sigma(q) be the supremum over canonical finite Gaussian one-sided BH models with covariance Sigma_sigma=(1-sigma²)Sigma+sigma² 11^T, where Sigma is arbitrary PSD unit diagonal and means arbitrary as in the original model. This is realized by theta+sqrt(1-sigma²)X+sigma Z 1. Every such model is an admissible original model, so W_sigma(q)<=F_1(q). For each fixed original finite model (theta,Sigma), its regularized Gaussian vector converges in law to the original as sigma down to zero, including singular Sigma. The bounded ordinary BH FDP has discontinuities contained in finitely many events p_i=qk/N, each of probability zero under the limit's continuous unit-variance Gaussian marginals. Hence the regularized FDR tends to the original FDR. Therefore liminf_{sigma->0}W_sigma(q) is at least the FDR of every original finite model, so it is at least F_1(q). Combining gives lim_{sigma->0} W_sigma(q)=F_1(q), for each fixed q. No monotonicity in sigma, uniform rate, or exchange of an unproved supremum/limit is asserted. If the current W1 continuity and finite-approximation lemma is proved, the completed tagged population supremum at fixed sigma equals W_sigma(q) by its canonical finite approximation conclusion. Consequently a future upper bound on that completed supremum with constants uniform in sigma would pass to the original envelope; continuity moduli themselves need not be uniform in sigma for this last reduction. The missing work remains the uniform bound, valid first variations at near-maximizers and q-scaled null-boundary payment.
