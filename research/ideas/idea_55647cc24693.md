Elementary organization of the standard uniform-integrability compactness argument for the proposed signed-gradient lemma; not a novelty claim or approved result. On the standard Borel probability space (parameter law mu), L2(mu) is separable. For a uniformly integrable sequence f_j in L1, clip each f_j at level n. The clipped sequence is bounded in L2; extract a weakly convergent subsequence by diagonalizing its coefficients in a countable orthonormal basis and using Bessel/Riesz-Fischer. Diagonalize over integer n. Its weak L2 limit g_n retains |g_n|<=n, by testing indicators of measurable sets, and weak L2 convergence implies convergence against every bounded test because mu is a probability measure. Uniform-integrability clipping errors e_n=sup_j||f_j-clip_n(f_j)||1 tend to zero. Weak lower semicontinuity of the L1 norm then gives ||g_n-g_m||1<=e_n+e_m. Hence g_n converges strongly in L1 to g, and the original diagonal subsequence converges weakly in L1 to g by the three-term clipping estimate.
To upgrade to actual weak compactness without silently invoking Eberlein-Smulian, use a countable generating algebra. Algebra-simple bounded tests approximate every bounded measurable b in mu-probability with a uniform bound. UI makes their integrals against the entire family converge uniformly. Thus on any bounded UI family the weak L1 topology is generated by countably many such tests and is metrizable. UI and L1 norm bounds survive weak closure and convexification: integral_E |f| is weakly lower semicontinuous, as a supremum of bounded linear tests. The weakly closed convex gradient set is therefore metrizable and sequentially compact by the clipping argument, hence compact. Spell out the density of the algebra-simple tests (a monotone-class argument) and the Hilbert subsequence argument. This proves the needed countably additive compactness inline, avoiding a new specialized-source dependency and any finitely additive dual replacement.
