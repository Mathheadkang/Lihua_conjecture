Test objects for the conjecture (T) of idea_9f8ffb204424 on the frozen-ray maximal likelihood ratio and for its Mehler form (S1) in idea_7727ae2d6679, with an estimator whose weights are bounded. Scripts, seeds and tables of the first local runs: research-local/frozen_ray_maxlr_diagnostic_20261110/ (maxlr_check.py, maxlr_tail.py, mehler_blocks.py, k_ratio.py, identity_check.py, RESULTS.txt). Larger runs belong on the configured remote server.

## Estimator

For atoms $x_k$ of norm at most one with weights $p_k$ and $L_w(g)=\sum_kp_k\exp(w\langle x_k,g\rangle-w^2\|x_k\|^2/2)$: draw $j$ uniformly from a grid $w_1=0<\dots<w_J=W_{\max}$ (step $0.5$), draw $k\sim p$, and set $g=w_jx_k+{}$standard Gaussian noise. The proposal has density $\bar L=\tfrac1J\sum_jL_{w_j}$ with respect to the Gaussian law, so $\mathbb E_\gamma\psi=\mathbb E_{\rm prop}[\psi/\bar L]$; for $\psi=\min\{c,\sup_{w\le W_{\max}}L_w\}$ or $\psi=\mathbf 1\{\sup_wL_w\ge\alpha\}$ the weights are at most a constant times $J$, by the curvature bound $\partial_w^2\log L_w\ge-1$. $W_{\max}$ must exceed the largest onset plus $\sqrt{2\log c}$ plus a margin. Only the Gram matrix of the atoms matters; for tree priors the sum over atoms is a recursion of cost $O(K)$ per $w$.

## First results (short local runs; Monte Carlo errors between 0.02 and 0.06)

$\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}$ at $c=10^2,10^4,10^6$; one direction is exactly $1.831,2.301,2.670$.
- Independent directions, $K=30$: $1.66,2.06,2.39$; $K=1000$: $1.50,1.85,2.17$.
- Cluster of $K=300$ atoms $\sqrt\rho\,e_0+\sqrt{1-\rho}\,e_k$, $\rho=0.3$: $1.87,2.25,2.58$; $\rho=0.05$: $1.57,1.92,2.23$.
- Trees $4\times11\times29$ with equal weights: $1.72,2.10,2.41$; $6^4$ with weights $(.1,.2,.3,.4)$: $1.68,2.02,2.32$; $3\times30\times30$ with weights $(.6,.3,.1)$: $1.81,2.25,2.59$.
- Random points on spheres, $K=1500$, $d=3$: $1.71,2.14,2.47$; $d=12$: $1.75,2.15,2.45$.
- Orthogonal atoms with norms $2^{-j}$, $j\le5$: $1.42,1.66,1.88$. Sparse prior ($1\%$ signal over 200 directions): $1.01$.

Tail constants $\alpha\sqrt{4\pi\log\alpha}\,\mathbb P\{\Lambda\ge\alpha\}$ for $\alpha=10,\dots,10^4$: between $0.57$ and $0.93$, against $0.86$ to $0.95$ for one direction. In-zone ratio $JD/(D')^2$ along the SNR path (notation of idea_dc6cf14ab94d): between $0.25$ and $0.61$, against $1/2$ for one direction.

## Families most likely to break (T), in order

1. Cascades with geometrically growing resolved weights and transitions packed inside the zone: levels $l$ with weights $a_l\propto2^l$ and branching chosen so that level $l$ resolves at $w_l$ with $\sum_{k<l}a_k(w_l^2-w_k^2)/2\in[1,\log c]$. Heuristic count: about $\log_2\log c$ transitions fit in the zone, and about $\sqrt{2\log c}$ with equal weights. Measure whether each in-zone transition adds a constant to $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}$.
2. Small coherent weight followed by a secondary transition: the cluster prior with $\rho\to0$ and $K$ tuned so that the atoms resolve when the cluster log-likelihood ratio is mid-zone. The crude bound $J\le D''$ fails to give the arc-length inequality (K) there by a factor of order $\log(1/\rho)$, so this is where the constant could grow.
3. Curves and low-dimensional manifolds of directions with strong local correlation (scan-type index sets), where sub-populations are arcs at all scales.
4. Mixtures of the above with very different norms, so that different sub-populations run on different SNR clocks.
5. For (S1): the same families with exact Mehler kernels at $r\in\{0.5,0.8,0.95\}$ and the level restricted to $x\ge U$. Present data: independent blocks at $q=10^{-2}$ give $1.07\to2.31$ at $r=0.95$ and $1.56\to1.55$ at $r=0.3$ as $K$ goes from $1$ to $1000$; the $q=10^{-4}$ rows of the local run are too noisy to use and need a better proposal.
6. Direct ascent on the Gram matrix for $K\le64$ atoms with common random numbers.

## What each outcome means

All families at or below $\sqrt{\log c/\pi}+O(1)$ with tail constants below one: support for the sharp form; record the largest additive excess. Excess growing like a multiple of $\sqrt{\log c}$: the sharp constant fails in the weak-loading class while the order may survive; hand the family to a BH realization test (can non-nulls steer the level to the maximizing SNR?). Excess growing faster than $\sqrt{\log c}$: (T) is false and the family is a counterexample lead for the order target inside the weak-loading population class. A finite grid is neither proof nor refutation.
