## Differential cancellation

For unit-variance jointly Gaussian $T_i$ and deterministic masses $\omega_i>0$ with $\sum_i\omega_i=1$, define
$$
X(x)=\sum_i\omega_i(T_i-x)_+,\quad
F(x)=\sum_i\omega_i1_{\{T_i>x\}},\quad
\psi(x)=\phi(x)-x\bar\Phi(x),\quad R(x)=X(x)/\psi(x).
$$
Away from coordinate breakpoints, $X'=-F$ and $\psi'=-\bar\Phi$. At a level crossing $R(x)=a$,
$$
R'(x)=\frac{a\bar\Phi(x)-F(x)}{\psi(x)}.
$$
Thus an upcrossing has $F(x)<a\bar\Phi(x)$. This cancellation is absent when each threshold scale is charged separately.

Let $U_a$ count strict upcrossings on $x>0$. Since $R$ is continuous and eventually zero for every finite realization,
$\Pr(\sup_{x\ge0}R(x)>a)\le\Pr(R(0)\ge a)+\mathbb EU_a$,
under a convention covering tangencies/exceptional levels. Since $\mathbb ER(0)=1$, the first term is at most $1/a$. Therefore a bound $\mathbb EU_A\le1/4$ at one universal $A>2$ would give a uniform positive acceptance probability and, after the hinge reduction and amplification, the Simes claim. This expected-upcrossing bound is stronger than necessary and has not been proved or falsified.

## Active-set formula

For a nonempty active set $I$, let
$$
\kappa=\sum_{i\in I}\omega_i,\qquad
\mu_I=\kappa^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}\omega_iT_i,\qquad
b_\kappa(x)=x+a\psi(x)/\kappa.
$$
On the region where exactly $I$ is active, $X=\kappa(\mu_I-x)$. An upcrossing has $\mu_I=b_\kappa(x)$ and $\kappa<a\bar\Phi(x)$; hence $x<z_{\kappa/a}$, where $z_s=\bar\Phi^{-1}(s)$. For $a\ge2$, a Rice/coarea calculation suggests the contribution
$$
\int_0^{z_{\kappa/a}}
\left(\frac{a\bar\Phi(x)}{\kappa}-1\right)
f_{\mu_I}(b_\kappa(x))
\Pr\{\text{active set}=I\mid \mu_I=b_\kappa(x)\}\,dx,
$$
where $f_{\mu_I}$ is the scalar Gaussian density when $\mu_I$ has positive variance. A zero-variance active average cannot equal the positive crossing threshold, and contributes zero. Singular joint laws, atoms at tangency levels, and the exact upcrossing convention require a formal regularization argument before using this as a theorem.

The change of variable $y=b_\kappa(x)$ cancels the displayed derivative factor. It leaves a sum over active sets whose conditional probabilities are evaluated at different thresholds and under different conditioning. They cannot be summed as disjoint probabilities. Bounding them by one loses dimension control.
