Final round5 slot1 nonisotropic boundary diagnostic. No admissible finite Gaussian BH counterexample is supplied and no candidate is written. This checks a genuinely anisotropic angular regime, rather than repeating isotropic dimension choices. It also illustrates why independent maximization of two coupled effects is not a valid counterexample mechanism.

Angular object and exact identity: in R^(m+1), take v=(sqrt(rho),sqrt(1-rho)w) with w uniform on S^(m-1), rho in [0,1]. For G=(Z,H), R=||H||, the small-loading angular likelihood curve factors exactly as

L_rho(a,Z,R)=exp(a sqrt(rho) Z-a^2 rho/2) L_m(a sqrt(1-rho),R).

Both factors use the SAME a. Supremizing each separately discards that constraint and creates a two-parameter product envelope which may have an extra logarithmic loss. That relaxation does not establish a realizable BH FDR gain.

Critical nonisotropic scaling: take fixed kappa>=0, rho_m=kappa/sqrt(m) for large m and a=m^(1/4)b. Let Y_m=(||H||^2-m)/sqrt(2m). The spherical Laplace expansion on bounded b and bounded Y_m predicts

log L_rho_m(m^(1/4)b,Z,||H||) -> sqrt(kappa)b Z-kappa b^2/2+b^2 Y/sqrt(2)-b^4/4,

where Z,Y are independent standard normals. This compact-parameter limit and its required uniform remainder are not independently verified here; no joint q,m,r,N limit is claimed. The exact limiting curve itself can nevertheless be checked without that transfer.

Exact limiting-envelope calculation: put mu_kappa(b)=(sqrt(kappa)b,b^2/sqrt(2)) for b>=0 and M_kappa(g)=sup_b exp(mu_kappa(b).g-||mu_kappa(b)||^2/2), g in R^2. There is a universal constant C with E min(1,q M_kappa(G_2))<=C q[1+sqrt(log(1/q))], uniformly in every kappa>=0. Proof: with phi_2 the standard planar Gaussian density, phi_2(g)M_kappa(g)=sup_b phi_2(g-mu_kappa(b)). The curve has nondecreasing nonnegative coordinates. Its segment inside radius 2B+2 has arclength at most the sum of coordinate increments, at most sqrt(2)(2B+2). An arclength-one net has J<=sqrt(2)(2B+2)+2 points. Every curve point in this segment lies within one of a net point, so the inequality ||x-y||^2>=||x||^2/2-||y||^2 gives

phi_2(g-mu)<=2exp(1/2) phi_(2I)(g-mu_net).

Hence the integral of the supremum translated density from this segment is at most 2exp(1/2)J. On ||g||<=B, the remaining curve has ||mu||>2B+2, thus distance ||g-mu||>B+2 and supremum density at most (2pi)^(-1)exp(-(B+2)^2/2). On ||g||>B, simply bound the capped integral by Pr(||G_2||>B)=exp(-B^2/2). Consequently

E min(1,q M_kappa)<=2exp(1/2)q J+(q B^2/2)exp(-(B+2)^2/2)+exp(-B^2/2).

Choosing B=sqrt(2log(1/q)) for q<=exp(-1/2) gives the claimed universal q[1+sqrt(log(1/q))] bound. No independent maximizers or fictitious BH ranks occur in this limiting-envelope proof. It remains valid for kappa=0, where the curve is a half-line.

Limits: an arbitrary angular measure need not yield this monotone planar curve. The compact spherical limit above, finite-r conditional tails, finite angular discretization, valid rare-alternative survival and the original largest BH crossing all require separate control. No maximal envelope is assumed attainable by a finite testing model. Therefore neither the anisotropic latitude ansatz nor its invalid split-product relaxation currently produces a new plausible checked main counterexample; arbitrary angular geometry remains unresolved.
