Round-4 search-summary proof step, derived from the current stage pack; this is a deterministic argument, not a proof of the whole compression target. For any finite empirical score tail H_n and fixed z, define continuous crossings by H_n(u_q(r)-sigma z)>=r, 0<r<=1. Any crossing r has k=n H_n(u_q(r)-sigma z) an integer with k/n>=r. Since u_q is decreasing, H_n(u_q(k/n)-sigma z)>=k/n, so a grid crossing at least r exists. Conversely each grid crossing is a continuous crossing. Therefore the supremum of continuous crossings, with zero for the empty set, is exactly the actual largest non-strict BH rank. At a positive actual rank r=k/n, if j=n H_n(u_q(r)-sigma z)>k, the same argument makes j/n>r a grid crossing, a contradiction; hence H_n(u_q(r)-sigma z)=r, including ties and r=1.

Suppose sup_t |H(t)-H_m(t)|<=gamma, r>=a is a qualifying rank of either law, 0<gamma<a/2, L=sup_{a/2<=s<=1}|u_q'(s)|, and h=L gamma/sigma. The mean-value bound gives u_q(r-gamma)-sigma(z+h)<=u_q(r)-sigma z. Monotonicity of score tails and the empirical error imply the other tail at the left threshold is at least r-gamma. Continuous/grid identification then gives an actual rank of the other law at z+h at least r-gamma. This works in both directions regardless of atom masses, sample multiplicities or number of roots. It explains why a missing tiny false seed cannot by itself evade macroscopic common-shift transfer. It also supplies the exact self-consistency premise used in the board's mixed-FDP comparison.
