## Setting and target

For $N$ coordinates with common correlation $\rho\in(0,1)$, write $Y_i=\sqrt\rho\,W+\sigma E_i$ with $\sigma=\sqrt{1-\rho}$ and $u_k=\overline\Phi^{-1}(qk/N)$. Let $F_N(\rho)$ be the Simes size. On the Schur-square path $\rho(t)=(1-t)\rho_0+t\rho_0^2$ one has $\dot\rho=-\rho(1-\rho)$, so
$$
f'(t)=-D(\rho(t)),\qquad D(\rho):=\rho(1-\rho)F_N'(\rho).
$$
The estimate to prove or refute is $D(\rho)\le Cq$ for all $N\ge1$, $\rho\in(0,1)$ and $q\le q_0$, with $C$ universal. Equivalently, the descent rate per unit of $\log(1-\rho)$ is $O(q)$.

## Exact formula (derivation complete)

**Root threshold.** The Simes event is $\{W\ge w^*\}$ with
$$
w^*=\min_k\frac{u_k-\sigma E_{(k)}}{\sqrt\rho},
$$
and $k^*$ the minimizing rank, which is almost surely unique except at finitely many $\rho$. Hence $F_N(\rho)=\mathbb E\,\overline\Phi(w^*)$.

**Differentiation.**
- Each candidate $g_k(\rho)$ is $C^1$, and $\partial_\rho g_k=(E_{(k)}/\sigma-u_k)/(2\rho^{3/2})$.
- Locally in $\rho$, $w^*$ is Lipschitz with an integrable constant, so differentiation under the expectation is justified.
- At the binding rank, $u_{k^*}=\sqrt\rho\,w^*+\sigma E_{(k^*)}$.

These give
$$
D(\rho)=\tfrac12\,\mathbb E\bigl[\varphi(w^*)\,(w^*-\sqrt\rho\,u_{k^*})\bigr]
=\frac{\sigma}{2\sqrt\rho}\,\mathbb E\bigl[\varphi(w^*)\,(\sigma u_{k^*}-E_{(k^*)})\bigr].
$$
This matches central finite differences to five digits. It is also a smooth, unbiased Monte Carlo estimator. Sorting the noise once per sample serves every $\rho$, which allows $N=10^6$.

**Leave-one-out form.** Let the $N-1$ other coordinates be i.i.d. $\mathcal N(\sqrt\rho\,w,\sigma^2)$ given $W=w$. Put
$$
\Lambda_r(w)=\mathbb P_w\bigl(Y'_{(r-1)}>u_r,\ Y'_{(k)}\le u_k\ (k<r),\ Y'_{(k)}\le u_{k+1}\ (k\ge r)\bigr).
$$
Then
$$
D=\tfrac12\,N\sqrt\rho\,\sigma\sum_r\varphi(u_r)\,\mathbb E\bigl[Z\,\Lambda_r(\sqrt\rho\,u_r+\sigma Z)\bigr],\qquad Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1).
$$
- $\Lambda_1$ is decreasing in $w$, so binding at rank 1 always contributes negatively.
- For $r\ge2$, $\Lambda_r$ is the probability of an up-set intersected with a down-set.

**Integration by parts.**
- $D=\tfrac12\mathbb E[(W^2-1)\pi(W)]-\tfrac12\sqrt\rho\sum_k u_k\,\mathbb E[\beta\Psi_k(W)]$. Here $\pi(w)=\mathbb P(\text{reject}\mid W=w)$, $\beta=\sqrt\rho/\sigma$, and $\beta\Psi_k$ is the part of $\pi'$ where rank $k$ binds.
- Dropping $u_k\ge u_q$ gives $D\le\tfrac12T$ with $T=\mathbb E[(W^2-\sqrt\rho\,u_qW-1)\pi(W)]$.
- For $N=1$ and in the clone limit, $T$ vanishes through the exact identity $\mathbb E[W^2-aW-1\mid Y\ge u]=0$ with $a=\sqrt\rho\,u$.

## Limits

**$\rho\to0$.** For fixed $N$, $F_N'$ is bounded near $0$, so $D=O(\rho)$. Since $F_N(\rho)\le q=F_N(0)$, the derivative $F_N'(0)\le0$, so near $0$ the path rises and there is no descent. Exact values for $N=10$ and $30$ at $q=0.1$ and $10^{-6}$ give $D\le0$ on $\rho\le0.85$.

**$\rho\to1$.** Using $|w^*-\sqrt\rho\,u_{k^*}|\le\sigma(\sigma|w^*|+|E_{(k^*)}|)$, one gets $D=O(\sigma\sqrt{\log N})\to0$ for fixed $N$. This is not uniform in $N$: the peak moves to $1-\rho\asymp1/u_q^2$, but its height stays bounded.

**Joint limit.** Set $1-\rho=\kappa^2/u_q^2$ and let $N\to\infty$ first. The binding rank is then dense with law-of-large-numbers order statistics, and the binding noise quantile $Z$ satisfies the hazard balance $h(Z)=\varphi(Z)/\overline\Phi(Z)=\kappa$. Then let $q\to0$. With $\delta=\kappa-Z=h(Z)-Z>0$:
$$
\frac{F}{q}\to\frac{\varphi(\delta)}{\kappa},\qquad
\frac{D}{q}\to\tfrac12\,\delta\,\varphi(\delta)\le\tfrac12\varphi(1)=0.120985 .
$$
- The maximum is attained at $\delta=1$, where $\kappa=0.519$ and $F\approx0.466q$.
- The limiting downward variation along one path is at most $0.1207q$.

## Numerical evidence

Exact values below use Gaussian quadrature over $W$ with an exact multinomial dynamic program. The method was checked against the bivariate formula ($N=2$), against $N=1$ (giving $q$) and against independence (giving $q$).

| $N$ | $q$ | max over the $\rho$ grid of $D/q$ | location |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 (exact) | $0.1$ | $0.094$ | $\rho=0.98$ |
| 10 (exact) | $10^{-6}$ | $0.100$ | $\rho=0.995$ |
| 30 (exact) | $0.1$ | $0.109$ | $\rho=0.95$ |
| 30 (exact) | $10^{-6}$ | $0.110$ | $\rho=0.99$–$0.995$ |
| $10^2$ to $10^6$ (Monte Carlo) | $10^{-1}$ to $10^{-6}$ | $0.116$–$0.121$ | $1-\rho\approx(0.4\text{ to }2)/(2\log(N/q))$ |

- Monte Carlo spikes $D/q\approx0.45$–$0.5$ at $\rho\approx0.33$ for $q=10^{-6}$ were rare-event artifacts. The exact value there is $-0.0014$ ($N=10$) and $-0.0027$ ($N=30$).
- For $q\le10^{-6}$ and small $\rho$ the Monte Carlo is unusable; only the exact runs are trusted there.
- $D$ changes sign once: negative on small and moderate $\rho$, positive only in the near-clone band.

## Why a proof is not yet available

Every bound that discards signs fails.
- **Relaxed quantity $T/2q$:** it grows as $q\to0$ ($0.38,\ 1.2,\ 2.1$ at $q=10^{-1},10^{-3},10^{-6}$). The terms $u_k-u_q$ cannot be dropped.
- **Positive part** $\mathbb E[\varphi(w^*)(w^*-\sqrt\rho\,u_{k^*})_+]/2q$: it equals $D$ in the near-clone band, but at small $\rho$ sparse-rank binding contributes $0.25,\ 0.34,\ 0.40$ at $q=10^{-2},10^{-3},10^{-4}$ (exact-scale range only), and the signed sum cancels this.
- **Keeping only the up-boundary flux of $\Lambda_r'$** (the $(y,y)$ corner) over-counts. $\Lambda_r$ is a narrow bump in $w$, and the $(x,y)$ corners cancel its rising flank.

A proof therefore needs two separate pieces:
- (i) $D\le Cq$ (in fact $D\le0$) away from the clone band, roughly where $\sigma u_q\ge K$, from a sign argument covering sparse-rank binding;
- (ii) a fluctuation-controlled version of the joint-limit computation inside the band $\sigma u_q\le K$. There $(\sigma u_{k^*}-E_{(k^*)})$ concentrates at $\delta$, but uniform-in-$N$ tail control of the binding order statistic is missing.

Neither piece is done.

## Hierarchical, level-weighted test (conditional on the unproved estimate)

For balanced trees, $-f'(t)=\sum_dD_d$ with $D_d=c_d(1-c_d)\,\partial_{c_d}F$. This was computed by common random numbers and central differences, and checked against the path derivative ($0.1069q$ in both). Nested near-clone trees with $1-c_d=\varepsilon_0r^d$, branching $3$ and $8$ coordinates per leaf were scanned for depths $0$ to $5$.

- $\max\sum_d[D_d]_+/q$ was $0.104$–$0.113$ at $q=0.1$ and $0.106$–$0.124$ at $q=10^{-3}$, with no growth in depth. The largest values occur at depths $4$–$5$, are based on $1500$ samples, and have levels of mixed sign; they are within noise of $\varphi(1)/2$.
- Level contributions split one budget rather than each adding their own; a typical split is $0.07+0.03+0.01$.
- This supports the depth-free conjecture $\sum_d[D_d]_+\le Cq$ for nonnegative ultrametric models. It was tested only on structured scans (no optimizer, $N\le1944$), and no mechanism is proposed.
