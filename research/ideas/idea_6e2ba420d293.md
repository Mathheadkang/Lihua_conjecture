Fix q=1/4,p=1/2,sigma=1/2,H=R,v0=+1,v1=-1, so rho=-1/2. Let the alternative mean quantile be vartheta(c)=2-c/2, i.e. a uniform positive mean law on[3/2,2]; the true atom has mean0 and mass1/2. The barrier g(c)=S^{-1}(q[p+(1-p)c])-2+c/2 is strictly convex. Numerical scalar checks using Python statistics.NormalDist (short local diagnostic) give slope bounds .3933581350701365 < .5 < .6072278668030282, so the minimum is interior. Exact critical equation phi(u*)=1/4 gives u*=sqrt(-2log(sqrt(2pi)/4)), r*=S(u*)/q, c*=(r*-p)/(1-p). Values: u*=.9668048695731916,r*=.6672831793476285,c*=.334566358695257,b=-.8659119510791798. The signed conditional jump is k(b,r*)-k(b,p)≈-.0023128233970084455, with k(b,p)≈.20372920725096036,k(b,r*)≈.2014163838539519. Thus the proposed formula predicts derivative +.0006342124673175036 for delta=+1 (local alternative mean lowering raises the fold threshold) and the negative of that for delta=-1. Check both signs against the actual largest-crossing integral, with a valid smooth bump/K and all hypotheses/other target conclusions. Do not replace the signed jump by an absolute value or infer the expansion merely from these coefficient calculations. This object does not refute either main order conjecture.
