Quantifier clarification for [structured target S](idea_7727ae2d6679.md). Preserve its exact authorized fixed-s0 statement with C(s0),q0(s0); no asset statement is changed by this risk record. Removing the possible 1/s0 factor would need a constant uniformly bounded as s0 tends to zero. However the passage to the unrestricted BH bound through jitter at each fixed finite model and fixed q ALSO needs a common positive small-q interval, or some additional argument establishing applicability at that fixed q along a sequence s0 tending to zero. Uniformity of C(s0) alone does not logically imply this if q0(s0) can tend to zero.

An abstract monotone-envelope example illustrates only the quantifier issue, not a Gaussian BH counterexample: for 0<s<=1 let H_s(q)=min(1,q/s), and q0(s)=exp(-1/s^2). For q<=q0(s), H_s(q)=q/s<=q sqrt(log(1/q)), so the constant is uniformly 1. Yet sup_s H_s(q)=1 for every fixed q>0. Each H_s is increasing as s decreases. Thus fixed-s0 small-level statements, even with a common multiplicative constant, cannot be passed to the unrestricted supremum without controlling their intervals.

A successful finite-level S proof may naturally give a universal q0 (for example through a universal U>=1 cutoff); if so record it explicitly as strengthened information in the proof. Until then, the later uniform-noise removal task has two quantifier obligations: uniform multiplicative control and applicability on a common q interval. This does not refute S, T, either BH target, or the Gaussian jitter continuity statement, and it does not establish a reduction to T. It prevents an order-of-limits shortcut. Reuse the original structured and shell ideas rather than duplicating their proposed mechanisms.
