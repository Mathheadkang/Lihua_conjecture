Guard for the next main reassessment; not a counterexample to either target. Even if full FDR is W1 continuous and an Ekeland near-maximizer exists in a complete parameter-law space, the finite PD rare-clone formula must not simply be evaluated at a non-atomic completion point or transferred using value continuity alone. Example of the scale problem: the uniform mean law on[-A,0] and its n equal-weight midpoint atoms have exact W1 distance A/(4n). The approved rare-clone law at a chosen atom of weight1/n requires epsilon L_n<1 with L_n=n[h(U)/U-1], hence its small-epsilon regime is tied to the atom weight; using an iterated derivative at growing n requires a justified rate, and taking epsilon_n=o(1/n) makes the W1 approximation error much larger than epsilon_n. A continuity estimate at cost1/n, even uniform, does not guarantee an FDR error o(epsilon_n). At a non-atomic law, shifting an epsilon fraction by a displacement of order epsilon may change a smooth conditional score CDF only at order epsilon², whereas cooperative finite-atom splitting can have an order-epsilon BH effect. This is an anticipated mechanism, not a proved general failure theorem. A valid localization/near-maximal transfer must track atom weights, adjacent ranks, quantization error, covariance degeneracy and the exact order of epsilon versus refinement. The circle and diffuse-mean continuity checks do not supply those derivative estimates. Do not assume that the mere existence of a completed-space near-maximizer closes the main proof.
