## Scope and statement

Consider any finite original one-sided Gaussian model T~N_N(theta,Sigma), with Sigma PSD, unit diagonal, and original null labels I_0={i:theta_i<=0}. Singular Sigma and arbitrary negative null means are permitted. Assume Sigma_ij<=0 for distinct i,j in I_0. No restriction is imposed on covariances involving alternatives, and no independence of alternatives or of alternatives from nulls is assumed. Write P_i=barPhi(T_i), A=N-|I_0|, and use the original ordinary BH procedure at level q in (0,1), with its actual largest qualifying rank and original null labels.

For A>=1, let t_A=qA/N, z_A=barPhi^{-1}(t_A), and

$$
G=\bigcap_{j\notin I_0}\{P_j\le t_A\},\qquad
\epsilon=\sum_{j\notin I_0}\mathbb P(P_j>t_A)
=\sum_{j\notin I_0}\Phi(z_A-\theta_j).
$$

Then

$$
\operatorname{FDR}_{\mathrm{original}}
\le \operatorname{FDR}_{\mathrm{aux}}+\mathbb P(G^c)
\le \min\{1,4eq\}+\epsilon,
$$

and in particular FDR_original<=min{1,4eq+epsilon}. Here the auxiliary p-value vector keeps every original null p-value and replaces every original alternative p-value by zero, with the same original null labels and ordinary BH denominator. It is only a comparison object for the count-calibration lemma; it is not asserted to be a Gaussian model with finite alternative means.

If A=0, no saturation event or error is needed and the same argument gives FDR_original<=min{1,4eq} directly.

## Actual-rank preservation on G

On G, at least the A original alternatives satisfy the BH threshold t_A, so the original actual rank R is at least A. The auxiliary rank is also at least A because its A alternative p-values are zero. For every integer r>=A, every original alternative lies below t_A<=qr/N on G. Consequently the original and auxiliary counts below qr/N both equal

$$
A+C(qr/N),\qquad C(s)=\sum_{i\in I_0}\mathbf1_{\{P_i\le s\}}.
$$

Thus they have exactly the same qualifying ranks r>=A, and both have a qualifying rank at least A. Their largest qualifying ranks therefore coincide on G. The null values and original labels are unchanged, so their false-rejection counts and FDPs coincide there as well. Since both FDPs belong to [0,1], FDP_original<=FDP_aux+1_{G^c} pathwise, giving the expectation comparison.

## Calibration of the auxiliary procedure

Take the conditioning sigma-field to be trivial; it contains the complete auxiliary alternative vector because that vector is identically zero. Its null Gaussian law is the original null marginal law, with finite mean vector and the possibly singular PSD covariance Sigma_{I_0,I_0}. Its off-diagonal entries are nonpositive by hypothesis. For every s in (0,q], the original null means theta_i<=0 give

$$
\mathbb P(P_i\le s)=\overline\Phi(\overline\Phi^{-1}(s)-\theta_i)\le s,
\qquad
\frac{\sum_{i\in I_0}\mathbb P(P_i\le s)}{Ns}\le\frac{|I_0|}{N}\le1.
$$

Hence the approved Gaussian sufficient condition in gaussian_bh_conditional_subpoisson_calibration applies with K=1, and bounds FDR_aux by min{1,4eq}. No conditioning on the original alternatives occurs, so no preservation of covariance signs under further conditioning is required.

## Exact error bound and asymptotic screening

The union bound gives P(G^c)<=epsilon without any independence assumption. Because every alternative score has marginal law N(theta_j,1), its summand is exactly Phi(z_A-theta_j). All parameters, including A, N, means, covariance and singularity pattern, may vary with q. For any such sequence q down to zero with epsilon=o(q sqrt(log(1/q))),

$$
\frac{\operatorname{FDR}_{\mathrm{original}}}{q\sqrt{\log(1/q)}}
\le\frac{4e}{\sqrt{\log(1/q)}}+o(1)\longrightarrow0.
$$

## Limitations

This is a bound for a specified subclass requiring nonpositive original null covariance and a small saturation error. The term epsilon can be large for unsaturated alternatives. The argument does not control arbitrary null covariance signs, does not establish the unrestricted main theorem, and is not a proof-verification or approval verdict for that theorem.
