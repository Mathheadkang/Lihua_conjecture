This is a rigorously checked obstruction to a possible proof reduction for [Sharp uniform upper-bound target for two-sided Gaussian BH](../assets/gaussian_bh_two_sided_sharp_upper.md), not a counterexample to that target. In dimension two, two-sided BH FDR need not be a convex function of the covariance at a fixed mean vector; its maximum over the correlation interval need not occur at a rank-one covariance.

## Exact setup and all parameter ranges

Fix any \(q\in(0,1)\). Set \(a=q/2\), \(b=q\),
\[
c_a=\overline\Phi^{-1}(q/4),\qquad c_b=\overline\Phi^{-1}(q/2),\qquad
\mu=\tfrac12\min\{c_a-c_b,c_b\}.
\]
Because \(0<q/4<q/2<1/2\), we have \(c_a>c_b>0\) and
\[
0<\mu<c_b,\qquad \mu<c_a-c_b.
\]
Let \(N=2\), \(\theta=(0,\mu)\), and consider
\[
\Sigma_+=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\Sigma_-=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\frac{\Sigma_++\Sigma_-}{2}=I_2.
\]
Both endpoints are PSD with unit diagonal. The exact jointly Gaussian endpoint models are \(T=(Z,Z+\mu)\) and \(T=(Z,-Z+\mu)\), respectively, with \(Z\sim N(0,1)\). The midpoint model is \(T=(Z_1,Z_2+\mu)\) for independent standard normal \(Z_1,Z_2\). No covariance-matched non-Gaussian mixture is used. The only true null is coordinate 1 at all three covariances; the mean vector is identical across them for this fixed \(q\).

## Pointwise BH identity

Write \(A_t=\{p_1^{(2)}\le t\}\) and \(B_t=\{p_2^{(2)}\le t\}\). For arbitrary p-values in this two-test model, BH rejects both exactly on \(A_b\cap B_b\). It rejects the null alone exactly on \(A_a\cap B_b^c\). Hence the pointwise identity, including threshold equalities, is
\[
\operatorname{FDP}
=\mathbf1_{A_a\cap B_b^c}+\tfrac12\mathbf1_{A_b\cap B_b}.
\]
The equality cases of the thresholds have probability zero under all three Gaussian laws, since each coordinate has variance one.

## Rank-one endpoint calculation

For \(\Sigma_+\), \(A_a=\{|Z|\ge c_a\}\) and \(B_b=\{|Z+\mu|\ge c_b\}\). The reverse triangle inequality and \(c_a-\mu>c_b\) give \(A_a\subset B_b\), so the first term in the FDP identity vanishes. On \(A_b=\{|Z|\ge c_b\}\), the condition \(B_b\) holds automatically for \(Z\ge c_b\). For \(Z\le-c_b\), the inequality \(\mu<c_b\) ensures \(Z+\mu<0\), so \(B_b\) holds exactly when \(Z\le-c_b-\mu\). Therefore
\[
A_b\cap B_b=\{Z\ge c_b\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{Z\le-c_b-\mu\},
\]
and
\[
f(\Sigma_+):=\operatorname{FDR}_{\rm BH}^{(2)}(q;2,\theta,\Sigma_+)
=\tfrac12\{\overline\Phi(c_b)+\overline\Phi(c_b+\mu)\}
=\frac q4+\frac12\overline\Phi(c_b+\mu)<\frac q2.
\]
For \(\Sigma_-\), the substitution \(Z\mapsto-Z\) preserves \(|T_1|\) and transforms the second coordinate into the \(\Sigma_+\) form, so \(f(\Sigma_-)=f(\Sigma_+)\).

## Independent midpoint calculation

At \(I_2\), the null p-value is uniform and independent of the alternative p-value. Write \(G=\mathbb P(B_b)\). The pointwise identity yields
\[
f(I_2)=a(1-G)+\tfrac12 bG=a=\frac q2.
\]
Consequently,
\[
f\!\left(\frac{\Sigma_++\Sigma_-}{2}\right)
>\frac{f(\Sigma_+)+f(\Sigma_-)}2.
\]
This is a strict failure of covariance convexity for every \(q\in(0,1)\) at the chosen fixed \(\theta\). In dimension two the only rank-one PSD unit-diagonal covariances are \(\Sigma_\pm\); since \(I_2\) beats both, rank-one maximization at this fixed mean also fails.

## Scope and independent numerical check

At \(q=0.05\), \(c_a=2.241402728\), \(c_b=1.959963985\), and \(\mu=0.140719372\), giving \(f(\Sigma_\pm)=0.021417192832<0.025=f(I_2)\). A lightweight standard-library normal-tail calculation also checked the strict inequality at \(q=10^{-6},10^{-3},0.01,0.5,0.99\).

All three FDR values are \(O(q)\), and their ratios to \(q\sqrt{\log(1/q)}\) tend to zero. This example therefore supplies no violating sequence for the original uniform asymptotic upper bound, does not compare covariances after optimizing over all means, and does not exclude a different valid reduction to singular models. Use it only to reject a general covariance-convexity or fixed-mean rank-one-extremality argument.
