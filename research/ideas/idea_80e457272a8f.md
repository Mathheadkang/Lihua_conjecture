The round-2 orthogonal-dust failure suggests a proof step for clause 1, rather than a counterexample. Fix m <= n and draw m iid uniform indices from {1,...,n}, independently of a jointly exchangeable n-parameter array. Conditional on all indices being distinct, their ordered induced array has exactly the law of the first m coordinates by exchangeability. The probability of a repetition is at most m(m-1)/(2n). Consequently every measurable [0,1]-valued functional of the sampled marked covariance array differs in expectation from its distinct-index counterpart by at most that probability. Apply this to the finite conditional Gaussian FDR functional of the sampled parameter array. Combining this comparison with the separately established uniform submodel-compression estimate should yield a uniform Cauchy estimate: first choose m to control compression, then n to control collisions. The precise compression hypotheses and the sampling interpretation must still be checked in execution; the round-summary failure line supplies no independent proof of that theorem. This step alone establishes neither finite-FDR continuity nor the remaining moment compactness, normalized extrema, or marked representation claims.
