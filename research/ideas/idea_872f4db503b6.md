Primary source checked: Ghosh and Sarkar, Further Results on Controlling the False Discovery Rate in Two-Sided Gaussian Mean Testing, arXiv:2608.21267v2, https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21267v2, Section 3, Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.2. Its two-sided z-test upper bound is sum over true nulls of G_tau_i(G_tau_i^{-1}(q)/N), where G_tau(u) is the chi-square-one survival function evaluated at tau times its inverse survival argument. This is not q times a conditional-standard-deviation factor. It addresses fixed covariance, not the unrestricted one-sided target.

Own asymptotic diagnostic: fix q in (0,1) and tau in (0,1), and put c=G_tau^{-1}(q)>0. From the elementary chi-square-one tail asymptotic, G_tau(c/N) is of order N^{-tau}(log N)^{(tau-1)/2}, with positive constants depending on q,tau. Thus N G_tau(c/N) diverges like N^{1-tau} times a logarithmic factor. A lower bound tau_i>=epsilon<1 supplied by covariance jitter therefore does not by itself make this published bound dimension-uniform. This diagnoses failure of this proposed use of the bound, not an FDR lower bound or counterexample to either main conjecture. An additional aggregate comparison theorem would still be needed; none is claimed.
