## Scope and precise falsified gate
This is a draft observation about an auxiliary mean-shift bridge, not a proof or counterexample to the unrestricted main theorem. Fix any q in (0,1/e). There is no dimension-uniform modulus omega_q(h) tending to zero as h decreases to zero such that the absolute difference of expected actual BH FDPs is at most omega_q(h) for every pair of lawful Gaussian models differing by a uniform mean shift of size h, even when all labels remain true-null in both models and covariance is the identity. Nor, for fixed h>0, can one bound the zero-mean FDR by any finite factor C(q,h) times the negative-mean FDR uniformly in dimension. Additive errors of order q are NOT excluded by this observation.

## Correct rank-score structural facts
For unit Gram vectors v_i and T_i(g)=theta_i+v_i dot g, descending ordered scores T_[r](g) are 1-Lipschitz: coordinatewise changes are bounded by the Euclidean displacement, and sorting preserves the sup-norm bound. Therefore H_(q,k)(g)=max over r>=k of [T_[r](g)-a(qr/N)], where a(t)=barPhi^{-1}(t), is 1-Lipschitz and {R_q>=k}={H_(q,k)>=0}. Uniformly adding h to all means adds exactly h to H. These statements retain the actual largest qualifying rank, singular Gram representations and negative null means. They do not compare the inverse-rank null-marked FDP.

## Lawful independent-Gaussian counterexample and full calculation
Let G_1,...,G_N be independent standard normals. Compare theta_i=0 to theta_i=-h_N, using the same fixed true-null set I_0={1,...,N} in both models. Take h_N>0 with h_N tending to zero and h_N sqrt(log N) tending to infinity, for example h_N=(log(N+1))^{-1/4}. Both models satisfy every Gaussian-model and null-label requirement. Since all tests are null, FDP=1_{R_q>0}.

At theta=0, FDR=q exactly for each finite N. Here is an inline proof, without invoking an unavailable library theorem. Set p_i to zero and let R^{i,0} be the resulting largest BH rank; it is at least one and depends only on the other p-values. Deterministically, {i rejected, R_q=r}={p_i<=qr/N, R^{i,0}=r}: for ranks at least r, replacing an already subthreshold p_i by zero leaves every qualifying-count comparison unchanged; the converse uses the same argument. Conditional independence and uniformity give E[1_{i rejected}/(R_q vee 1)]=sum_r P(R^{i,0}=r)(qr/N)/r=q/N. Summing i yields q.

At theta=-h_N, the independent p-value CDF is F_N(t)=barPhi(a(t)+h_N). Directly changing variables in the Gaussian integral gives

F_N(t)/t <= exp[-h_N a(t)-h_N^2/2].

Indeed barPhi(a+h)=integral_a^infinity exp(-h z-h^2/2) phi(z) dz. The exact event {R_q>0} is the union over all actual qualifying ranks r=1,...,N of {at least r p-values are <=qr/N}. A union over the r-element subsets and independence give

P_{-h_N}(R_q>0) <= sum_{r=1}^N binom(N,r) F_N(qr/N)^r
 <= sum_{r=1}^N {e q exp[-h_N a(qr/N)-h_N^2/2]}^r.

Every summand is at most (e q)^r, since qr/N<=q<1/e<1/2. For each fixed r, a(qr/N)/sqrt(2 log N) tends to one, so its summand tends to zero. Extend the summand by zero when r>N and apply dominated convergence to the summable geometric sequence (e q)^r. Thus the negative-mean FDR tends to zero, whereas the zero-mean FDR is q. The proof uses neither concentration assumptions nor a rank cutoff error. The same argument works with any fixed h_N=h>0, proving the failure of every finite multiplicative downward-shift comparison uniform in N.

## Stronger warning for a mathematical intermediate with positive shifted means
If instead one starts with lawful theta=0 and artificially keeps all original null labels after adding any fixed h>0, the resulting labeled expected FDP tends to one. This is NOT a lawful all-null model because its means are positive. It only diagnoses a proposed mathematical comparison that uses fixed original labels after an upward shift. The rank-one event alone gives P(R_q>0)>=1-[1-barPhi(a(q/N)-h)]^N. The tail ratio is at least exp[h a(q/N)-h^2/2], so N barPhi(a(q/N)-h) tends to infinity and the displayed probability tends to one. No count concentration is used.
