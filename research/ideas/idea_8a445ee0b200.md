Suggested by the teaching chat during the researcher's requested cross-field brainstorm. This is a proposed stress test and repair task for the step AFTER FDR continuity. It is not a counterexample to gaussian_bh_common_loading_wasserstein_fdr_continuity, not a refutation of the approved fixed-model clone variation, and not a counterexample to the main conjecture.

## Specific issue
Even if F_{pi_n}->F_pi in W1 is proved, this does not transfer finite-model rare-clone derivatives to a nonatomic Ekeland point. The approved derivative takes epsilon->0 at each fixed finite positive-definite model. Its useful perturbations have W1 cost O(epsilon^2), but the expansion radius can shrink with the separation between competing BH crossings.

## A one-dimensional calibration
Use one common residual direction, all true tags, and theta uniform on [-a,0]. At any fixed sigma in (0,1), the scores are theta+Y with Y standard normal. Put u(r)=barPhi^{-1}(qr), U=u(1), and m(U)=q/phi(U). Direct largest-crossing analysis gives
$$
F_\pi=\bar\Phi(b_a),\qquad
b_a=\min_{0<r\le1}\{ar+u(r)\}.
$$
Choose a>m(U). The minimized function has a unique interior minimum, since its first derivative is a-q/phi(u(r)) and its second derivative is q^2 u(r)/phi(u(r))^2>0 for q<1/2.

For the equal-weight mean grid theta_j=-aj/N,
$$
b_N=\min_{1\le j\le N}\{aj/N+u(j/N)\}.
$$
Near the interior minimum, neighboring competing grid crossings differ by O(N^{-2}); avoid exact ties by a suitable subsequence or a small asymmetric mesh. Thus a perturbation which moves a winning finite-atom crossing linearly can be absorbed by a neighboring crossing on a scale much smaller than the atom's mass 1/N.

These collinear finite grids have singular covariance. Their crossing thresholds may be analyzed directly, but the approved POSITIVE-DEFINITE rare-clone theorem cannot be quoted on them. To test that theorem's transfer, add a small independent Gaussian component gamma>0 while preserving unit marginal variances, then explicitly track the N, epsilon and gamma limit order.

## Decisive comparison, not a preasserted result
Compare:
(a) epsilon->0 at fixed N and fixed gamma>0, followed by atomization/jitter limits;
(b) atomization first, then an explicitly defined rare perturbation of the nonatomic law;
(c) joint sequences with epsilon N->0 but epsilon above versus below the competing-root gap O(N^{-2}).
The finite rare-law feasibility condition and actual largest crossing must hold on every sequence. Determine whether the normalized FDR increment retains the finite Mills coefficient or vanishes. A smoothly spread population may change only at the epsilon^2 scale under O(epsilon) mass displaced O(epsilon); this is a hypothesis to check, not a conclusion imported from ordinary continuity.

A warning for localization: a parameter cell of width eta may have mass w=O(eta^d). A method requiring epsilon<<w to form the rare split, but also eta<<epsilon to replace the cell by an atom, has no automatic compatible scale. Prove an appropriate replacement estimate instead of choosing a diagonal sequence by assertion.

## Constructive repair target
Seek a one-sided deformation-recovery statement for the actual completed population space, rather than equality of all finite derivatives. For an explicitly defined admissible deformation T_epsilon and a specified signed Mills quantity D(pi), the useful form is
$$
W_1(T_\varepsilon\pi,\pi)=o(\varepsilon),\qquad
\liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\frac{F(T_\varepsilon\pi)-F(\pi)}{\varepsilon}\ge D(\pi).
$$
At a valid Ekeland near-maximizer, F(nu)<=F(pi)+eta W1(nu,pi), this would imply D(pi)<=0. Both the deformation and the limiting signed quantity must be constructed, including diffuse components and zero-mean null boundaries. If the finite-atom coefficient disappears in the toy model, use a relaxed directional quantity or a different scale; do not force the old formula through the limit. The current continuity target already disclaims this bridge, so this idea supplies a next test rather than alleging a defect in that statement.
