The second search round's finite strong-signal, four non-antipodal mark candidate fails to refute the identity while retaining the random actual largest BH rank and rare lower-rank events. This exposes a proof-step obstruction: making a finite signal deterministically rejected, or freezing its rank before differentiation, discards contributions that are small but not identically zero. Keep all original rank strata and their signed FDP face jumps throughout the calculation.

A possible route for the remaining general first-order obligation is to truncate the finite Gaussian vector at radius R_epsilon = sqrt(C log(1/epsilon)) with C chosen so the discarded Gaussian probability is o(epsilon). On this truncated event, fixed marks are O(R_epsilon), the normalized-score correction is O(epsilon^2 R_epsilon), and every crossing lies in a tube of width O(epsilon R_epsilon) around an original coordinate threshold face. Positive definiteness gives bounded two-coordinate Gaussian densities for the fixed model, suggesting O(epsilon^2 R_epsilon^2) = o(epsilon) control for intersections of two distinct-coordinate tubes. Away from these intersections, retain the actual largest-rank coordinate-face jump and integrate its signed contribution before removing truncation.

This is a proposed proof route, not a completed proof or a new counterexample. It still needs exact tube inclusions, treatment of all finite threshold indices and deterministic ties, a justified domination/removal of truncation for g(Y), and a rigorous estimate that the normalization correction changes the expectation by o(epsilon). Constants may depend on the fixed positive-definite model and grid. No singular-original extension or uniform FDR bound is asserted.
