Concrete open test, not a checked counterexample. Take original N=2, Sigma=I, theta=(0,1), q=1/4, chosen i=1 (a zero-mean true null), unit directions e1,e2. Apply the target's rare downward mean quantiles only to i; the alternative remains an exact K-copy block independent of the null score. All true-null labels remain lawful, and epsilon L<1 suffices for small epsilon; the original FDR is exactly q/2 by independent uniform-null BH calibration. Put U=u2=S^{-1}(q), u1=S^{-1}(q/2). The signed chosen-null face masses are mu1=phi(u1) Phi(U-1) and mu2=(1/2)phi(U)S(U-1). Thus the proposed derivative is -[1/U-m(u1)]mu1-[1/U-m(U)]mu2. This calculation checks the proposed face coefficient and model hypotheses, not the actual iterated derivative. Still to establish: actual finite-copy to common-population-root limit, the two background regimes with or without the exact alternative block, fractional selected-null FDP and clipping at the alternative's atom, and the target-scale expansion including both ranks. A heuristic height O(epsilon) times width O(epsilon) for fractional FDP is only a proof obligation; do not turn it into a failed-NOT candidate until fully checked. This genuinely adds a mixed-label/fractional-FDP mechanism beyond the scalar zero-null endpoint already checked in round1.
