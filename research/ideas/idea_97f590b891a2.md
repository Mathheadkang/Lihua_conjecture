Scope: a new bounded diagnostic for the open level-smoothing approach idea_10275f4814c1, not a proof of a universal comparison, not an asset, and not a new completion root. The calculation is agent-produced and has not received independent verification. It varies the rare-alternative profile in the concrete small-null-loading population family reconstructed in plan_one_sided_order_fdp_entropy_mills_coherence_20261107, Progress P4a. No fixed-q uniform finite sampling or bootstrap approximation is used.

Fix q in (0,S(1)] and y=sqrt(2 log(1/q)). First fix a continuous bounded function g_q on [0,y] with g_q(0)>0, g_q(a)>0 for 0<a<y and g_q(y)=0. Require that W_r(a)=g_q(a)S(a/r)/q is decreasing on [b_r,y] for all sufficiently small r, where b_r=r S^{-1}(r^2). This explicit monotonicity requirement makes W_r a valid finite-mass survival profile. The function g_q may depend arbitrarily on the fixed q but is fixed before r decreases to zero. Put tau_r=W_r(b_r), pi_r=1-r-tau_r, choose c_r by S(c_r)=q(r+tau_r)/(1-q pi_r), and use the source three-component type law: null mass pi_r at (mean,loading)=(0,r), primary mass r at (c_r+b_r,-1), and secondary mass tau_r with mean a/r+a and loading -1, whose a survival is W_r(a)/tau_r. These are original positive-mean alternatives and zero-mean nulls; every deterministic finite type realization has unit marginal variances and PSD Gaussian covariance.

Conditional on the common Z=z, define q-normalized null and secondary curves A0,q and A2,q at score cutoff c. On any compact K contained in (0,y), for c=z/r+h and bounded h, direct hazard integration gives uniformly
$$
A_{0,q}\longrightarrow d(z):=q e^{z^2/2},\qquad
A_{2,q}\longrightarrow g_q(z)e^{zh}.
$$
Indeed beta=r(c+z)/(1+r)=z+rh/(1+r), and A2,q=g_q(beta)S(beta/r)/S(c). The primary contribution vanishes on K. At testing level t=lambda q with fixed 1<=lambda<=2 and lambda d(z)<1, the crossing equation is
$$
\lambda[d(z)+g_q(z)e^{zh}]=1,
\qquad h={1\over z}\log{1/\lambda-d(z)\over g_q(z)}.
$$
Thus the crossing remains c=z/r+O(1), while its conditional FDP tends to lambda d(z).

The earlier-crossing exclusion is not omitted: along a hypothetical earlier sequence with rc tending to x<z, bounded g_q gives A2,q=O(exp[-c(z-rc)/(1+r)]) tending to zero if c tends to infinity, while bounded c gives vanishing alternative mass. The null curve has limit q exp(xz-x^2/2)<=d(z)<1/lambda. If rc tends to z but h=c-z/r tends to minus infinity, the same secondary tail ratio tends to zero, leaving lambda d(z)<1. Bounded negative h is excluded by the displayed limiting crossing equation. Therefore compact strict brackets around the displayed root exclude all earlier crossings exactly as in the source calculation.

If lambda d(z)>1, the first crossing instead occurs at rc tending to x=z-sqrt(z^2-2 log(1/(lambda q)))<z; the alternative terms vanish there and the conditional FDP tends to 1. For compact z<0 the source primary bracket and rc_r tending to zero give limiting FDP lambda q. The exceptional boundaries have zero Gaussian measure. Consequently this entire class of modified profiles has limiting conditional FDP
$$
D_lambda(z)=\begin{cases}\lambda q,&z\le0,\\ \min\{1,\lambda q e^{z^2/2}\},&z>0,\end{cases}
$$
which is nondecreasing in lambda. Bounded convergence therefore gives nondecreasing limiting population FDR; these fixed-q, then r-to-zero multiplicative profile modifications do not produce an order-q sqrt(log(1/q)) cliff between q and 2q.

Exact limits of this diagnostic: no uniform error bound in q, r, g_q, dimension or empirical approximation has been proved; no finite Gaussian cliff is ruled out. A potential next test must leave this bounded fixed-profile regime, for example by profiles with exponential r-dependence, near-tangential competing first crossings, or a simultaneous q,r degeneration. These are proposed tests, not constructed counterexamples. The universal expected rank-inflation deficit E[FDP_q 1{J_q<1/2}]=O(q) remains unpaid; neither failure of this restricted test nor the known finite-sampling obstruction resolves that gate or exhausts the main target.
