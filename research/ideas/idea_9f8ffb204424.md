## Statement

Let $Z=(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)$ be a centered Gaussian vector with unit variances and arbitrary correlation, $p=(p_1,\dots,p_n)$ a probability vector, and
$$
L_w=\sum_{i=1}^n p_i\exp\Big(wZ_i-\frac{w^2}{2}\Big),\qquad \Lambda=\sup_{w\ge0}L_w .
$$
Conjecture (T). There is a universal constant $C$, independent of $n$, $p$ and the correlation matrix, such that for all $c\ge2$ and $\alpha\ge2$
$$
\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}\le\sqrt{\frac{\log c}{\pi}}+C,\qquad
\mathbb P\{\Lambda\ge\alpha\}\le\frac{C}{\alpha\sqrt{\log\alpha}} .
$$
The order form ($C\sqrt{\log c}$ in the first display) is the main claim; the leading constant $1/\sqrt\pi$ is the sharp form. Latent form: $Z_i=\langle x_i,G\rangle$ with unit vectors $x_i$ and $G$ standard Gaussian; more generally $L_w(g)=\int\exp(w\langle x,g\rangle-w^2\|x\|^2/2)\,\mu(dx)$ for a probability measure $\mu$ on the unit ball of a separable Hilbert space. Then $L_w$ is the likelihood ratio of $\mathrm{Law}(wX+G)$, $X\sim\mu$, against the Gaussian law: a Gaussian channel with input law $\mu$ at signal-to-noise parameter $w$, evaluated on pure noise, with the SNR swept while the noise is frozen.

One direction (all $Z_i$ equal to one $Z$) gives $\Lambda=\exp(Z_+^2/2)$, so exactly $\mathbb P\{\Lambda\ge\alpha\}=\overline\Phi(\sqrt{2\log\alpha})\sim(\alpha\sqrt{4\pi\log\alpha})^{-1}$ and $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}=\tfrac12+\sqrt{\log c/\pi}+c\,\overline\Phi(\sqrt{2\log c})$. With $c=1/q$ this is Lei's one-sided constant. The sharp form says that one direction is extremal up to an additive constant.

## Why this is the BH object

(a) Weak loadings. For a zero-mean null $T=\langle a,G\rangle+s\varepsilon$ with $\|a\|=r$, $s^2=1-r^2$, the conditional tail ratio $\overline\Phi((x-\langle a,g\rangle)/s)/\overline\Phi(x)$ tends to $\exp(w\langle a/r,g\rangle-w^2/2)$ when $r\to0$, $x\to\infty$, $rx\to w$: Lei's extremal regime. With $\mu$ the empirical law of the null loading directions (unequal small norms are absorbed by putting $\mu$ on the ball), the null tail-ratio field at level $x$ is $L_{rx}(g)$: the BH level is the SNR. In the clone (population) limit the conditional FDP is at most $q\pi_0L_{rx^*(G)}(G)$, so $\mathrm{FDR}\le q\pi_0\,\mathbb E\min\{1/(q\pi_0),\Lambda\}$. The number of null directions, the factor dimension and any hierarchy among directions sit inside $\mu$, and (T) is uniform in $\mu$.

(b) Every finite model (verified algebra). In latent form $T_i=\theta_i+\langle v_i,G\rangle$, $\|v_i\|=1$, on $\{R\ge1\}$ with $x^*=u_R$ one has $\mathrm{FDP}=qV(x^*)/(N\overline\Phi(x^*))$, and $\mathbf 1\{t\ge x\}\le e^{x(t-x)}$ with $\theta_i\le0$ gives
$$
\mathrm{FDP}\le q\,\pi_0\,\sqrt{2\pi}\,h(x^*)\,L^0_{x^*}(G),\qquad
L^0_w(g)=\frac1{N_0}\sum_{i\in I_0}\exp\Big(w\langle v_i,g\rangle-\frac{w^2}{2}\Big),\quad h=\phi/\overline\Phi .
$$
With $H=\sqrt{2\pi}\,h(\overline\Phi^{-1}(q/N))\le\sqrt{2\pi}\,(1+\sqrt{2\log(N/q)})$ this is $\mathrm{FDR}\le q\pi_0H\,\mathbb E\min\{(q\pi_0H)^{-1},\Lambda^0\}$, where $\Lambda^0$ is $\Lambda$ for the null directions with equal weights.

## What (T) would give

1. Weak-loading population class: the order bound, and the sharp one-sided constant under the sharp form, uniformly in the number and geometry of null directions. This goes beyond the approved directional-entropy theorem (entropy $o(\sqrt{\log(1/q)})$) and the slow-dimension theorem.
2. All-null in that class: Simes rejects iff $\Lambda\ge1/q$ in the population limit, so the rejection probability is at most $Cq/\sqrt{\log(1/q)}$.
3. EVERY finite Gaussian model, by (b): $\mathrm{FDR}\le q\pi_0H(\sqrt{\log(1/q)/\pi}+C)\approx2q\pi_0\sqrt{\log(N/q)\log(1/q)}$, the geometric mean of the harmonic rate and the target. By the same route the verified flat-mixture lemma below gives only $q\pi_0H(1+u_1/\sqrt{2\pi})\approx2q\pi_0\log(N/q)$. I know of no universal Gaussian bound that is sub-harmonic in $N$; novelty not checked.

## What it would not give

The unrestricted target. Step (b) loses the hazard factor $h(x^*)\ge U$, the Mills ratio between an indicator and an exponential kernel. Conditional smoothing removes it only when the nulls have idiosyncratic noise bounded below (structured-class idea). For nearly singular nulls in many nearly independent directions every null-side free-level relaxation loses (idea_d353cd54790b, idea_e3cb787bf90f, idea_58004013c128), and the Gaussianity of the non-nulls must enter. The sign-cube free energy of idea_4cbdb4b7e0d9 is $\log\Lambda$ for the sign-cube prior; (T) is the general statement behind that test case.

## Structure (verified)

1. Ornstein-Uhlenbeck orbit: $P_tL_w=L_{e^{-t}w}$, so $\sup_{w\le W}L_w=\sup_{t\ge0}P_tL_W$ is the OU maximal function of a log-convex density; $\Lambda$ is log-convex in $g$ and $\{\Lambda<\alpha\}$ is convex.
2. Fixed $w$: the tail $C/(\alpha\sqrt{\log\alpha})$ is the Gaussian $L^1$-regularization theorem (Talagrand's convolution conjecture in Gauss space), since $L_w=P_1L_{ew}$. (T) asks for the same tail for the maximal function along the orbit. Under the martingale coupling ($w^2=t$, $G$ replaced by $B_t/\sqrt t$) Doob's inequality gives the tail $1/\alpha$ with equality, hence $1+\log c$: the gain is specific to the frozen ray.
3. Flat-mixture lemma: $\mathbb E\sup_{a\le w\le b}L_w\le1+(b-a)\int\|x\|\,\mu(dx)/\sqrt{2\pi}$ (positive variation in $w$, then Cameron-Martin). It reproduces Lei's constant whenever the relevant SNR range is $[0,U]$ and explains why Lebesgue measure appears in the one-factor proof.
4. Sub-critical lemma: $\mathbb P\{L_w\ge\alpha\}\le\alpha^{-1}\exp\{-\tfrac12(\log\alpha/w-w/2)_+^2\}$.
5. Integer grid: $\{\Lambda>\alpha\}\subseteq\bigcup_{j\ge0}\{L_j\ge e^{-1/8}\alpha\}$, from $\partial_w^2\log L_w\ge-1$.

Taking the regularization theorem as given, items 2, 4, 5 yield the tail $C\sqrt{\log\log\alpha}/(\alpha\sqrt{\log\alpha})$ for the part of $\Lambda$ coming from $w\le\sqrt{2\log\alpha}+O(1)$. The open part is super-critical SNR (late onset, as for many independent directions), where the mechanism is that each planted configuration spends SNR-time $O(\sqrt{\log c})$ between detection ($L\approx1$) and the cap ($L=c$).

## Evidence

Exact or heuristic computations: one direction (above); $K$ orthonormal equal atoms: tail $\sim(\alpha\sqrt{4\pi\log(K\alpha)})^{-1}$ (the leader; the bulk self-averages); uniform sphere in dimension $d$: $\Lambda\approx\exp(\xi_+^2/2)$ with $\xi$ the normalized fluctuation of $\|G\|^2$ (the wide-empirical-null mode), the one-direction tail again; two-level trees: time in the zone at most $\sqrt{2\log c/(1-\delta^2)}$.

Monte Carlo (bounded-weight importance sampling, short local runs): for independent directions ($K=30,1000$), clusters with coherent weight $0.3$ and $0.05$, three- and four-level trees ($K$ between $1300$ and $2700$), random points on spheres ($d=3,12$), mixed norms and a sparse prior, $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}$ at $c=10^2,10^4,10^6$ is at or below the one-direction values $1.83,2.30,2.67$ within Monte Carlo error (largest excess $0.04\pm0.035$), and $\alpha\sqrt{4\pi\log\alpha}\,\mathbb P\{\Lambda\ge\alpha\}$ lies in $[0.57,0.93]$ for $\alpha=10,\dots,10^4$ against $0.86$ to $0.95$ for one direction.

## Falsification

A sequence of priors with $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}/\sqrt{\log c}\to\infty$ refutes (T) and is a lead for a BH counterfamily in the weak-loading class (the non-nulls would still have to steer the level to the maximizing SNR). A prior exceeding $\sqrt{\log c/\pi}$ by a diverging amount refutes only the sharp form.
