Suggested by this chat in response to the researcher's request to try proving [the Schur-square variation approach](idea_6b48b2fe25e1.md). These are complete side-chat calculations for restricted classes, saved as a draft observation; no native proof verification or approval is claimed. The unrestricted dimension-uniform bound remains unproved.

## Setup

Let $Q=\bar\Phi$ be the standard Gaussian survival function, and let $F_q(R)$ denote the one-sided Gaussian Simes rejection probability at level $q$ and correlation matrix $R$. Write
$$
R_t=(1-t)\Sigma+t(\Sigma\circ\Sigma),\qquad f(t)=F_q(R_t).
$$
Downward variation means the negative variation of this scalar function on $[0,1]$, denoted $V_-(f)$. For absolutely continuous $f$ it is $\int_0^1[-f'(t)]_+dt$. The variation-measure definition also applies to continuous singular limits.

## Two groups of exact clones: V_- at most 2q

Let $N$ coordinates consist of two nonempty groups of copies of two unit-variance centered Gaussian variables with correlation $r_0$. Let group masses be $w=m/N$ and $1-w$. The path keeps both groups exactly tied, and their cross-correlation is $r(t)=r_0+t(r_0^2-r_0)$, a monotone function with range contained in $[-1,1]$.

For correlation $r$, let $C_r(a,b)$ be the joint distribution function of their one-sided uniform marginal p-values. Exact largest-rank counting yields
$$
f(r)=q+C_r(q,q)-C_r(qw,q)-C_r(q,q(1-w)).
$$
Indeed the rejection event is the union of the first group alone qualifying at $qw$, the second alone qualifying at $q(1-w)$, and both qualifying at $q$. Inclusion-exclusion cancels the intersection of the first two events.

For each fixed $a,b$, $C_r(a,b)$ is nondecreasing in $r$ by the bivariate Gaussian density derivative formula. For $q<1/2$, the three copula terms are zero at $r=-1$, and equal $q$, $qw$, $q(1-w)$ at $r=1$. Their total variations sum to $2q$. Hence the total variation of $f(r)$ over all $r\in[-1,1]$ is at most $2q$, and its downward variation on any monotone subpath is at most $2q$. This is uniform in $N$, masses and starting correlation.

For the signed rank-one starting model $r_0=-1$, the path traverses the full interval and $f(-1)=f(1)=q$. Upward and downward variations are equal, so the stronger bound $V_-\le q$ holds. For a single clone group the path is constant and $V_-=0$.

## Every trivariate Gaussian model: V_- at most 16q

Assume initially $\Sigma$ is positive definite and $0<q<1/2$. Put
$$
p_1=q/3,\quad p_2=2q/3,\quad p_3=q,\qquad z_\ell=Q^{-1}(p_\ell).
$$
For each pair $i,j$ let $k$ be the other coordinate and freeze its p-value $v$. If $v\le p_1$, rejection is already forced and the mixed derivative is zero. Otherwise the three-face identity has these three cases:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{range of }v & x& y&\text{range of }Z_k\\
(p_1,p_2] & z_2&z_3 &[z_2,z_1)\\
(p_2,p_3] & z_1&z_3 &[z_3,z_2)\\
(p_3,1) & z_1&z_2 &(-\infty,z_3)
\end{array}
$$
Within each case the mixed derivative is $\delta_y\otimes\delta_y-\delta_x\otimes\delta_y-\delta_y\otimes\delta_x$. After averaging the remaining coordinate, each face equals the marginal bivariate density at its corner times the conditional probability of the displayed interval. Bounding that conditional probability by one gives
$$
|J_{ij,t}|\le
2\phi_{r_{ij}(t)}(z_3,z_3)
+\phi_{r_{ij}(t)}(z_2,z_2)
+2\phi_{r_{ij}(t)}(z_2,z_3)
+2\phi_{r_{ij}(t)}(z_1,z_3)
+2\phi_{r_{ij}(t)}(z_1,z_2).
$$
Here $\phi_r$ denotes the centered bivariate unit-variance Gaussian density with correlation $r$.

For fixed $x,y$, monotonicity of $r_{ij}(t)$ and the bivariate Gaussian derivative formula give
$$
\int_0^1 |\dot r_{ij}(t)|\,\phi_{r_{ij}(t)}(x,y)\,dt
\le \int_{-1}^{1}\phi_r(x,y)\,dr
\le\min(Q(x),Q(y)).
$$
The last integral is the difference of the joint upper-tail probabilities at correlations $1$ and $-1$; the bound by the smaller marginal does not require evaluating the endpoints. Therefore the integrated absolute contribution for one pair is at most
$$
2p_3+p_2+2p_2+2p_1+2p_1
=\frac{16q}{3}.
$$
Price's formula is $f'(t)=\sum_{i<j}\dot r_{ij}(t)J_{ij,t}$. There are three pairs, so
$$
V_-(f)\le \int_0^1 |f'(t)|dt\le16q.
$$

For a singular starting correlation matrix, use $\Sigma^\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)\Sigma+\varepsilon I$ and its Schur-square path. The estimates are uniform in $\varepsilon$. For each fixed dimension the resulting probabilities converge to the singular-path probabilities, since Simes-event boundary is contained in finitely many deterministic marginal threshold hyperplanes, all of Gaussian probability zero. Negative variation is lower semicontinuous under pointwise convergence (test every finite partition), so the same bound holds in the variation-measure sense. Absolute continuity at the singular limit need not be assumed for this conclusion.

## Numerical exploration, not a certificate

A temporary script evaluated the exact trivariate face derivative and integrated its negative part for 89 correlation matrices: 80 random rank-two/rank-three frames with small independent regularization, and nine equicorrelation models. At $q=0.05,0.01,0.001$, the largest estimated downward variations were respectively $0.007796$, $0.001583$, and $0.0001584$, each below $0.159q$. The maximizer in this sample had off-diagonal entries approximately $(-0.995642,0.984544,-0.996461)$. These quadratures are exploratory, not interval-certified or exhaustive. They do not establish a universal bound.

## Exact unresolved step

The trivariate argument bounds the conditional interval indicators by one. In general dimension, frozen remaining coordinates produce many random leave-two-out ranks and hence many corner thresholds. Applying that same bound term by term introduces dimension-dependent counts. No uniform bound on the signed aggregate $\sum_{i<j}(\rho_{ij}^2-\rho_{ij})J_{ij,t}$, or its integrated negative variation, was obtained. The small factors in the exact covariance contraction must be retained before summing. The partial results neither prove nor refute the unrestricted Simes conjecture.
