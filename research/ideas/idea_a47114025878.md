This is a fresh read-only reconstruction of the deterministic identity in idea_aaa81d69e1fc plus an explicit obstruction to its proposed uniform ONE-SIDED harmful-jump lemma. It is not a refutation of either Gaussian BH FDR target, and it does not establish a two-sided obstruction.

## Checked deterministic identity and its scope

Let a=q/m and let r:(0,1)->{1,...,m} be a step function with finitely many changes, where point values at changes may be ignored. Put H(u)=min{u/r(u),a}. On every constant-r interval, H'(u)=1{u<ar(u)}/r(u) almost everywhere. Since H(0+)=0 and H(1-)=a for q<1, summing interval integrals gives exactly

$$
\int_0^1 \frac{\mathbf1\{u\leq ar(u)\}}{r(u)}\,du
=a-\sum_t\bigl[H(t+)-H(t-)\bigr].
$$

This remains true with singular Gaussian covariance: for each null coordinate i, Gaussian regression G_{-i}=rho_i G_i+W_i has W_i independent of G_i even when the residual covariance is singular. For each fixed residual, one-sided affine Gaussian paths cross a fixed BH grid threshold at most once per coordinate; two-sided absolute-affine paths cross at most twice. A constant coordinate tied to a grid threshold creates no infinite collection of path changes. Ordinary BH uses weak threshold inequalities; isolated equality points or tangencies do not affect this integral. The endpoint limits follow from r being bounded between 1 and m and from q<1.

For a zero-mean one-sided null, BH's forced-zero leave-one-out identity makes its FDR contribution exactly this integral averaged over W_i. For theta_i<=0, the actual null p-value barPhi(theta_i+G_i) is at least U=barPhi(G_i), while every other statistic retains its actual mean in r_i. This gives only an upper bound by the U-integral; it does not use or imply global FDP monotonicity. For a two-sided zero-mean null, conditioning on its independent equiprobable sign gives the average of the two branch identities with G_i=+/-barPhi^{-1}(u/2).

## Explicit admissible Gaussian family

Fix q in (0,1/2), an integer K>=2, m=2K, a=q/(2K), and z_v=barPhi^{-1}(v). Let Z be standard normal. Use K true nulls and K alternatives:

$$
T_i=Z-z_a\quad(1\leq i\leq K),\qquad
T_{K+j}=\theta_j-Z,\quad \theta_j=z_{aj}+z_{a(j+1)}\quad(1\leq j\leq K).
$$

Every marginal variance is one; the covariance is ss^T with s=(1,...,1,-1,...,-1), hence PSD, unit diagonal and singular. The true-null means -z_a are negative. Each alternative mean theta_j is positive because a(K+1)<q<1/2 for K>=2.

For any true null i, its residual W_i is identically zero. Parameterize G_i=Z=z_u. For u>=a, every other true-null statistic z_u-z_a is nonpositive, hence its p-value is at least 1/2>q and cannot contribute to the forced-zero BH rank. The alternative means theta_j strictly decrease with j, so their p-values are ordered by j. Moreover

$$
p_{K+j}(u)\leq a(j+1)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\theta_j-z_u\geq z_{a(j+1)}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u\geq aj.
$$

Consequently, on [a,aK] the exact forced-zero ordinary BH count is

$$
r_i(u)=1+\max\bigl(\{j\in\{1,\ldots,K\}:u\geq aj\}\cup\{0\}\bigr).
$$

At each u=aj, r_i jumps from j to j+1. The clipped reciprocal-rank function drops from a to aj/(j+1), so this single harmful jump has size a/(j+1). Additional changes outside this interval can only increase the nonnegative harmful-jump functional D. Therefore

$$
D(r_i)\geq a\sum_{j=1}^{K}\frac1{j+1}=a(H_{K+1}-1),\qquad
\sum_{i\in I_0}\mathbb E D(r_i)\geq\frac q2(H_{K+1}-1).
$$

Here H_l denotes the harmonic number. Choosing K=ceil(exp(1/q)) makes this last lower bound tend to 1/2 as q decreases to zero; its ratio to q sqrt(log(1/q)) therefore diverges. This analytically refutes the uniform one-sided positive-jump estimate proposed in idea_aaa81d69e1fc, under the full target's allowed composite nulls and q-dependent dimensions.

## Actual FDR remains small; precise obstruction

Any false rejection requires the common true-null p-value barPhi(Z-z_a) to be at most q. Hence

$$
\operatorname{FDR}\leq\mathbb P\{Z\geq z_a+z_q\}
=\bar\Phi(z_a+z_q)\leq\bar\Phi(z_a)=a,
$$

which tends to zero and is far below the target scale. The slack already arises from replacing these very negative true-null p-values by U. In this construction that U-integral itself is large, so retaining favorable jumps alone cannot repair the composite-null reduction. A viable full one-sided argument must retain the negative-null attenuation, prove a suitable least-favorable-mean reduction, or use another bound; the uniform D lemma as currently stated is false.

The two-sided target requires theta_i=0, so this negative-null family is outside its null class. Cloning zero-mean two-sided nulls also forces a large rank at small u and blocks this particular harmonic construction. No two-sided harmful-jump lemma or full FDR upper bound has been established or refuted here.
