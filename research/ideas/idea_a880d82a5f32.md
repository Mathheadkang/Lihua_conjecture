UNPROVED approach, 12 November 2026. Serves [the global-null Simes question](idea_b897932e6b15.md).

By the all-or-nothing law, it suffices to prove that the worst-case Gaussian Simes size is below 1 at one fixed level $\alpha_0$ (any constant, even close to 1), uniformly in $N$ and $\Sigma$. Dense-rank crossings are already of order $q$ (log-convex tilt plus Lehec, [dense crossings](idea_a2ee64f07126.md)); only sparse crossings remain.

Tools to adapt: (1) A. Mulgund, Stochastic domination of Gaussian maxima, arXiv:2607.14087 (2026), for correlation $R$ with $R-\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}/m\succeq0$, a mixed-sign class: a Gaussian product inequality for log-concave functions with zero first moments, plus a variational choice of exponential tilts, truncation points and a Gaussian change of measure, gives domination of the maximum by the independent maximum. Test whether the same machinery bounds the probability that the empirical tail count exceeds the Simes line at a few high thresholds. (2) X. Zhang's marked anchor rotations (arXiv:2611.02513), which preserve the outside law and handle mixed signs in the two-sided case. (3) The hinge-kernel form ([hinge form](idea_e1c816206945.md)). (4) Chi-Ramdas-Wang's group intuition: positive dependence within groups and negative dependence across groups.

First test: reprove the $K=3$ simplex and mixed-sign three-vector cases (exact sizes $1.0004q$ and $1.0018q$ at $q=0.05$) with a bound uniform in the level. Failure criterion: if the tilt argument cannot avoid a factor growing with the number of high-threshold windows, record the precise loss.
