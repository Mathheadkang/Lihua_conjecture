## Precise surface statement

Let G be standard Gaussian in R^d, let v_i,w be unit vectors, and let c_i>=0 satisfy
$$
\sum_i c_i v_i v_i^{\mathsf T}\preceq I_d,\qquad
\beta=\sum_i c_i.
$$
For u>=1, t=barPhi(u), h(u)=phi(u)/t, and any thresholds a_i>=u,
$$
\phi(y)P(v_i\cdot G>=a_i\ \forall i\mid w\cdot G=y)
\le \sqrt e\,h(u)\,t^\beta \tag{FSG}
$$
for every y under the natural Gaussian conditional version. In particular h(u)<=u+1, giving a universal C(u+1)t^beta surface bound. Singular joint covariance, arbitrary numbers of vectors, arbitrary exposure w, and negative true-null means through a_i=u-theta_i>=u are all included.

Zero-weight constraints may be discarded, enlarging the event; the proof's identity concerns only positive-weight original constraints. Higher thresholds are relaxed to u before the proof. This relaxation is essential: retaining individual tails on the right with prefactor h(u) would be false.

## Complete the frame and express the surface

Add unit eigenvectors of the PSD slack I-sum_i c_i v_i v_i^T with their nonzero eigenvalues as weights. Denote both original and added indices by i. The completed frame satisfies
$$
\sum_i c_i v_i v_i^{\mathsf T}=I_d,\quad
\sum_i c_i=d,\quad
\sum_i c_i(v_i\cdot w)^2=1.
$$
Original scalar densities are f_i(z)=phi(z)1{z>=u}/t. Added densities are f_i(z)=phi(z). All integrate to one and
$$
M_i=\|f_i\|_\infty\le h(u).
$$
The exact frame identity gives, for x=yw+z with z in H=w^\perp,
$$
\prod_i f_i(v_i\cdot x)^{c_i}
=t^{-\beta}\phi_d(x)\,
1_{\{\text{all positive-weight original }v_i\cdot x>=u\}}.
$$
Integrating on H therefore equals the target Gaussian surface divided by t^beta. Gaussian normalizations match because sum_i c_i=d.

## Hyperplane frame and scalar integrals

Write b_i=v_i dot w and l_i=sqrt(1-b_i^2). For l_i>0 set e_i=Proj_H(v_i)/l_i and d_i=c_i l_i^2. The projected frame is exact:
$$
\sum_{l_i>0}d_i e_i e_i^{\mathsf T}=I_H.
$$
Define g_i(s)=f_i(l_i s+b_i y)^{1/l_i^2}. Then the variable-dependent surface integrand is product g_i(e_i dot z)^{d_i}. The geometric Brascamp-Lieb inequality on H gives its integral at most product_i(integral g_i)^{d_i}. Since f_i is a bounded probability density,
$$
\int g_i
=\frac1{l_i}\int f_i(a)^{1/l_i^2}da
\le\frac1{l_i}M_i^{1/l_i^2-1}.
$$
Raising to d_i gives the factor
$$
l_i^{-c_i l_i^2}M_i^{c_i b_i^2}.
$$
If l_i=0, that coordinate is constant on H and contributes directly f_i(b_i y)^{c_i}<=M_i^{c_i}, the same M_i exponent because b_i^2=1. Zero c_i terms contribute one. If dim H=0, the integral and the projected-frame product are empty and the same direct factors prove the assertion.

## Pay the entire penalty without dimension loss

The factors involving M_i multiply to at most h(u)^{sum_i c_i b_i^2}=h(u). For the remaining factors,
$$
\log\prod_{l_i>0}l_i^{-c_i l_i^2}
=-\frac12\sum_i c_i(1-b_i^2)\log(1-b_i^2)
\le\frac12\sum_i c_i b_i^2=\frac12.
$$
Here -(1-z)log(1-z)<=z on [0,1], with the zero-limit convention at z=1. Thus the hyperplane integral is at most sqrt(e) h(u), proving (FSG). No inverse eigenvalue, conditional variance, dimension, number of directions or covariance-rank factor appears.

## Inline geometric Brascamp-Lieb proof for the densities used here

The needed version is: unit vectors e_i in R^r and weights d_i>=0 with sum_i d_i e_i e_i^T=I_r imply
$$
\int_{\mathbb R^r}\prod_i g_i(e_i\cdot x)^{d_i}dx
\le\prod_i\left(\int_\mathbb R g_i\right)^{d_i}.
$$
Our g_i are powers of translated/scaled Gaussian or truncated Gaussian densities; they are smooth and strictly positive on an interval (a half-line or all of R). Normalize their integrals to one. Let T_i be the increasing map transporting density g_i to standard Gaussian:
$$
g_i(s)=\phi(T_i(s))T_i'(s)
$$
on its support interval. Put Theta(x)=sum_i d_i T_i(e_i dot x)e_i on the convex intersection of these support slabs. Its derivative is positive definite wherever defined, and Theta is the gradient of a strictly convex function, hence injective on this convex domain.

The frame synthesis contraction gives
$$
\|\Theta(x)\|^2\le\sum_i d_i T_i(e_i\cdot x)^2.
$$
Also the frame determinant inequality is
$$
\det\left(\sum_i d_i q_i e_i e_i^{\mathsf T}\right)
\ge\prod_i q_i^{d_i},\qquad q_i>0. \tag{BD}
$$
For completeness, put V's columns equal to sqrt(d_i)e_i. Cauchy-Binet writes the determinant as sum_S alpha_S product_{i in S}q_i, with alpha_S=det(V_S)^2>=0 and sum_S alpha_S=1. Differentiation at every q_i=1 shows sum_{S containing i}alpha_S=d_i. Weighted arithmetic-geometric mean then gives (BD).

Using (BD) with q_i=T_i' and sum_i d_i=r, the original integrand is
$$
(2\pi)^{-r/2}
e^{-\sum_i d_iT_i^2/2}
\prod_i(T_i')^{d_i}
\le\phi_r(\Theta(x))\det D\Theta(x).
$$
Injective change of variables bounds its integral by the Gaussian mass of Theta's image, at most one. Endpoint limits handle the half-line supports; on the open support domain all maps are smooth and the displayed argument applies directly. Approximation from interior compact subdomains and monotone convergence preserve the inequality. Restoring the scalar normalizations proves the needed geometric inequality without an undeclared black-box theorem.

## What remains for BH

This full-Gram surface estimate is stronger in covariance scope than idea_ffde21d1df38's independent-null factorial bound, but it replaces integer subset size by a fractional-frame weight beta. To apply it to the main target one must derive a real fractional-cover allocation of the exact signed whole-share jump at actual physical BH ranks, and control velocity marks before summing. Selecting a fractional packing independently at every rank can repeat charges and is not justified. No such allocation is claimed by (FSG), and its proof alone does not settle the universal theorem.

The complete quantitative surface lemma is therefore a new first gate that has been worked out, with the actual BH aggregation explicitly separate. The target's original quantifiers, status and Proof remain untouched.
