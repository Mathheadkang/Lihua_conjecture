Open question recorded at the researcher's request, 12 November 2026, after the parallel round of routes. Agent-written summary of project findings; nothing here is a verified result beyond the approved nodes it cites.

## The question

For a finite $N$ and a unit-diagonal positive semidefinite $\Sigma$ (singular allowed, correlations of either sign), let $Z\sim\mathcal N_N(0,\Sigma)$, $p_i=\overline\Phi(Z_i)$, and let $B(q)=\sup_{N,\Sigma}\Pr\{\exists k:\ p_{(k)}\le qk/N\}$, the worst-case size of the one-sided Simes test (equivalently, the all-null FDR of ordinary BH).

**Q.** Is there a universal $C$ with $B(q)\le Cq$ for all small $q$? Equivalently (by the amplification below), is $B(\alpha_0)<1$ for some fixed level $\alpha_0$?

## Why it is the crux

- **Necessary for the main theorem.** The all-null independent-block amplification ([the amplification observation](idea_8699fa13f1f6.md)) gives $B(q)\ge1-(1-B(q/M))^M$ for every $M$. So any uniform bound on the worst-case FDR that tends to zero as $q\to0$ (in particular the target $Cq\sqrt{\log(1/q)}$) forces $B(t)\le C't$. A Gaussian family with $B(q)/q$ unbounded would therefore refute Lei's conjecture, not only a route. This step should be checked independently.
- **Sufficient for the sign-splitting route** (its verified reduction needs exactly the linear Simes bound, plus two other lemmas), and it settles the no-alternative part of both rare caps ([the rare-direction cap observation](idea_13a0fb394818.md)).
- Every set-aside lemma of the round stalls in the same regime, so progress here is likely to transfer.

## What is known (project and library)

- Positive correlations: Simes size at most $q$ (approved library node from Chi, Ramdas and Wang).
- Dense-rank crossings ($k/N\ge e^{-\delta u}$): order $q$ for any correlation signs, by a log-convex Gaussian tilt and Lehec's semi-log-convex tail bound ([dense crossings](idea_a2ee64f07126.md)).
- Pairwise nonpositively correlated crossers: order $q^2$ via Slepian and a union bound (Slepian is not in the library).
- Independent, fully correlated and block models satisfy the hinge-kernel tail bound below.
- Searches found no large-size family: global-null simplex frames stay at most a constant times $q$; orthogonal one-null staircase copies stay below about $22q$.

## Where it is stuck

Sparse crossings: very high thresholds crossed by few coordinates, with many weakly correlated directions of both signs. Under independence this regime carries all of the Simes mass. Per-window union bounds cost about $q$ per window over about $\log N$ windows; the Gibbs (exponential-kernel) form loses a Mills factor of about $\sqrt{\log N}$; angular-entropy bounds lose their entropy term for spread-out mixed-sign configurations.

## Suggested attacks

1. **Hinge-kernel tail bound** ([hinge form](idea_e1c816206945.md)): Simes rejection forces $\sup_x \frac1N\sum_i(Z_i-x)_+\big/\mathbb E(G-x)_+\ \ge c\sqrt{\log(1/q)}$. A dimension-free bound $\Pr(\text{ratio}\ge a)\lesssim 1/a$, summed over dyadic scales, would settle Q.
2. **Numerical search for a counterexample** in mixed-sign high-dimensional families (random signed frames, signed equiangular frames, sparse signed graphs, sign-cube mixtures), tracking $B(q)/q$ as $q$ decreases; certify any hit with interval arithmetic, as Dobriban did for the two-sided case.
3. **Literature:** the negative-dependence results of Chi, Ramdas and Wang beyond the imported positive theorem; reports that Simes is liberal under negative correlation (Block et al., 2008, as cited in secondary sources, unverified); Gaussian comparison and anti-concentration results whose constants depend only on expected maxima.
4. **Sign decomposition:** combine the positive-dependence and nonpositive-dependence cases through a mixed-sign comparison inequality, if one exists for this event.

## Literature status and evidence (added 12 November 2026)

- **Stated open in the literature.** Chi, Ramdas and Wang, Multiple testing under negative dependence (arXiv:2212.09706v4; Bernoulli 31(2), 2025), Theorem 8: for Gaussian dependence, (i) nonnegative correlations give size at most $\alpha$; (ii) nonpositive correlations give size at most $\alpha+2\alpha^2+6\alpha^3$ for $\alpha\le0.1$ (at most $1.26\alpha$); (iii) for every fixed $K$ and every Gaussian dependence, $\limsup_{\alpha\downarrow0}\Pr(\text{Simes rejects})/\alpha\le1$, not uniform in $K$. Verbatim from the paper: "It remains an open question to find a useful upper bound for $\Pr(S_K(P)\le\alpha)$ over all Gaussian-dependent $P$ for practical values of $\alpha$", and "A simple conjecture is that (20) or a similar inequality holds for all Gaussian-dependent $P$, but a proof seems to be beyond our current techniques." So Q, with $C\approx1.26$, is their conjecture. Checked at the level of the arXiv HTML text only.
- **Classical small cases.** Hochberg and Rom (1995) and Samuel-Cahn (1996): one-sided Simes is slightly anticonservative for negatively correlated normals; for $K=2$ the size at $\alpha=0.05$ is about $0.0501$ (as quoted by Chi, Ramdas and Wang).
- **Two-sided analogue.** X. Zhang, Exact Simes inequalities for two-sided Gaussian tests (arXiv:2611.02513, Nov 2026): validity for every fixed $m$ at small enough levels uniformly in the covariance, for bounded-degree correlation graphs, and for slowly growing independent blocks; the general two-sided case is stated open. Its marked anchor rotations may transfer to the one-sided case.
- **Possible tool.** A. Mulgund, Stochastic domination of Gaussian maxima (arXiv:2607.14087, 2026): for correlation $R$ with $R-\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}/m\succeq0$, a mixed-sign class, the maximum is stochastically dominated by the independent maximum, via a Gaussian product inequality for log-concave functions with zero first moments plus exponential tilts. It treats only the maximum (the $k=1$ Simes term), but the technique handles mixed signs.
- **All-or-nothing reduction (agent derivation from the amplification observation; check independently).** $B(q)\ge1-(1-B(q/M))^M$ and $B$ is nondecreasing, so either $B(t)\le Ct$ for all small $t$, or $B(q)=1$ at every level. Equivalently, $B(\alpha_0)<1$ at one level gives $B(t)\le t\log(1/(1-B(\alpha_0)))/\alpha_0$ for $t\le\alpha_0$, up to the monotone interpolation between $\alpha_0/M$ grid points.
- **Numerical evidence (agent Monte Carlo, scratchpad only, not project results).** About 600 configurations: simplices and simplex blocks, antithetic pairs, random signed sphere frames ($d$ up to 100), sign-cube frames, mixed-sign one-factor models, and an adversarial search over loadings for $d\le5$. Levels down to $q=10^{-4}$ (smaller only for tiny $N$), $N$ up to $10^4$. Every high reading fell back to $1$ within error on fresh samples. The largest confirmed excess, computed by exact integration, is $1.0018q$ (best 3-vector frame in $\mathbb R^2$ at $q=0.05$), falling to $1.0000$ by $q=0.001$. No family shows size$/q$ growing as $q$ falls. Limits: Gaussian factor models only, no importance sampling, high-rank structures only as random frames.
