## Subclass and finite-level bound

Take any finite original one-sided Gaussian BH model with unit-diagonal PSD covariance Sigma, original null set I_0={i:theta_i<=0}, m=|I_0|, A=N-m, and arbitrary level q in (0,1). Suppose Sigma_ij<=0 for distinct original null labels i,j. Singular covariance and negative original null means are permitted. Alternative means and all covariances involving alternatives are unrestricted. In particular the alternatives may be unsaturated and arbitrarily correlated with the null vector; no independence or conditioning on alternatives is imposed.

Write b=e q(A+1). If b<1, then the original actual-rank BH procedure satisfies

$$
\operatorname{FDR}_{\mathrm{BH}}\le
\min\left\{1,\frac{b}{1-b}\right\}.
$$

In particular, if q<=1/[2e(A+1)], then FDR_BH<=2e q(A+1), uniformly in N, null count, covariance rank, null means, and alternative means within this subclass. If m=0, FDR is identically zero.

## Unconditional count moment

Let C(s)=sum_{i in I_0}1_{P_i<=s}. The Gaussian sufficient-condition argument in the approved gaussian_bh_conditional_subpoisson_calibration, applied to the null marginal law with trivial conditioning and deterministic auxiliary alternative p-values, supplies the unconditional count inequality. Every original null has a superuniform marginal, and the original null covariance is nonpositive off the diagonal. Consequently, for s in (0,q] and lambda>=0,

$$
\mathbb E e^{\lambda C(s)}
\le \exp\{ms(e^\lambda-1)\}.
$$

This invocation concerns only the null marginal count law. It does not condition on the original alternatives or assume that their conditioning preserves covariance signs.

## Actual-rank event containment

Let R,V be the original actual total and false-rejection counts. On {V=v>=1}, at most A alternatives are rejected, so R<=v+A. The original BH threshold is qR/N. Therefore

$$
\{V=v\}\subseteq
\left\{C\left(\frac{q(v+A)}N\right)\ge v\right\}.
$$

This uses the original labels and original denominator. The deterministic threshold is at most q because v<=m and m+A=N. At that threshold the sub-Poisson mean budget is

$$
\frac{mq(v+A)}N\le q(v+A)=:\mu_v.
$$

Since b<1, v/mu_v=1/[q(1+A/v)]>1 for every integer v>=1. With lambda_v=log(v/mu_v), Chernoff gives

$$
\mathbb P(V=v)
\le \exp\{-v\log(v/\mu_v)+v-\mu_v\}
\le \left[e q\left(1+\frac Av\right)\right]^v
\le [e q(A+1)]^v=b^v.
$$

Finally FDP<=1_{V>=1}, so

$$
\operatorname{FDR}_{\mathrm{BH}}
\le\sum_{v=1}^m\mathbb P(V=v)
\le\sum_{v=1}^\infty b^v=\frac b{1-b}.
$$

The bound FDP<=1 supplies the displayed cap.

## Search implication and limitation

For q-dependent finite models in this subclass, A=O(sqrt(log(1/q))) implies q(A+1)->0 and a bounded FDR/[q sqrt(log(1/q))]. If A=o(sqrt(log(1/q))), this normalized ratio tends to zero. In particular a fixed number of original alternatives cannot produce a main-order counterexample, even with arbitrarily many nulls and without saturation or block independence.

The argument requires nonpositive original null covariance. Its coefficient grows with A and it does not control arbitrary positive null covariance or arbitrary alternative counts. It is a draft subclass observation, not a proof or verification verdict for the unrestricted main theorem.
