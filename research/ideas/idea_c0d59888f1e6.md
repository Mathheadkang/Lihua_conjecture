This explicitly tests the proposed envelope-equation approach idea_27baeafdd0f3. It is not a counterexample to the frozen-ray conjecture or either BH root. The two-coordinate family already belongs to a class for which the three frozen-ray assertions survive; the new obstruction concerns a stronger POINTWISE generator bound.

For A>0 and 0<b<1 take independent coordinates G_1,G_2~N(0,1), v_1=e_1,v_2=e_2, and deterministic weights
$$
w_1=\frac{e^{-Ab}}{2\cosh(Ab)},\qquad
w_2=\frac{e^{Ab}}{2\cosh(Ab)}.
$$
Let f_t(x)=e^{-t^2/2}(w_1 e^{t x_1}+w_2 e^{t x_2}) and Lambda(x)=sup_{t>=0} f_t(x). At x=(A+b,A-b), set alpha=e^{A^2/2}/cosh(Ab). Then exactly
$$
f_t(x)=\alpha\,e^{-(t-A)^2/2}\cosh\{b(t-A)\}.
$$
Because log cosh(b d)<=b^2 d^2/2, the maximum is unique at t=A and is strict with partial_tt log f_A=-(1-b^2). Lambda is smooth near this point: for nearby x, half the coordinate gap stays below one, so log f_t remains strictly concave in t.

The posterior weights at t=A are both 1/2. Thus m=(1,1)/2, C=(1/4)[[1,-1],[-1,1]], x^TCx=b^2, and the local envelope equation for L=Delta-x.grad gives
$$
\Lambda(x)=\alpha,\qquad
\frac{L\Lambda(x)}{\Lambda(x)}
=\frac{1+A^2b^2}{2(1-b^2)}.
$$
At fixed b=1/2, alpha tends to infinity with A, while L Lambda/Lambda is unbounded. Even a uniform positive lower bound on 1-x^TCx therefore does not permit a universal pointwise L Lambda<=C Lambda. At fixed A, letting b increase to one also makes the denominator vanish. At the limiting b=1, the unique maximizer is quartically flat (log f_{A+d}-log alpha=-d^4/12+O(d^6)), and alpha can be arbitrarily large by increasing A.

Consequence for the route: any useful dimension-free estimate must be genuinely integrated, level-set, or signed; it cannot replace the envelope curvature by a universal pointwise multiple of Lambda. These are exact deterministic calculations at points in the Gaussian support, not probability estimates or evidence against the conjecture. Independent mathematical verification is still required before reusing the observation as a proved lemma.
