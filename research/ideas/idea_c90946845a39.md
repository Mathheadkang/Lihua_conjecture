Concrete proposed outer-limit mechanism to verify, not a proved node. For each original rank r, define the hypothetical k=r-1 upper stable clone branch nu_r(m) by m=u(k+nu)-s S^{-1}(nu), with deterministic birth m_r(s) and chosen-score birth threshold b_r(s) after removing compensation/normalization. Expected b_r(s)=u_r+(s/t_s)log A_r+o(s/t_s), A_r=1/[r h(u_r)]. Consider a finite rank-threshold surrogate: rank r qualifies if n_{-i}(u_r)>=r, or n_{-i}(u_r)>=r-1 and T_i>=b_r(s); take its common largest qualifying integer rank, preserving original labels and signed jumps. This is only a comparison functional, not a Gaussian BH model. For small s the b_r remain decreasing in r. Maximality forces exactly r-1 unchanged selected scores when the chosen block is counted, and exactly r otherwise. A hypothetical high root is feasible for the true population curve whenever enough unchanged scores exceed u(r-1+nu_r). If it loses an unchanged score despite n_{-i}(u_r)>=r-1, some T_j lies in [u_r,u(r-1+nu_r)), a strip of width at most C(1-nu_r). Original PD gives a bounded conditional density of T_j given T_i; since nu_r depends only on T_i, the integrated strip probability is bounded by C E[1-nu_r(T_i)] on the high branch, which the upper-branch integral estimate suggests is O((s/t_s)^2). Extra unchanged atoms can only make a high root feasible and lead to a later common crossing; they must be handled by largest-rank maximality, not blockwise optimization. Low roots below a high-root birth should have exponentially small clone fraction for r>=2 because the fixed gap between u_{r-1} and u_r remains; the r=1 no-root case needs separate endpoint control. If a complete common-crossing sandwich and fractional-FDP error bound follow, the surrogate changes FDP only in disjoint thin T_i bands at the deterministic u_r, and its leading expectation is the signed sum of individual threshold shifts against mu_{i,r}(total). Essential unfinished steps: rigorous global branch classification and sandwich, all population atom boundaries, uniform-in-background clipping estimates, low roots and r=1 behavior, and exact compensation asymptotics. Do not assert that local root calculations alone prove this global comparison.
