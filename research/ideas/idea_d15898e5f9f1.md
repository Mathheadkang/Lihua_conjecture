## Provenance and status

The root agent proposed the fractional-frame uncentered velocity bound in the first section. I checked its layer-cake calculation and then derived the scalar marked-tail and fixed-subset integrated interpolation kernels below. These are draft agent calculations, not native independent verification. They complement the centered Mills-drift identity idea_0b153b121437; they do not discard that identity or assert the final actual-rank aggregation.

## 1. An absolute marked surface from fractional packing

Use the frame notation of idea_ac8371a287d5: sum_i c_i v_i v_i^T<=I, beta=sum_i c_i, and A={v_i dot G>=u for all original indices}, u>=1, t=barPhi(u). Let Y=w dot G and D=zeta dot G be any standard Gaussian exposure and velocity; no orthogonality is needed in this first bound.

The augmented frame with original weights c_i/2 and added vector plus or minus zeta of weight 1/2 remains at most I. For s>=0, the unequal-threshold hyperplane proof gives
$$
\phi(y)P(A,\ \pm D>=s\mid Y=y)
\le\sqrt e\,h(\max\{u,s\})\,t^{\beta/2}\barPhi(s)^{1/2}.
$$
The hazard bound also applies for s in [0,1] directly through the scalar density maxima, since h(s)>=h(0)>phi(0). Layer cake gives
$$
\phi(y)E[|D|1_A\mid Y=y]
\le2\sqrt e\,t^{\beta/2}
\left[h(u)\int_0^u\sqrt{\barPhi(s)}\,ds
+2\sqrt{\barPhi(u)}\right]
\le C h(u)t^{\beta/2}. \tag{AV}
$$
The integral over [0,infinity) of sqrt(barPhi(s)) is finite; the tail identity follows by differentiating sqrt(barPhi(s)). Additional arbitrary marks M in [0,1] may be kept on the left. Negative null means are covered by event inclusion, as before.

Thus a frame of total packing weight beta>=2 pays at least one full factor t without a drift term. The cost for beta near one needs a separate treatment; no exponent loss is silently ignored.

## 2. A scalar marked-tail inequality without loss of the tail exponent

Let X,Y,D be jointly standard Gaussian with Cov(Y,D)=0, allowing singular laws. For every A>=1,
$$
\sup_y\phi(y)E[|D|1_{\{X>=A\}}\mid Y=y]\le C\phi(A). \tag{SV}
$$
Here the exposure may be arbitrarily correlated with X, as may the velocity.

To check this, put b=Cov(X,Y), sigma=sqrt(1-b^2), and for sigma>0 let Z=(X-bY)/sigma. Then Z is standard and independent of Y. Because D is also independent of Y, write D=k Z+sqrt(1-k^2)H with H independent of (Y,Z). At Y=y the threshold is z=(A-b y)/sigma, and
$$
E[|D|1_{\{X>=A\}}\mid Y=y]
\le E[|Z|1_{\{Z>=z\}}]+E|H|\,barPhi(z)
\le\phi(z)+2\phi(0)1_{\{z<0\}}+C\,barPhi(z).
$$
The first term obeys phi(y)phi(z)<=phi(0)phi(A), since
$$
y^2+\frac{(A-b y)^2}{1-b^2}
=A^2+\frac{(y-bA)^2}{1-b^2}.
$$
If z<0 then |y|>A, so phi(y)<=phi(A). For the remaining surface probability, if |b|<=1/2, writing the joint density and integrating X>=A bounds it by C barPhi(A)<=C phi(A). If |b|>1/2, use phi(x)<=phi(A) for x>=A and integrate the conditional Y density over all x; that integral is 1/|b|<=2. This proves the same bound. If |b|=1, the tail event is determined by Y and D remains independent of Y, giving the result directly.

Consequently a Gaussian velocity with standard deviation sigma_D, orthogonal to its unit-variance exposure, has the same bound multiplied by sigma_D. This avoids the half-exponent loss of (AV) for a single averaged tail event.

## 3. Universal physical speed on the symmetric covariance path

Use the exact path and K from idea_0b153b121437:
$$
\Sigma_s=(1-s)I+s\Sigma,\quad
v_j=\Sigma_s^{1/2}e_j,\quad
v_j'=K_s v_j,\quad
K_s=\tfrac12(\Sigma-I)\Sigma_s^{-1}.
$$
For every physical coordinate j and 0<s<1,
$$
\|v_j'\|^2
=\tfrac14 e_j^{\mathsf T}(\Sigma-I)^2\Sigma_s^{-1}e_j
\le\frac1{4s(1-s)}. \tag{SP}
$$
Indeed, for lambda>=0,
$$
\frac{(\lambda-1)^2}{1-s+s\lambda}
\le\frac{\lambda}{s}+\frac1{1-s};
$$
integrating this scalar inequality against the spectral measure at e_j uses e_j^T Sigma e_j=1. Also v_j dot v_j'=0, so the physical position and its Gaussian velocity satisfy the orthogonality required in (SV). The bound uses no rank, eigenvalue or dimension restriction.

## 4. Fixed selected-null subsets gain independent noise before the endpoint

Fix a deterministic null subset S of cardinality v>=2. Let
$$
a_S(s)=\frac1v\sum_{i\in S}v_i(s),\qquad
\rho_S(s)^2=\|a_S(s)\|^2.
$$
Because the covariance path is linear,
$$
\rho_S(s)^2=\frac{1-s}{v}+s\rho_S(1)^2
\le1-\frac{1-s}{2}.
$$
On the null exceedance event X_i+theta_i>=u for every i in S, theta_i<=0, the average score a_S dot G is at least u. Thus the normalized average Z_S=a_S dot G/rho_S is at least u/rho_S. Apply (SV), including any actual-rank mark M in [0,1], to the exposure Y_j=v_j dot G and its velocity dotY_j=v_j' dot G. It gives, uniformly in the exposure value y,
$$
\phi(y)E[|\dot Y_j|1_{\{\forall i\in S:X_i+\theta_i>=u\}}M\mid Y_j=y]
\le C\|v_j'\|\phi(u/\rho_S(s))
$$
$$
\le C\|v_j'\|\,t\,u\,
e^{-(1-s)u^2/4}. \tag{FS}
$$
The last inequality uses phi(u)<=t(u+1)<=2tu and rho_S^(-2)-1>=1-rho_S^2>=(1-s)/2. If the average vector vanishes, the simultaneous positive-threshold event is empty and the bound is interpreted as zero.

Combining (SP) with
$$
u\int_0^1\frac{e^{-(1-s)u^2/4}}{\sqrt{s(1-s)}}\,ds\le C,\qquad u>=1,
$$
proves the integrated kernel
$$
\int_0^1 \phi(y_s)
E[|\dot Y_j|1_{\{\forall i\in S:X_i+\theta_i>=u\}}M_s
\mid Y_j=y_s]\,ds
\le C\,barPhi(u). \tag{IFT}
$$
The exposure values y_s and marks M_s may vary arbitrarily with s. The displayed integral bound follows by splitting at s=1/2: the first half has an exponentially small factor in u, and in the second half set z=u sqrt(1-s).

This is a real time-integrated gain: the factor u in a fixed surface estimate is paid by the width of the independent-noise layer. All original negative null means, singular final covariance and arbitrary exposure means are allowed.

## 5. Exact limitation for the BH plan

The fixed subset S and fixed u are essential to the present statement. For a fixed physical rank, u=u(qr/N) is constant, so the actual BH face mask can be included. However the identities do not allow summing over all selected subsets S or replacing them by a time-varying adaptive subset without a cover argument. There are also singleton selected-null states v=1, for which rho_S(s)=1 and the independent-noise gain disappears. They cannot be dismissed: iid null BH has such states with probability of order q.

Thus (AV), (SV), (SP), and (IFT) are concrete complementary kernels for a real fractional/near-coherent rank allocation. They are not a claimed global RMS-speed certificate and do not assert any per-coordinate qU/N payment. The exact signed drift and quantile corrections remain preserved in idea_0b153b121437. No pending main plan is created by simply renaming the missing subset/rank cover as a theorem.

### Replication calibration supplied by the root agent

Uniformly duplicating every original test twice preserves all p-values, null labels, physical BH thresholds after dividing ranks by two, and the exact FDP/FDR. In particular every false-rejection state then has at least two false rejections. Consequently a global O(q) estimate cannot be inferred merely from applying (IFT) to states with at least two selected nulls: that would turn the known q sqrt(log(1/q)) lower families into O(q) families after duplication. The covariance path used in (IFT) starts from independent noise for the physical copies and breaks their equality before the final endpoint; it is not the duplicated original path. A valid adaptive cover must explicitly account for this multiplicity/path cost. This check does not invalidate the fixed-subset kernel, but it identifies a necessary obstruction to the naive summation of that kernel.
