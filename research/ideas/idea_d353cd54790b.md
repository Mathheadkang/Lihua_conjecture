This is a proposed obstruction to an auxiliary proof route, not a counterexample to either main order target. A fresh native plan worker and the root independently considered the same stress test. The calculation below remains draft evidence until a fresh reviewer checks it.

Let 0<q<=1/2, choose an integer N>=max(2,1/q), and take independent T_i~N(0,1), with one-sided p_i=barPhi(T_i). Thus theta=0, Sigma=I_N, every hypothesis is a true null, and p_i are independent uniform(0,1). Define V(t)=sum_i 1{p_i<=t} and M_q=sup_{q/N<=t<=q} V(t)/(N t). Consider the strongest bounded form of a free-threshold FDP envelope, B_q=min(1,q M_q).

Write Y=N min_i p_i. Its density on (0,N) is f_Y(y)=(1-y/N)^(N-1). For q<=y<=1, t=y/N lies in [q/N,q] because Nq>=1. At least one p-value is <=t, so M_q>=1/y, and therefore B_q>=q/y since q/y<=1. Moreover f_Y(y)>=(1-1/N)^(N-1)>=exp(-1) for 0<=y<=1 (the last inequality follows from log(1-x)>=-x/(1-x), 0<x<1). Consequently

$$
E B_q >= e^{-1}q int_q^1 dy/y = e^{-1} q log(1/q).
$$

Since log(1/q)/(1+sqrt(log(1/q))) is unbounded as q decreases, no finite universal A makes E B_q<=A q[1+sqrt(log(1/q))] for all these finite Gaussian models. In particular even clipping the envelope at one does not remove its excess logarithm.

For comparison, actual ordinary largest-rank BH in this same independent all-null model has FDR exactly q. Here is a direct check, included to avoid an imported black box. Let R_i^0 be its rejection count after replacing p_i by zero. This count is at least one and depends only on the other p-values. The deterministic step-up identity gives 1{i rejected}/(R vee 1)=1{p_i<=q R_i^0/N}/R_i^0. To verify it: if i is rejected with count r, then at every higher candidate rank its original p-value already passes, so setting it to zero cannot create a new higher qualifying rank and R_i^0=r. Conversely if p_i<=q R_i^0/N, rank R_i^0 remains qualified after restoring p_i, while restoring cannot increase a rank; hence R=R_i^0 and i is rejected. Independence now gives conditional expectation q/N for each i, and summing gives q.

Thus the logarithmic loss is introduced by optimizing over all thresholds before taking expectation. A new route must retain the actual BH largest-crossing constraint or another quantitatively justified selection restriction. This observation does not rule out such a restricted maximal estimate, and is not evidence against the universal-order conjecture itself. The second fresh plan worker is exploring other mechanisms; no pending plan was created for this disproved gate.
