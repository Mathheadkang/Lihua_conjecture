Scope diagnostic, not a counterexample to the PD clone lemma. Take original N=2, K=1, both true means zero, T1=T2=Z with Z,Y independent standard normal, v1=v2=e0, and perturb only T1 to (Z+epsilon Y)/sqrt(1+epsilon^2), with c=0 and w=e1. Original covariance is singular, so it fails the lemma's positive-definiteness hypothesis. Let U=S^{-1}(q), a=S^{-1}(q/2)>U. The all-null BH FDR is P(T1>=U,T2>=U) plus the union of single-coordinate a-tail events outside this intersection. That latter excess is bounded by P(|T1-T2|>=a-U)=O(exp(-c_q/epsilon^2)). The intersection is P(Z>=max(U,U sqrt(1+epsilon^2)-epsilon Y)), so (F_epsilon-q)/epsilon tends to -phi(U) E[(-Y)_+]=-phi(U)/sqrt(2pi). A verbatim coordinate-face formula with other statistic held at its coincident face value gives Delta_{1,2}=1 at U and Delta_{1,1}=0 at a, so its predicted integral of g(Y)=Y is zero. Thus the PD proof cannot be extended to coincident singular faces merely by substituting a degenerate covariance; shared-face directional activation contributes at first order. A singular extension needs joint-face/tangent-cone data or a separately justified limiting procedure. Original unrestricted targets and current PD statement remain unchanged.
