Draft elementary observation; no unrestricted FDR upper bound is claimed. Fix a finite admissible model, q<1/2, and the ordinary largest BH rank R. At rank k let z_k=Q^{-1}(qk/N) for one-sided tests, and z_k=Q^{-1}(qk/(2N)) for two-sided tests. For x>=0 let J_k(x) consist of false nulls with theta_j>=z_k+x (one-sided) or |theta_j|>=z_k+x (two-sided), and let m=|J_k(x)|>=k. Write T_j=theta_j+g_j, where g is any centered unit-diagonal Gaussian vector. On {R<k}, fewer than k statistics pass the rank-k cutoff, so at least m-k+1 members of J_k(x) fail. Such a failure implies -g_j>x in the one-sided case, or -sign(theta_j)g_j>x in the two-sided case. Hence pathwise (m-k+1)1{R<k} <= sum_{j in J_k(x)}1{-sign_j g_j>x}, sign_j=1 or sign(theta_j), respectively. Taking expectation gives P(R<k)<=min(1,m Q(x)/(m-k+1)), with arbitrary correlations and singular covariance allowed. This uses only C_k>=k => R>=k, not a false converse.

For two-sided tests let V_k(g)=sum_{theta_i=0}1{|g_i|>=z_k}, and let m_k count false nulls with |theta_j|>=z_k. Centered true-null indicators agree at g and -g; each of those m_k alternatives passes at least one reflected cutoff because max(|theta_j+g_j|,|theta_j-g_j|)>=|theta_j|. Therefore C_k(g)+C_k(-g)>=2V_k(g)+m_k. If V_k(g)+ceil(m_k/2)>=k, at least one of the two integer counts is >=k, so max(R(g),R(-g))>=k. These are rank-floor/rare-small-rank controls only. They do not bound both reflected FDPs or close a rank-sum estimate; an attempt to use them must retain this missing obligation. The statement and derivation still need independent audit.
