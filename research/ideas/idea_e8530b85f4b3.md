## P0 and A1(b): finite-sample BH identity

For each positive threshold t_k=qk/N, the equivalence between an order-statistic qualification and n_x(t_k)>=k is exact with ties. If the largest qualifying rank r is positive and m=n_x(t_r)>r, monotonicity of t_k implies n_x(t_m)>=m, contradicting maximality. Hence R=r. Decreasing a p-value cannot reduce any count, so forcing coordinate i to zero produces a maximal rank r0>=1 and r0>=r. On the event x_i<=t_r0, the original and forced vectors have the same count at t_r0; therefore r>=r0, equality holds, i is rejected, and R=r0. On its complement, r<=r0 and either r=0 or its threshold is at most t_r0<x_i; hence i is not rejected. Thus rejection is exactly x_i<=t_r0 and the reciprocal denominator on that event is 1/r0=(q/N)/t_r0. Substituting the exact residual reconstruction gives the asserted pointwise identity, including ties and zero rejections. The proof's count identity n_x0(t)=n_x(t)+1{x_i>t} is used only for t>=0, where it holds (it need not hold at negative t, which are never used).

## A1(a): arbitrary Gaussian covariance, including singular laws

A true two-sided null has theta_i=0 and Var(T_i)=1. The residual is a linear transformation of the joint Gaussian observation, has zero mean, covariance Sigma_-i,-i-c_i c_i^T, and zero covariance with T_i. Expanding its characteristic function with T_i gives a block-diagonal covariance and hence a product characteristic function even when either full covariance or residual covariance is singular. This proves independence without conditioning on a density for Y_i. Its covariance is PSD because it is a covariance; equivalently, evaluate the original PSD quadratic form on (-c_i^T v,v). No invertibility of Sigma or residual covariance is used. N=1 is covered by the zero-dimensional residual convention.

## A1(c): all exogenous finite-grid Borel baselines

For every t in the positive finite grid {q/N,...,q}, the standard-normal null gives P(p(|T_i|)<=t)=t. Independence therefore gives conditional expectation 1 for a(T_i;g(Y_i)) for every Borel grid-valued g, with no regularity or nondegeneracy requirement on the law of Y_i. To check the specific baseline and the remaining measurability claims, the coordinate comparisons p(|theta_j+c_ij x+y_j|)<=t_k are closed-set indicators of |theta_j+c_ij x+y_j|>=u_k. ## A1(d): exact FDR decomposition and bounds

The sum of the pointwise contributions over true nulls is exactly V/(R vee 1). Finite summation and expectation yield q*pi0+(q/N)*sum Delta_i. Nonnegativity of each a gives Delta_i>=-1. The last asserted sum equals FDR and is at most 1 pointwise before expectation. An empty null set gives zero on both sides. All quantities are finite for every fixed N and q>0; a bound N/q is sufficient and does not assert a dimension-uniform estimate.

## A1(e): branches, arbitrary intercepts, endpoints, and finite steps

For a centered unit Gaussian, sign is uniform and independent of magnitude, whose density on [0,infinity) is 2*phi; their pair is independent of Y_i by (a). The event T_i=0 has probability zero. Conditional integration of the nonnegative bounded Borel integrand a(sigma*s;tau_i(sigma*s,y)), then averaging signs and Y_i, gives the exact branch formula. Joint Borel measurability proved above and Tonelli ensure y -> Psi(f_i,y^sigma) is measurable even for a singular/atomic residual law.

For every fixed y, each arbitrary intercept b_j=theta_j+y_j, each coefficient c_ij (including zero), each branch sign, and each u_k>0, the inactive set {s>=0: |b_j+sigma*c_ij*s|<u_k} is the intersection of [0,infinity) with an open interval when the slope is nonzero; it is the full half-line or empty when the slope is zero. Thus each indicator has at most two finite endpoint locations. The finite collection has at most 2*N*(N-1) such locations. On each intervening open interval every indicator, and hence the maximal rank and branch threshold, is constant. Coincident endpoints may create an exceptional isolated value at that endpoint, but cannot add endpoint locations. Replacing each finite endpoint value by its right limit yields a right-continuous finite-step function, still grid-valued and strictly positive, differing on only a finite set. Its Psi integral is unchanged because integration is against the continuous density 2*phi(s) ds. This also covers an endpoint at zero, zero slopes, perfectly correlated coordinates, repeated coordinates, and N=1. No monotonicity or right-continuity of the original branch function is assumed. The reference to A4 is unnecessary here because the interval geometry follows directly from the displayed affine inequality; no later A2 result was used or audited.

## A1(f): arbitrary positive-integer replication factors

For M repeated copies of every coordinate, n_x'=M*n_x. If r>0, rank Mr qualifies at its unchanged original threshold. If any k>Mr qualified, m=ceil(k/M)>r would satisfy t'_k<=t_m and n_x(t_m)>=n_x(t'_k)>=k/M; integer counts give n_x(t_m)>=m, a contradiction. Therefore the new rank is Mr. When r=0, the lower bound 0 is supplied by the explicit {0} convention in the maximum, rather than by calling 0 a qualifying positive rank; the same contradiction excludes every k>0. This is a one-line convention clarification in the copied proof. The rejection set is exactly all copies of the original rejection set, so V'=MV, R'=MR and FDP is unchanged also at R=0. Duplicating the Gaussian coordinates and means produces an admissible PSD unit-diagonal Gaussian model, and its true nulls are exactly the null copies. Applying (b) to each copy gives E[1{i rejected}/(R' vee 1)]=(1/M)E[1{i rejected}/(R vee 1)], because the numerator vanishes when R=0. Combining this with the q/(MN) and q/N identities yields Delta_i'=Delta_i for every copy, without asserting that its forced-zero threshold itself is unchanged.
