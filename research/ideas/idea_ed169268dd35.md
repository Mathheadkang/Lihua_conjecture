## A one-sided bound suffices to test spectral-mode stability

Suggested by this chat and saved at the researcher's request on 12 November 2026. Draft supplemental calculation. It uses the source's fixed-positive-correlation population limit stated above; that source has not been imported and approved here. It does not require proving the conjectured exact small-correlation asymptotic.

Use $Q=\bar\Phi$ and let $G,\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_N$ be independent standard normals. Set
$$
T_i=\sqrt{\rho}\,G+\sqrt{1-\rho}\,\epsilon_i,
\qquad 0<\rho<1.
$$
Conditional on $G=g$, the coordinate law has density $f_{\rho,g}$ with
$$
\sup_{y\in\mathbb R}\frac{f_{\rho,g}(y)}{\phi(y)}
=(1-\rho)^{-1/2}e^{g^2/2}.
$$
This follows by completing the square; the maximizing point is $y=g/\sqrt{\rho}$. If $g\le0$ and $y\ge0$, its conditional upper-tail probability is at most $Q(y)$, since $(y-\sqrt{\rho}g)/\sqrt{1-\rho}\ge y$.

For a fixed $0<q<1/2$, a population Simes crossing at a positive threshold requires a conditional-to-marginal tail ratio at least $1/q$. The density bound implies the same bound on every tail ratio. Thus, for $0<\rho<1-q^2$, a crossing requires
$$
g\ge\sqrt{2\log(1/q)+\log(1-\rho)}.
$$
Accordingly the fixed-$\rho$, large-$N$ rejection limit from the cited source is at most
$$
Q\!\left(\sqrt{2\log(1/q)+\log(1-\rho)}\right).
$$
As $\rho\downarrow0$ this upper bound tends to $Q(\sqrt{2\log(1/q)})<q$, whereas the independent finite-model size is exactly $q$. Conditional on the stated population-limit theorem, this already prevents uniformly vanishing absolute acceptance errors when a small common mode is removed; an exact limit formula is unnecessary for that obstruction.

The common covariance component $\rho\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}$ has trace fraction $\rho$. Therefore a spectral-peeling proof cannot assume that deleting a covariance component changes Simes acceptance by at most a universal constant times its trace fraction. [The fixed-level proof program](idea_10f14a1fa91d.md) needs threshold-sensitive control instead. This remains a risk/countercheck to a proof technique, not a counterexample to the unrestricted Simes bound.
