A new analytic screening derivation for the small-coherent-weight secondary transition in [the existing stress tests](idea_57338934f2f7.md), refining [the earlier risk](idea_3ac09cc6cec1.md). It is a DRAFT observation requiring independent verification, not an approved lemma, not a native confirmed counterexample, and not a falsification of (T), either two-level target assertion, (S), or BH.

## Finite family and proposed divergence
For z>=1 put rho=S(z), r=sqrt(rho), s=sqrt(1-rho). For every finite K>=2, use the equal-weight unit vectors x_j=r e_0+s e_j, j=1,...,K. This is the two-level model b1=1,b2=K,a=rho, with no assumptions altered. Let v_K=sqrt(2log K), u_K=v_K-z (positive for large K), and w_K=u_K/s. Define D,J,m exactly as in the planted-SNR program. Proposed conclusion:
lim_{K->infinity} J_cluster(w_K)D_cluster(w_K)/D_cluster'(w_K)^2
= rho [rho+(1-rho)J_z]/[2{rho+(1-rho)S(z)}^2],
where J_z=z phi(z)+S(z)+phi(z)^2/Phi(z).
With rho=S(z), this is at least (1-rho)z^2/[2(2-rho)^2], which tends to infinity as z->infinity. Also D_cluster(w_K)->infinity, so the condition D>=1 in (K) is met. If checked, this refutes ONLY the proposed universal inequality (K).

## Exact finite-K product identities
The common channel coordinate is a known shift r w+G_0, independent of the REM channel u X+G on the K basis vectors with u=s w. Therefore
D_cluster(w)=rho w^2/2+D_REM(s w),
m_cluster(w)=rho+(1-rho)m_REM(s w),
J_cluster(w)=rho+(1-rho)J_REM(s w).
The score contributions are rG_0 and s times the REM score, independent and centered. Entropy factors add. For any finite prior differentiation under the planted integral gives D'(w)=E s_w(Y)+E[gradient_y log L_w(Y).X]=w E||E[X|Y]||^2=w m(w): E s_w=0, gradient_y logL=w E[X|Y], and the last identity is conditional expectation. Domination is elementary for each finite K and fixed w window.

## REM critical-window limits, with details to check
Under the planted REM law, by permutation symmetry choose planted label 1. Then Y_1=u_K+G_1, Y_j=G_j for j>=2. At that observation write
A_K=K^{-1} exp(u_K^2/2+u_K G_1),
B_K=K^{-1} sum_{j>=2}exp(u_K G_j-u_K^2/2).
The posterior mass of the planted label is A_K/(A_K+B_K). If G_1>z then A_K->infinity; if G_1<z then A_K->0, since log A_K=v_K(G_1-z)+z^2/2-zG_1.

Truncate each unplanted G_j at v_K. The probability any exceeds v_K is <=K S(v_K)->0. The truncated B_K has expectation (K-1)Phi(z)/K and variance tending to zero. Indeed its second moment divided by K is
(2pi)^(-1/2) exp(-z^2) int_0^infinity exp(-(v_K-2z)t-t^2/2)dt=O_z(1/v_K).
For the fixed-observation derivative
B'_K=K^{-1}sum_{j>=2}(G_j-u_K)exp(u_KG_j-u_K^2/2),
the truncated expectation is -(K-1)phi(z)/K and its variance is bounded by
(2pi)^(-1/2)exp(-z^2) int_0^infinity (z-t)^2 exp(-(v_K-2z)t-t^2/2)dt=O_z(1/v_K).
Thus B_K->Phi(z), B'_K->-phi(z) in probability for each fixed z as K->infinity. These are the same endpoint second-moment substitutions as the approved iid critical-truncation argument; they do not invoke an unverified REM thermodynamic formula.

It follows that m_REM(u_K)=E_planted[A_K/(A_K+B_K)]->S(z), using conditional-expectation symmetry. The REM score (A_K G_1+B'_K)/(A_K+B_K) converges in probability to G_1 on {G_1>z} and to -phi(z)/Phi(z) on {G_1<z}. Its fourth moment is bounded by3, by conditional Jensen applied to the complete-data score <X,G>~N(0,1), so squared scores are uniformly integrable. Therefore
J_REM(u_K)->E[G^2 1{G>z}]+phi(z)^2/Phi(z)=J_z.

Finally D_REM(u_K)=O_z(v_K), sufficient to make D_REM(u_K)/w_K^2->0: log(A_K+B_K)<=log(1+A_K)+log(1+B_K); E log(1+B_K)<=log2; and log(1+A_K)<=log2+(log A_K)_+, whose expectation is O_z(v_K). Nonnegativity of KL supplies the other side.

## Ratio and quantifiers
For fixed z, w_K->infinity, D_cluster/w_K^2->rho/2, m_cluster->rho+(1-rho)S(z), and the displayed J limit follows from independence. The limit ratio above follows without exchanging the z and K limits. Since phi(z)>=z S(z), J_z>=z^2 S(z), giving the stated quadratic lower bound when rho=S(z). For any proposed universal constant choose z large, then a FINITE K sufficiently large; hence this is an admissible finite-prior family if each calculation is verified. It is not a single finite excess or an appeal to an infinite-dimensional prior. Explicit certified values of K(z) have not been supplied.
