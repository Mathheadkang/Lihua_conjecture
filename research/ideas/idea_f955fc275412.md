Unproved refinement of idea_7cf267513eef, not a counterexample or a proved asymptotic. In the common-gate two-sided family, consider the endpoint conditional tail ratio h(s,b)=exp(-lambda*s^2/2)[cosh(bs)+cosh((alpha+b)s)]/2. At b=-alpha/2 it reduces to exp(-lambda*s^2/2)cosh(alpha*s/2), and this is pointwise minimal in b. A possible stress test tunes lambda and/or alpha so sup_{s>0} exp(-lambda*s^2/2)cosh(alpha*s/2)=1/q, producing an endpoint double tangency in the shared factor b (if parameters can be chosen within the family). For the preceding null-noise path, h_r(s,b)=[exp(-r^2*lambda*s^2/2)cosh(r*b*s)+exp(-lambda*s^2/2)cosh((alpha+b)*s)]/2. Check the actual sign of the r perturbation; it must not be guessed. If a disappearing conditional no-rejection interval produces a square-root gate cusp, a covariance derivative transverse to the path might become large although the physical FDR change stays bounded. This could test whether the integrated cross derivative has an unbounded negative part. Critical cautions: neither existence/sign of such cusp nor its finite-array score asymptotics have been established; the sign needed for the negative part may be opposite; integration in r and finite-n Gaussian smoothing can cancel the singularity; the relevant BH threshold is the largest qualifying rank and must be controlled throughout. The original family may fix alpha from q, so any retuning must be explicitly separated from the mandatory fixed-parameter witness test. This is only an optional falsification direction after the exact cost reduction.
