# Definition (mixtures and iid-generated Gram laws in Panchenko)

Let $M=\mathbb R^{\mathbb N\times\mathbb N}$, where $\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, with the product topology and Borel sigma-algebra $\mathcal M$. Let $\mathcal P$ denote the probability measures on $(M,\mathcal M)$.

A law $\mathbb P\in\mathcal P$ is a mixture of laws $\mathbb Q(u,\cdot)$ if there are a probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathrm{Pr})$ and a probability kernel $\mathbb Q:\Omega\times\mathcal M\to[0,1]$ such that $\mathbb Q(u,\cdot)\in\mathcal P$ for every $u$, $\mathbb Q(\cdot,A)$ is $\mathcal F$-measurable for every $A\in\mathcal M$, and
$$
\mathbb P(A)=\int_\Omega\mathbb Q(u,A)\,d\mathrm{Pr}(u)
\qquad(A\in\mathcal M).
$$

A law of a [Gram-de Finetti matrix in Panchenko](lib_panchenko2009_gram_de_finetti_matrix.md) is generated by an iid sample if it is the distribution of
$$
\bigl(\langle h_l,h_{l'}\rangle+a_l\delta_{ll'}\bigr)_{l,l'\ge1},
$$
where $(h_l,a_l)_{l\ge1}$ is an iid sequence with a Borel probability law $\eta$ on $\ell^2\times[0,\infty)$. Here $\ell^2$ is the separable real Hilbert space of square-summable sequences, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ its inner product, and $\delta_{ll'}=1$ if $l=l'$ and $0$ otherwise. Thus $a_l$ is a nonnegative diagonal correction; $\eta$ need not be a product measure and $h_l,a_l$ may be dependent within a sampled pair.

The iid representation is a property of each individual component law. A measurable choice of its representing $\eta$ as the outer mixture variable varies is not part of this definition.
