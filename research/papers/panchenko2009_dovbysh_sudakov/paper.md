# On the Dovbysh-Sudakov representation result

Dmitry Panchenko. arXiv:0905.1524v1 [math.PR], 11 May 2009.

## 1 Introduction: definitions and equations (1.1)-(1.2)

An infinite real random array $R=(R_{ll'})_{l,l'\ge1}$ is a Gram-de Finetti matrix when it is symmetric, all its finite principal submatrices are nonnegative definite, and their laws are unchanged by simultaneously permuting the row and column indices. These are finite-dimensional distribution conditions. In this source, “positive definite” includes nonnegative definite matrices.

Let $M=\mathbb R^{\mathbb N\times\mathbb N}$ with the product topology, let $\mathcal M$ be its Borel sigma-algebra, and let $\mathcal P$ be the probability measures on it. A law $\mathbb P\in\mathcal P$ is a mixture of laws $\mathbb Q(u,\cdot)$ if a probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathrm{Pr})$ and a probability kernel $\mathbb Q$ satisfy, for every $A\in\mathcal M$,
$$
\mathbb P(A)=\int_\Omega\mathbb Q(u,A)\,d\mathrm{Pr}(u). \tag{1.1}
$$
A law is generated by an iid sample if, for a probability measure $\eta$ on the real Hilbert space $\ell^2$ times $\mathbb R^+=[0,\infty)$, it is the law of
$$
\bigl(h_l\cdot h_{l'}+a_l\delta_{ll'}\bigr)_{l,l'\ge1}. \tag{1.2}
$$

Here $(h_l,a_l)_{l\ge1}$ is iid with law $\eta$; the dot is the Hilbert inner product and $\delta_{ll'}$ is the Kronecker delta.

## Proposition 1

Every law of a Gram-de Finetti matrix has a mixture representation (1.1) in which each component law $\mathbb Q(u,\cdot)$ is generated by an iid sample in the sense of (1.2).

The source states the theorem for general real-valued arrays, including unbounded arrays; no moment assumption is made here. Its proof treats bounded arrays in Section 2 and the unbounded extension in Section 3. The nonnegative correction in (1.2) is also confirmed by the construction $a_l=t_l-\|h_l\|^2$ immediately before Lemma 3 on printed p. 6. Proof details are not imported.
