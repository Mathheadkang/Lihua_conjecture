## Goal

Target: [Compact all-null exchangeable arrays carry the Schur-square path and attain the loaded Simes supremum](../assets/gaussian_simes_schur_array_compactification.md).

Prove the six parts using only: Prokhorov compactness on a compact metrizable array space; the Schur product theorem; the identification of the all-null tagged BH FDP with the Simes rejection indicator, so that part 2 is the all-null restriction of part 1 of [the approved array compactification](../assets/gaussian_bh_exchangeable_array_variational_compactification.md); [the positive-Gaussian Simes theorem](../library/chi2024_negative_dependence/assets/lib_chi2024_positive_gaussian_simes.md) applied conditionally for part 3; and one elementary tool used in parts 4 and 6. That tool says the Simes rejection event depends on the observation only through finitely many marginal threshold comparisons, whose boundaries are Gaussian-null hyperplanes because every marginal is standard normal, singular covariance included.

## Plan

1. [x] **Threshold form of the Simes event (finite $N$).** For $p\in[0,1]^N$, the event $\{\exists k\le N:p_{(k)}\le qk/N\}$ equals $\{\exists c\in\{1,\ldots,N\}:\#\{i:p_i\le qc/N\}\ge c\}$. Proof: if $p_{(k)}\le qk/N$, then at least $k$ coordinates are $\le qk/N$; conversely, if $\#\{i:p_i\le qc/N\}\ge c$, then $p_{(c)}\le qc/N$. With $p_i=\overline\Phi(T_i)$ and $t_c=\overline\Phi^{-1}(qc/N)$, each set $\{p_i\le qc/N\}$ is $\{T_i\ge t_c\}$. So the indicator $\chi_N(T)$ of the event is locally constant at every $T\in\mathbb R^N$ outside the finite union $H_N=\bigcup_{i,c}\{T_i=t_c\}$.

2. [x] **Continuity of $F_q$ on $\mathcal C_N$, singular allowed.** Let $\Gamma_k\to\Gamma$ in $\mathcal C_N$, and set $T_k=\Gamma_k^{1/2}g$ and $T=\Gamma^{1/2}g$ with a single $g\sim\mathcal N_N(0,I_N)$. The PSD square root is continuous on the PSD cone, so $T_k\to T$ surely. Since $\Gamma_{ii}=1$, each $T_i\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$, so $\mathbb P(T\in H_N)=0$. By step 1, $\chi_N(T_k)\to\chi_N(T)$ almost surely, and bounded convergence gives $F_q(\Gamma_k)\to F_q(\Gamma)$.

3. [x] **Part 1, compactness.** Let $\Omega_0=[-1,1]^{\{(i,j):i<j\}}$, with the arrays symmetrized and $C_{ii}=1$; this is a compact metrizable product space. The all-null laws in $\mathfrak P_0$ are fixed-mark laws on the closed subset $K_0\subset\Omega_0$ of arrays whose finite principal matrices are all PSD. (PSD of each finite principal matrix is a closed condition depending on finitely many coordinates.) Joint exchangeability is closed under weak limits, because $\int h\circ\pi\,dP=\int h\,dP$ for bounded continuous $h$ and each finitary permutation $\pi$, a continuous map. The all-null condition is closed: the mark coordinates are fixed, and the mark space $E_{\rm mark}$ carries a product topology with discrete tags, so $\{\theta_i=0,\ell_i=1\ \forall i\}$ is closed. Prokhorov's theorem on a compact metrizable space gives compactness of $\mathfrak P_0$, a closed subset of the compact probability space.

4. [x] **Part 1, path invariance and joint continuity.** The array map $(t,\omega)\mapsto S_t\omega$ is continuous on $[0,1]\times K_0$, since it acts coordinatewise by the polynomial $g_t$. It maps $K_0$ into $K_0$: $g_t([-1,1])\subset[-1,1]$, $g_t(1)=1$ keeps the unit diagonal, and on each finite principal matrix $C$ it gives $(1-t)C+t(C\circ C)$, which is PSD by the Schur product theorem and convexity. $S_t$ commutes with every finitary relabeling, so it preserves joint exchangeability. It keeps the marks, so it preserves all-nullness, and $S_0=\mathrm{id}$. For joint continuity, take $t_k\to t$ and $P_k\to P$, and bounded continuous $h$ on $K_0$. Then $h\circ S_\cdot$ is uniformly continuous on the compact set $[0,1]\times K_0$, so
$|\int h\circ S_{t_k}dP_k-\int h\circ S_tdP|\le\sup_\omega|h(S_{t_k}\omega)-h(S_t\omega)|+|\int h\circ S_t\,dP_k-\int h\circ S_t\,dP|\to0$.

5. [x] **Part 2.** Every $P\in\mathfrak P_0$ lies in $\mathfrak P$, because the mark $(0,1)\in E_{\rm mark}$. Conditional on $\omega$ with all $\theta_i=0$ and $\ell_i=1$, the tagged FDP is $D_n=R_n/(R_n\vee1)=\mathbf 1\{R_n\ge1\}$. Also $R_n\ge1$ iff $\widehat k_q>0$ iff $\exists k:p_{(k)}\le qk/n$, and $T^{(n)}\mid\omega\sim\mathcal N_n(0,M_n(\omega))$ with $M_n=(1-\sigma^2)C_{1:n,1:n}+\sigma^2\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}$. Hence $\mathbb E[D_n\mid\omega]=F_q(M_n(\omega))$ and $\mathcal F_n(P;q,\sigma)=\Phi_{n,\sigma}(P;q)$ on $\mathfrak P_0$. Since $\sigma\in(0,1)$ and $q\le1/4<1/2$, part 1 of the approved compactification gives the existence of the limit, uniform convergence over $\mathfrak P\supset\mathfrak P_0$, and continuity of $\mathcal F(\cdot;q,\sigma)$. Restriction to the subspace $\mathfrak P_0$ keeps all three, with $\Phi_\sigma=\mathcal F|_{\mathfrak P_0}$. Continuity of $f^\sigma_{P,q}$ is the composition of $t\mapsto S_tP$ (step 4) with $\Phi_\sigma$.

6. [x] **Part 3.** Fix $P\in\mathfrak P_0$, $n$, and $\omega$ in the support of $S_1P$. The covariance $M=(1-\sigma^2)C_{1:n,1:n}+\sigma^2\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}$ is in $\mathcal C_n$. Its off-diagonal entries are $(1-\sigma^2)C_{ij}+\sigma^2\ge0$, because $S_1$-arrays have $C_{ij}=c^2\ge0$ for $i\ne j$ (step 4 with $t=1$, where $g_1(x)=x^2$). With $Y=-T\sim\mathcal N_n(0,M)$, $p_i=\overline\Phi(T_i)=\Phi(-T_i)$ is a common decreasing transform of $Y_i$ with uniform marginals. So $p\in\mathcal G_M\cap\mathcal U^n$, and $\{\exists k:p_{(k)}\le qk/n\}=\{S_n(p)\le q\}$, with $S_n$ the Simes function $\min_k(n/k)p_{(k)}$. The library theorem gives $F_q(M)\le q$. Averaging over $\omega$ gives $\Phi_{n,\sigma}(S_1P;q)\le q$ for all $n$; let $n\to\infty$ by step 5.

7. [x] **Part 4, embedding.** For $\Sigma\in\mathcal C_N$, the array $C_{ab}=\Sigma_{I_aI_b}$ has unit diagonal and entries in $[-1,1]$. Each principal matrix $(\Sigma_{I_aI_b})_{a,b\le n}=E^{\mathsf T}\Sigma E$ is PSD, where $E$ is the $N\times n$ selection matrix with columns $e_{I_a}$. Since the $I_a$ are iid, $P_\Sigma$ is jointly exchangeable and all-null, so $P_\Sigma\in\mathfrak P_0$. For $a\ne b$, $g_t(\Sigma_{I_aI_b})=(R^\Sigma_t)_{I_aI_b}$, including the tie case $I_a=I_b$, where both sides equal $1$ because $g_t(1)=1$. Also $R^\Sigma_t\in\mathcal C_N$ (step 4 applied to $\Sigma$). Hence $S_tP_\Sigma=P_{R^\Sigma_t}$.

8. [x] **Part 4, value of the embedded functional.** For $\Sigma'\in\mathcal C_N$, put $\Gamma=(1-\sigma^2)\Sigma'+\sigma^2\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\in\mathcal C_N$. Conditional on $I=(I_a)$, $M_n=(\Gamma_{I_aI_b})_{a,b\le n}$; the diagonal and tie entries both equal $1$. So $T^{(n)}\overset d=(Y_{I_a})_{a\le n}$ with $Y\sim\mathcal N_N(0,\Gamma)$ independent of $I$. Let $m^{(n)}_j=n^{-1}\#\{a\le n:I_a=j\}$ and $\pi_i=\overline\Phi(Y_i)$. The size-$n$ Simes event equals $E(m^{(n)},\pi)=\{\exists j:m^{(n)}_j>0,\ L_j(m^{(n)},\pi)\ge0\}$, where $L_j(m,\pi)=\sum_im_i\mathbf1\{\pi_i\le\pi_j\}-\pi_j/q$. Proof: if $p_{(k)}\le qk/n$ and $p_{(k)}=\pi_j$, then $\#\{a:p_a\le\pi_j\}\ge k\ge n\pi_j/q$. Conversely, $c=nG(\pi_j)\ge n\pi_j/q$ with $c\ge1$ gives $p_{(c)}\le\pi_j\le qc/n$. With uniform weights $u=(1/N,\ldots,1/N)$ the same argument shows $E(u,\pi)$ is the size-$N$ Simes event for $Y$. For fixed $\pi$, each $L_j$ is linear in $m$. So $\mathbf1_{E(m,\pi)}$ is constant on a neighborhood of $u$ unless $L_j(u,\pi)=0$ for some $j$, i.e. $\pi_j=qc/N$ for some integer $c\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$. That set is a finite union of marginal hyperplanes $\{Y_j=t_c\}$, which is null. The strong law gives $m^{(n)}\to u$ almost surely, independently of $Y$. Bounded convergence yields $\Phi_{n,\sigma}(P_{\Sigma'};q)\to F_q(\Gamma)$. Applying this with $\Sigma'=R^\Sigma_t$ and step 7 gives the displayed formula for $f^\sigma_{P_\Sigma,q}(t)$.

9. [x] **Part 5.** $\Phi_\sigma$ is continuous on the compact set $\mathfrak P_0$ (steps 3, 5), so its maximum is attained. Lower bound: step 8 at $t=0$ gives $\Phi_\sigma(P_\Sigma;q)=F_q((1-\sigma^2)\Sigma+\sigma^2\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T})$ for every $N$ and $\Sigma$, so $\max\ge B^\sigma(q)$. Upper bound: for $P$-almost every $\omega$, $C_{1:n,1:n}\in\mathcal C_n$, so $F_q(M_n(\omega))\le B^\sigma(q)$ by definition. Hence $\Phi_{n,\sigma}(P;q)\le B^\sigma(q)$ for all $n$, and so $\Phi_\sigma(P;q)\le B^\sigma(q)$.

10. [x] **Part 6.** Inclusion: $(1-\sigma^2)\Sigma+\sigma^2\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}$ is a convex combination of two elements of $\mathcal C_N$, so $B^\sigma(q)\le B(q)$, hence $\limsup_{\sigma\downarrow0}B^\sigma(q)\le B(q)$. For each fixed $N$ and $\Sigma\in\mathcal C_N$, step 2 gives $F_q((1-\sigma^2)\Sigma+\sigma^2\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T})\to F_q(\Sigma)$ as $\sigma\downarrow0$, so $\liminf_{\sigma\downarrow0}B^\sigma(q)\ge F_q(\Sigma)$. Take the supremum over $N$ and $\Sigma$. No uniformity in $N$ is needed, because the supremum is approached by one fixed finite model at a time.
