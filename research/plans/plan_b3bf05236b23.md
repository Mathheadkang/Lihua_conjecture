## Plan

1. [x] **Set up measurable all-scale envelopes and handle the scalar case.** Write $\phi(x)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/2}$ and $\overline\Phi(x)=\int_x^\infty\phi(t)\,dt$. Prove the elementary Mills bound $\overline\Phi(x)\le\phi(x)/x$ for $x>0$ directly by integrating $t\phi(t)$. For every fixed finite sample, $w\mapsto L_w$ is continuous and tends to zero at infinity. Consequently its supremum over $[0,\infty)$ equals its supremum over nonnegative rationals, is measurable and finite, and has a maximizer. For $K=1$, calculate $\Lambda=e^{(Z^+)^2/2}$ and, with $v_c=\sqrt{2\log c}$,
   $$
   \mathbb E\min(c,\Lambda)=\tfrac12+\frac{v_c}{\sqrt{2\pi}}+c\overline\Phi(v_c),\qquad
   \mathbb P(\Lambda\ge\alpha)=\overline\Phi(\sqrt{2\log\alpha}).
   $$
   Check these meet the stated working constants for all $c,\alpha\ge2$.

2. [x] **Make the critical split for $K\ge2$.** Put $u=\sqrt{2\log K}$, and define
   $$
   f_i(w)=K^{-1}e^{wZ_i-w^2/2}\mathbf1_{\{Z_i\le u\}},\quad B(w)=\sum_{i=1}^K f_i(w),\quad F=\sup_{w\ge0}B(w),
   $$
   $$
   X_i=K^{-1}e^{Z_i^2/2}\mathbf1_{\{Z_i>u\}},\qquad R=\sum_{i=1}^K X_i.
   $$
   Verify pointwise $0\le f_i(w)\le1$ for every $w\ge0$ by optimizing the scalar exponential and using $Z_i\le u$. Verify $\Lambda\le F+R$, $F\le K$, $B(0)\le1$, continuity and vanishing at infinity of $B$, and the rational-supremum measurability of $F$ and every deletion envelope. If all retained coordinates are absent, $B\equiv0$; otherwise it has an attained maximum. Nothing here truncates the maximizing scale $w$.

3. [x] **FIRST QUANTITATIVE FAILURE GATE: pay for the whole collective field.** For $Q_w=(Z-w)e^{wZ-w^2/2}\mathbf1_{\{Z\le u\}}$, derive by Gaussian tilting and direct differentiation
   $$
   \mathbb E Q_w=-\phi(u-w)\le0,\qquad
   \mathbb E(B'(w))_+\le\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(B'(w))}\le\sqrt{\mathbb E Q_w^2/K}.
   $$
   Independence is essential in the variance identity; justify each fixed-$w$ expectation before integrating. On $0\le w\le u/2$, prove
   $$
   \mathbb E Q_w^2\le(1+w^2)e^{w^2},\qquad
   \int_0^{u/2}\sqrt{\mathbb E Q_w^2/K}\,dw
   \le\left(\frac u2+\frac{u^2}{8}\right)e^{-u^2/8}\le2.
   $$
   For $w\ge u/2$, perform the substitution $Z=u-s$ without dropping the factor $K^{-1}$, and establish the EXACT identity
   $$
   \frac{\mathbb E Q_w^2}{K}=\frac{e^{-(w-u)^2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
   \int_0^\infty(u-w-s)^2e^{-(2w-u)s-s^2/2}\,ds.
   $$
   Since $2w-u\ge0$, use $(u-w-s)^2\le2(u-w)^2+2s^2$ and the two half-Gaussian integrals to bound this by $((w-u)^2+1)e^{-(w-u)^2}$. Integrate its square root over $w\ge u/2$ by enlarging the shifted domain to $\mathbb R$; $\sqrt{t^2+1}\le|t|+1$ gives the bound $2+\sqrt{2\pi}$. For every finite $T$, use the fundamental theorem of calculus to get $\sup_{0\le w\le T}B(w)\le B(0)+\int_0^T(B'(w))_+dw$; then use Tonelli and monotone convergence in $T$. Conclude
   $$
   \mathbb EF\le1+2+2+\sqrt{2\pi}<8.
   $$
   **Stop at the first invalid identity or nonuniform integral in this step and record the exact obstruction.** Do not replace this calculation by a fixed-time estimate, a finite grid without its error, a single-leader asymptotic, or an assumed collective bound. Later steps are conditional on this gate actually being proved.

4. [x] **Recover the sharp truncated-moment coefficient directly.** Use, for nonnegative summands,
   $$
   \min(c,\Lambda)\le F+\min(c,R)\le F+\sum_{i=1}^K\min(c,X_i).
   $$
   With $v=\sqrt{2\log(Kc)}>u$, compute the Gaussian integral EXACTLY:
   $$
   K\mathbb E\min(c,X_1)=\frac{v-u}{\sqrt{2\pi}}+cK\overline\Phi(v)
   =\frac{\sqrt{\log(Kc)}-\sqrt{\log K}}{\sqrt\pi}+cK\overline\Phi(v).
   $$
   The integral starts at $u$, since $X_1$ is zero below $u$; do not restore the removed interval. Check $cK\overline\Phi(v)\le1/(\sqrt{2\pi}v)\le1$ for $K,c\ge2$, and $\sqrt{a+b}-\sqrt a\le\sqrt b$ for $a,b\ge0$. Together with Step 3 this gives $\mathbb E\min(c,\Lambda)\le\sqrt{\log c/\pi}+9$, uniformly in all parameters.

5. [x] **Prove the deletion variance estimate inline.** Let $F_{-i}=\sup_{w\ge0}\sum_{j\ne i}f_j(w)$ and $d_i=F-F_{-i}$. At a COMMON maximizer $w_*$ of $B$, show $0\le d_i\le f_i(w_*)\le1$ and $\sum_i d_i\le F$, hence $\sum_i d_i^2\le F$. No measurable maximizer selection is needed for these pointwise inequalities. Supply the elementary independent-coordinate variance tensorization proof: induct on $K$ using the conditional variance decomposition for the first coordinate, and bound each conditional variance of an averaged function by the average conditional variance using Jensen (equivalently, use its independent-copy square-difference formula). This proves
   $$
   \operatorname{Var}(H)\le\sum_i\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(H\mid Z_{-i})
   $$
   for square-integrable $H$. Since $F_{-i}$ is a function of $Z_{-i}$ alone,
   $$
   \operatorname{Var}(F)\le\sum_i\mathbb E(F-F_{-i})^2\le\mathbb EF\le8.
   $$
   Justify square integrability first by $F\le K$. For $\alpha\ge32$, $\alpha/2-\mathbb EF\ge\alpha/4$ and Chebyshev yields $\mathbb P(F\ge\alpha/2)\le128/\alpha^2$. No external Efron--Stein statement or unverified source is being assumed.

6. [x] **Use the conditional rare-count law instead of an unconditional scalar-envelope mean.** Set $p=\overline\Phi(u)>0$, $M=\sum_i\mathbf1_{\{Z_i>u\}}$, and $\lambda=Kp$. By Mills and $K\phi(u)=1/\sqrt{2\pi}$, check $\lambda\le1/(\sqrt{2\pi}u)\le1$ for every $K\ge2$. Thus $M$ is binomial with parameters $K,p$. Prove, by first conditioning on the exact exceedance-index set and then using symmetry, that conditional on $M=m$ the $m$ positive marks are independent with the law of $K^{-1}e^{Z^2/2}$ given $Z>u$ (their sum is insensitive to the index ordering).

7. [x] **Quantify both count regimes for every sufficiently large tail threshold.** Work with $\alpha\ge32$ (the calculation below already works for $\alpha\ge16$). The case $m=0$ contributes zero. For $1\le m\le\sqrt\alpha$, put $t=\alpha/(2m)\ge2$ and $v=\sqrt{2\log(Kt)}>u$. If $R\ge\alpha/2$, at least one positive mark is at least $t$. The conditional union bound and Mills therefore give
   $$
   \mathbb P(R\ge\alpha/2\mid M=m)\le\frac{m\overline\Phi(v)}p
   \le\frac{2m^2}{Kp\,\alpha\sqrt{2\pi\log\alpha}},
   $$
   because $Kt=K\alpha/(2m)\ge\sqrt\alpha$ and $v\ge\sqrt{\log\alpha}$. Keep the possible bound greater than one: averaging it is still valid. Prove $\mathbb EM^2/(Kp)=1+(K-1)p\le2$, so the entire low-count contribution is at most $4/(\sqrt{2\pi}\alpha\sqrt{\log\alpha})<2/(\alpha\sqrt{\log\alpha})$. For $M>\sqrt\alpha$, expand the fourth raw moment using the falling factorial $(M)_j=M(M-1)\cdots(M-j+1)$:
   $$
   \mathbb EM^4=\mathbb E(M)_4+6\mathbb E(M)_3+7\mathbb E(M)_2+\mathbb EM
   \le\lambda^4+6\lambda^3+7\lambda^2+\lambda\le15.
   $$
   Verify $\mathbb E(M)_j=(K)_jp^j$ also when $j>K$ (both are zero), and use Markov to get $\mathbb P(M>\sqrt\alpha)\le15/\alpha^2$. Conclude $\mathbb P(R\ge\alpha/2)\le2/(\alpha\sqrt{\log\alpha})+15/\alpha^2$.

8. [x] **Assemble the exact maximal tail and check the finite ranges.** Since $\Lambda\le F+R$, for $K\ge2$ and $\alpha\ge32$ combine Steps 5 and 7 to get
   $$
   \mathbb P(\Lambda\ge\alpha)\le\frac{2}{\alpha\sqrt{\log\alpha}}+\frac{143}{\alpha^2}
   \le\frac{145}{\alpha\sqrt{\log\alpha}},
   $$
   explicitly verifying $\sqrt{\log\alpha}/\alpha\le1$. For $2\le\alpha<32$, use the probability bound one and $\alpha\sqrt{\log\alpha}\le32\sqrt{\log32}<150$. Combine with the scalar calculation in Step 1 to obtain $C_{\mathrm{tail}}=150$ for all $K$ and all real $\alpha\ge2$. Recheck there was no hidden relation between $K$ and $\alpha$, nor between $K$ and $c$.
