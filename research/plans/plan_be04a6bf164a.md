## Plan

1. [x] **Construct coherent Gaussian lines before making simultaneous assertions.** For $v\ne0$ put $e=v/\|v\|$, $\eta=X(e)$, and $Y(w)=X(w)-\eta\langle w,e\rangle$ on the approved jointly measurable series representative. Prove by finite-dimensional Gaussian approximation that $\eta$ is standard normal and independent of the orthogonal Gaussian field generated by $Y$. Fubini gives a full-measure set of orthogonal backgrounds on which $Y(w)$ is finite for $\pi$-almost every $x$. On each such background define the whole line by $Y(w)+y\langle w,e\rangle$ for every real $y$; translation by $tv$ changes $y$ to $y+t\|v\|$. This supplies a single measurable field for every line parameter, rather than intersecting uncountably many null-set complements. Prove agreement with the approved representative for Gaussian-almost every background and, for each fixed direction, invariance of the resulting line functionals under a representative change. Do not take a samplewise supremum of $X$ on a Hilbert ball. Treat $v=0$ as a constant line.

2. [x] **Identify actual marked ranks, selected masses, and measurability.** On $\mu=\pi\otimes\gamma$ use exactly the score $T_t$ from the target. Since $c\ge s$, its conditional tail is continuous, strictly decreasing, and takes values strictly between zero and one at every finite threshold. For every line parameter and common score $z$, prove that the qualifying-rank set is closed away from zero, that a positive supremum is attained, and that rank one is impossible at finite $z$. If the tail at a positive largest rank were strictly larger than that rank, continuity would give a larger qualifying rank; hence $\mu(\mathcal R_t)=R_t$. Include the empty-set convention at zero and the extended rank-one convention. Measurability must not rely on testing only rational qualifying ranks, which could miss an isolated contact: for each $r>0$, use the minimum of the continuous barrier on $[r,1)$, localized away from one, to express $\{R_t\ge r\}$ measurably. Alternatively use compact suprema of the continuous qualifying function. Obtain $0\le\nu_t\le R_t$ and identify $D_t=f_{t,X}(R_t)$ from the mass identity.

3. [x] **Prove the first gate on the common particle/private-mark space.** For two score fields $T\le T'$ almost everywhere, their qualifying tails are ordered, so their globally largest ranks satisfy $R\le R'$. At positive rank, $u_q(R')\le u_q(R)$; consequently each point selected by $T$ is selected by $T'$. Include zero rank explicitly. Apply this deterministic implication to $z_1\le z_2$ to prove nesting, and integrate both $1$ and the fixed tag $\ell$ on the set difference to get $0\le\Delta\nu\le\Delta R$. Next use $|T_t(X,z)-T_u(X,z)|\le K|t-u|$ pointwise to compare with $T_u(X,z\pm h)$, $h=K|t-u|/\sigma$. Establish both set inclusions and both numerical sandwiches for all real $t,u,z$ on each coherent line. Check that the argument uses no sign restriction on $b_v$ or homogeneity of the tags, so it covers mixed labels and several loading directions. Stop if this marked-set implication fails; a rank-only comparison cannot replace it.

4. [x] **Obtain the stated whole-payoff Lipschitz constant.** On the convex triangle $0\le n\le r\le1$, bound the partial derivatives of $g_a$ where $r>a$ by $|\partial_n g_a|\le1/a$ and $|\partial_r g_a|\le\|\chi_a'\|_\infty+1/a=W_a$. Use its continuous zero extension across $r=a$ and integration on segments to obtain the target two-coordinate bound. For every bounded nondecreasing $R(z)\in[0,1]$, prove the translation estimate by its Lebesgue-Stieltjes measure: Tonelli gives $\mathbb E[R(Z+h)-R(Z-h)]\le 2h\|\phi\|_\infty\operatorname{Var}(R)\le2h/\sqrt{2\pi}$. Endpoint values at discontinuities do not affect the Gaussian integral. The sandwiches give pointwise bounds for both $|R_t-R_u|$ and $|\nu_t-\nu_u|$ by this same rank increment. Apply the $g_a$ estimate and integrate to obtain precisely $C_{a,\sigma,\tau}|t-u|\|v\|$. Thus the entire line function is Lipschitz and locally absolutely continuous, independently of $\pi,q,s$ except through the stated parameters.

5. [x] **Prove conditional kernels and quantile calculus for fixed tags, without a lawful-competitor argument.** On an admissible line, differentiate the integrated kernels directly, using $|b_v|\le K$, $k_x\le1/(s\sqrt{2\pi})$, and uniform bounds for Gaussian density derivatives at fixed $s$. Dominated differentiation gives $\dot H=h_b$ and $\dot V=h_{0b}$, jointly continuously on compact threshold/translation sets. Positivity of $h$ and the implicit equation give the exact $\dot Q$ and $\dot f$ in the target, locally uniformly on compact subintervals of $(0,1)$. Derive the global coupling estimate $|Q_t(r)-Q_u(r)|\le K|t-u|$ from ordered tails of bounded score shifts. Also prove
   $$
   |V_t(Q_t(r))-V_u(Q_u(r))|\le\frac{2K|t-u|}{s\sqrt{2\pi}},\qquad
   |\dot Q(r)|\le K,\qquad |\dot f(r)|\le\frac{2K}{r s\sqrt{2\pi}}.
   $$
   Differentiate in rank to get $(V(Q))'=h_0(Q)/h(Q)\in[0,1]$, hence $f\in[0,1]$ and $|f'|\le1/r$. At rank one, prove continuity to unchanged total true mass and $\dot f(1)=0$; at ranks at most $a$, retain the zero cutoff convention. These are conditional analytic shifts of the background only; do not invoke the mean-variation dependency's lawful-domain hypothesis for $b_v$.

6. [x] **Derive the actual-rank survival identity and compact all-active envelope.** A positive rank $\rho<1$ qualifies exactly when $z\ge B_t(\rho)$. Attainment in step 2 shows $R_t\ge r$ iff a qualifying rank lies in $[r,1)$; compact attainment of the suffix infimum then gives $\{R_t\ge r\}=\{z\ge M_t(r)\}$ including non-strict contact. Prove $Q_0(\rho)\to-\infty$ as $\rho\uparrow1$ and $u_q(\rho)\to u_q(1)<\infty$, so $B_0(\rho)\to+\infty$. Since $|B_t-B_0|\le K|t|/\sigma$ on $(0,1)$, all minimizing points for a fixed $r<1$ and small translations lie in one compact $[r,b_r]\subset[r,1)$. This also works uniformly for lower endpoints in a fixed compact subset of $(0,1)$. On that compact use the uniform expansion $B_t=B_0+t(-\dot Q/\sigma)+o(t)$. An upper bound from every baseline active minimizer and a lower bound from convergent subsequences of perturbed minimizers prove
   $$
   \dot M(r)=\min_{\rho\in\mathcal A(r)}[-\dot Q(\rho)]/\sigma.
   $$
   Retain every active point; require no unique minimizer, fold nondegeneracy, or uniform convergence of minimum derivative quotients over $r$.

7. [x] **Supply pointwise survival limits, rank convergence, and endpoint domination.** From step 6 obtain pointwise right differentiation of $S(M_t(r))$ for $a\le r<1$. The global quantile bound gives $|M_t(r)-M_0(r)|\le K|t|/\sigma$, hence
   $$
   \left|\frac{S(M_t(r))-S(M_0(r))}{t}\right|\le\frac K{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}},\qquad |\dot M(r)|\le K/\sigma.
   $$
   Extend survival at one by zero; its quotient there is zero. Dominated convergence proves convergence in $L^1([a,1],dr)$ as well as against every fixed bounded rank weight. Separately, the marked rank sandwiches and monotonicity in $z$ prove $R_t(X,z)\to R_0(X,z)$ at every continuity point of $z\mapsto R_0(X,z)$; its discontinuity set is countable and Gaussian-null. These statements establish the needed convergence without assuming differentiability, Sobolev regularity, or bounded variation of a selector as a function of the Gaussian background.

8. [x] **Derive the complete signed conditional response with explicit fixed-$s$ bounds.** Set $P_t(r)=\chi_a(r)f_t(r)$, extended by zero below $a$, and establish absolute continuity of $P_0$ on $[0,1]$, including its value at one, with
   $$
   w(r)=P_0'(r),\qquad |w(r)|\le\chi_a'(r)+\chi_a(r)/r,\qquad
   \int_a^1|w(r)|dr\le1+\log(1/a).
   $$
   Split the exact conditional payoff difference as
   $$
   \mathbb E_Z[P_t(R_t)-P_0(R_t)]
   +\mathbb E_Z[P_0(R_t)-P_0(R_0)].
   $$
   For the first quotient, compact-uniform fixed-rank differentiation and step 7's rank convergence give pointwise convergence to $\chi_a(R_0)\dot f(R_0)$ when $a<R_0<1$. At $R_0<a$ it is eventually zero; at $R_0=a$ use $\chi_a(R_t)\to0$ and the uniform true-tail quotient bound from step 5. Rank one is Gaussian-null, and its unchanged convention may be kept. The quotient is dominated by $2K/(a s\sqrt{2\pi})$. For the second term use the exact signed layer-cake identity
   $$
   \mathbb E_Z[P_0(R_t)-P_0(R_0)]
   =\int_a^1w(r)[S(M_t(r))-S(M_0(r))]dr.
   $$
   Fubini follows from $w\in L^1$; step 7 permits its differentiation. The two resulting signed terms are exactly the target formula. Their absolute values are bounded separately at fixed $s$ by $2K/(a s\sqrt{2\pi})$ and $K[1+\log(1/a)]/(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})$. Prove joint measurability of the formula by measurable kernels, compact infima, and limits of measurable difference quotients; the derivative of the envelope need not be defined by choosing one measurable minimizer. No discarded dilution term or positive-part estimate may enter the identity.

9. [x] **Identify ordinary directional derivatives for Gaussian-almost every background.** Step 4 gives ordinary differentiability at Lebesgue-almost every coordinate on each admissible Gaussian line. Apply Fubini in the independent orthogonal background and the scalar Gaussian coordinate, whose density is positive, to conclude ordinary differentiability at the random baseline almost surely. At every ordinary differentiability point, step 8's right response equals that ordinary derivative. Define the derivative measurably by two-sided difference-quotient limits on this full-measure set and zero elsewhere. Its bound is the stronger whole-payoff bound $C_{a,\sigma,\tau}\|v\|$ from step 4, rather than the separate fixed-$s$ response estimates. Repeat the conditional argument at each line baseline to identify the response at almost every point on almost every line, with one direction-dependent exceptional set.

10. [x] **Prove weighted one-dimensional Gaussian integration by parts with the correct scaling.** Condition on $Y$, write $F(y)=\mathfrak f_\pi(Y+y e)$ and $J(y)=\zeta(Y+y e)$. Choose coherent versions so that $F$ is bounded Lipschitz, $J$ is bounded $C^1$, and $J'$ is bounded almost everywhere (continuity extends essential bounds along good lines if needed). Their product is locally absolutely continuous and $(JF)'=J'F+JF'$ is integrable against the scalar Gaussian density. On $[-L,L]$ use ordinary absolutely continuous integration by parts and let $L\to\infty$; boundedness gives vanishing Gaussian boundary terms and dominated convergence handles derivative terms. Since $X(v)=\|v\|\eta$, $D_vF=\|v\|F'$ and $D_v\zeta=\|v\|J'$, multiplication by $\|v\|$ gives exactly the weighted identity in the target. Use the bounds on $F,J,D_vF,D_v\zeta$ to justify outer conditioning and absolute integrability. Set $\zeta=1$ for the unweighted identity. For $v=0$, both directional derivatives and $X(v)$ are zero.
