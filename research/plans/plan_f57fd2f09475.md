## Plan

1. [x] **Conditional empirical tails, with an explicit distribution-free gate.** Conditional on the residual Gaussian vector G, the sampled pairs (A_{I_j},ell_{I_j}) are iid from the deterministic finite tagged law. Define H(t)=N^{-1}sum_i 1{A_i>=t}, V(t)=N^{-1}sum_i ell_i 1{A_i>=t}, and their m-sample versions. For each of the two classes, prove symmetrization using an independent ghost index sample and Jensen, obtaining an upper bound twice the expected conditional Rademacher supremum. Given the sampled pairs, order scores decreasingly: the total-tail signed sums are a subset of prefix partial sums of m independent signs. For the tagged class first retain the k true-tagged sampled occurrences, order them decreasingly, and use prefix sums of k independent signs (zero if k=0). Ties merely restrict the available prefixes, so cannot increase either supremum. Prove the L2 maximal inequality E max_{j<=k}|sum_{i<=j}epsilon_i| <= 2 sqrt(k), by the usual stopped-partial-sum argument or a fully proved Doob L2 inequality. Thus each expected supremum is <=4/sqrt(m), and for Delta=max{sup_t|H_m-H|,sup_t|V_m-V|}, E[Delta|G]<=8/sqrt(m). The classes are separable by rational thresholds with one-sided limits; alternatively their differences change only at finitely many observed/full scores. This verifies measurability and includes all atoms. With E_gamma={Delta<=gamma}, establish P(E_gamma^c)<=p_m(gamma):=min{1,8/(gamma sqrt(m))}; E_gamma depends only on G and I, hence is independent of Z. This replaces parameter-coupling costs in the adapted proof's step 4.

2. [x] **Exact finite-grid identification and self-consistency.** For either empirical law with n=N or m, define a continuous-rank crossing by H_n(u_q(r)-sigma z)>=r for 0<r<=1. If r qualifies, k=n H_n(u_q(r)-sigma z) is an integer and k/n>=r. Since u_q is decreasing, k/n is itself qualifying. Conversely every grid crossing is a continuous crossing, and H_n(u_q(k/n)-sigma z)>=k/n is equivalent to p_(k)<=qk/n. Consequently the largest continuous crossing equals the actual largest non-strict ordinary BH rank. At a positive largest grid rank r=k/n, a count j=n H_n(u_q(r)-sigma z)>k would make j/n a larger grid crossing, a contradiction. Hence the total tail at the actual cutoff equals r exactly, and the rejection count is k, with all threshold ties included. Treat r=1 using the same argument (the count is at most n), and no crossing by rank zero and FDP zero. This reproves the finite part of adapted steps 2 and 11 rather than assuming a simple crossing.

3. [x] **Two-way trimmed common-shift transfer.** Use a trimming parameter c in (0,1), distinct from the later FDP denominator cutoff a. Set L_{q,c}=sup_{c/2<=r<=1}q/phi(S^{-1}(qr)) and h=L_{q,c}gamma/sigma, where 0<gamma<c/2. On E_gamma, if r>=c qualifies for either empirical law at z, the mean-value inequality gives u_q(r-gamma)-sigma(z+h)<=u_q(r)-sigma z. The other law's upper tail at the new threshold is therefore >=r-gamma. By step 2 its actual rank at z+h is >=r-gamma. Prove this in both directions, uniformly for every z, including r=1. Denoting full and sampled actual ranks by R(z),S_m(z), deduce the all-z sandwich R(z-h)-c-gamma<=S_m(z)<=R(z+h)+c+gamma, using the trivial bound for source ranks below c. This is adapted step 5 with zero score discrepancy and a uniform empirical-tail discrepancy in place of coupling-mass loss.

4. [x] **Integrated rank bound independent of the number of switches.** Both rank functions are nondecreasing [0,1]-valued functions of z, because all scores increase with the same positive slope sigma. Reprove, as in adapted step 6, that E_Z[f(Z+h)-f(Z-h)]<=2h/sqrt(2pi) for every such f: use level sets {f>y}, each an upper interval, and Tonelli; each shifted-indicator difference is supported on an interval of length <=2h. Step 3 gives on E_gamma, |S_m(z)-R(z)|<=c+gamma+R(z+h)-R(z-h). Conditional integration over Z is legitimate because E_gamma depends only on G,I. On its complement use the bound one. Obtain the uniform gate

   $$
   \mathbb E|\rho_m-\rho_N|\le Q_m(q,\sigma;c,\gamma):=c+\gamma+\frac{2L_{q,c}\gamma}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}+p_m(\gamma).
   $$

   For every fixed q,sigma this bound can be made arbitrarily small, first fixing c, then gamma, then m, and finally making c small. Only ranks have been asserted monotone.

5. [x] **Original mixed-FDP comparison on two positive macroscopic ranks.** Fix 0<a<1/2. On E_gamma and r=R(z),s=S_m(z)>=a, put b_r=u_q(r)-sigma z and b_s=u_q(s)-sigma z. The true-tag tail is dominated by the total tail on every threshold interval, so nesting gives |V(b_r)-V(b_s)|<=|H(b_r)-H(b_s)|. By step 2, H(b_r)=r and H_m(b_s)=s; hence |V(b_r)-V_m(b_s)|<=|r-s|+2gamma. Since V_m(b_s)<=s, subtraction of the two original fractions proves |D_N-D_m|<=(2|r-s|+2gamma)/a. Integrating, and charging at most p_m(gamma) for E_gamma^c, bounds the macroscopic contribution by (2Q_m+2gamma)/a+p_m(gamma). Reprove this algebra rather than importing any monotonicity of mixed FDP; it replaces the random-cutoff band argument of adapted steps 7--8.

6. [x] **Apply the approved microscopic bound in its precise finite scope.** Invoke only clause 2 of the already approved dependency gaussian_bh_common_loading_wasserstein_fdr_continuity, with H=R^d and the deterministic uniform empirical parameter law of the full array. Its means are finite, directions unit, tags in the closed tagged domain, so it belongs to P_1(E); no nonsingularity or residual independence is required. For the sampled array, condition on the complete index list I first. This gives another deterministic finite uniform parameter law satisfying the same conditions and using the same unit directions, including exact duplicate directions and false-tagged zero in the analytic completion. Independence of I from (G,Z) permits applying the bound for each fixed index list and then averaging. Do not condition on G when applying this Gaussian marginal bound. Thus for j=N,m, E[D_j 1{0<rho_j<=b}]<=B_{q,sigma}(b); rank zero contributes zero. The dependency's already proved finiteness of I_z(b)=integral_(0,b] t^{-1}dF_z(t) and I_z(b)->0 at fixed z,sigma are available black boxes, requiring no new library lemma.

7. [x] **Close the one-microscopic-rank gap with two scales.** Cover every rank pair by: both <=2a; both >=a; or minimum <a and maximum >2a. In the first region use |D_m-D_N|<=D_m+D_N and step 6 to charge <=2B_{q,sigma}(2a). The last region forces |rho_m-rho_N|>a, so its expected cost is <=Q_m/a by Markov. Step 5 handles the middle region. Allowing overlaps only enlarges the nonnegative upper bound. Consequently the complete coupled error is at most

   $$
   2B_{q,\sigma}(2a)+(1+3/a)Q_m(q,\sigma;c,\gamma)+2\gamma/a+p_m(\gamma).
   $$

   Verify this gate before claiming convergence. Define e_m(q,sigma) as the minimum of 2 and the infimum of this expression over 0<a<1/2, 0<c<1, 0<gamma<c/2. Every member bounds the same expected error, hence the infimum also does. Given epsilon, choose a to control B, then c small relative to a, then gamma to control the remaining fixed-(a,c) terms, and then m large to control p_m. This proves e_m->0 without incorrectly dividing a rank-trimming remainder a by the same a. This is the quantitative two-scale version of adapted step 9.

8. [x] **Compact-q uniformity at fixed positive common loading.** For K compact in (0,1/2), let q_-=min K>0 and q_+=max K<1/2. At fixed c, continuity of q/phi(S^{-1}(qr)) on K times [c/2,1] gives L_{K,c}<infinity. The dependency's finite microscopic integrals and monotonicity of their integration domain yield

   $$
   \sup_{q\in K}B_{q,\sigma}(2a)\le
   \mathbb E_Z\min\{1,q_+ I_Z(2q_+a)\}\longrightarrow0.
   $$

   Prove this by bounded convergence, since I_z(b)->0 for every fixed z and sigma; here 2q_+a<1. Replace L_{q,c} by L_{K,c} in step 7 and make the same choices of a,c,gamma,m for every q in K. This proves sup_{q in K}e_m(q,sigma)->0. No passage to sigma=0 or uniformity as sigma decreases is used.

9. [x] **Exactly-m-test canonical supremum consequence.** Start with any finite canonical common-loading model. For each deterministic index list, the sampled vector has means theta_{I_j} and covariance tau^2 <v_{I_j},v_{I_k}>+sigma^2, positive semidefinite with diagonal one. Its actual canonical null tags are preserved even at zero means; this claim uses canonical input, unlike the analytic completion allowed earlier. It is an admissible exactly-m-test common-loading model even with repeated indices and singular covariance. Thus E_{G,Z}[D_m|I]<=W_{m,sigma}(q), and the coupled bound gives E D_N<=W_{m,sigma}(q)+e_m(q,sigma). Take the supremum over all finite canonical input arrays; the reverse inequality W_{m,sigma}<=W_sigma is immediate from class inclusion. Do not require a maximizing array or convergence of sampled parameters in Wasserstein distance.
