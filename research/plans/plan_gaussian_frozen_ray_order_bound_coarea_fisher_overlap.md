## Goal

Target: [asset](../assets/gaussian_frozen_ray_order_bound.md).

Prove $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda(Z)\}\leq C_{\mathrm{FR}}\sqrt{\log c}$ for every $c\geq2$, uniformly in $n$, $\Sigma\in\mathcal C_n$ (singular allowed, either sign) and subprobability weights. The statement is not changed.

Idea. Use no window around a random maximizer. Instead, bound $\min\{c,\Lambda\}$ by the total variation of the clipped profile $w\mapsto L_w$ (idea_489cd8d14502). Its signed part integrates to zero, so only $\mathbb E\int|\partial_wL_w|\mathbf 1\{e^2<L_w<c\}\,dw$ is left. Written under the tilted measures $L_w\,d\mathbb P$, this is a first absolute moment of the Fisher score $D_w=\partial_w\log L_w$. Its second moment is the curvature $\kappa_w=-\partial_w^2\log L_w$, by an exact integration by parts in $w$. A Cauchy-Schwarz step with the level-dependent weight $w/\sqrt{\log L_w}$ then reduces everything to first-order quantities. Exact Gaussian integration-by-parts identities turn these into the self-overlap $p^{\mathsf T}\Sigma p$. One of them is an exact telescoping identity: it gives $\int_0^\infty w\,\mathbb E[L_w\,p^{\mathsf T}\Sigma p\,k(\log L_w)]\,dw=|\omega|\int k$ for every level weight $k$. The shared scale $w$ is kept throughout (idea_6d87d51a889d). Spread posteriors, which broke the failed plan, enter only through $\kappa_w$ and $D_w$, and both are small exactly when the profile is flat in $w$.

## Notation

Assume $|\omega|=\sum_i\omega_i\in(0,1]$ and drop indices with $\omega_i=0$. Fix $c\geq e^{8}$; for $2\leq c<e^8$ the bound $\min\{c,\Lambda\}\leq e^8$ suffices. Put $\ell=\log c\geq8$. For $w\geq0$ put $e_i=e^{wZ_i-w^2/2}$ and $L_w=\sum_i\omega_ie_i$. Let $F_w=\log L_w$ and $p_i=\omega_ie_i/L_w$ (the posterior at scale $w$). Further let $S=\sum_ip_iZ_i$, $D_w=\partial_wF_w=S-w$, $\kappa_w=-\partial_w^2F_w=1-\operatorname{Var}_p(Z_J)$, $u_i=(\Sigma p)_i$, and $|m|^2=p^{\mathsf T}\Sigma p=\sum_ip_iu_i\in[0,1]$. Write $\mathbb E_{Q_w}[X]=\mathbb E[L_wX]$, an unnormalized tilted expectation of total mass $|\omega|$. By the shift identity (step 1 of the failed plan), $\mathbb E_{Q_w}[g(Z)]=\sum_i\omega_i\mathbb E[g(Z+w\Sigma_{\cdot i})]$. Gaussian integration by parts is $\mathbb E[Z_i\Phi(Z)]=\sum_k\Sigma_{ik}\mathbb E[\partial_k\Phi(Z)]$. Here $\partial_kF_w=wp_k$ and $\partial_kp_j=wp_j(\delta_{jk}-p_k)$.

## Plan

The function $v(w)=\min\{c,\max\{e^2,L_w\}\}$ is locally absolutely continuous with $v(0)=e^2$ and $\sup v=\max\{e^2,\min\{c,\Lambda\}\}$. Hence $(\min\{c,\Lambda\}-e^2)_+\leq\int_0^\infty(\partial_wL_w)_+\mathbf 1\{e^2<L_w<c\}\,dw$. Claim: $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}\leq e^2+\int_0^\infty\mathbb E[(\partial_wL_w)_+\mathbf 1\{e^2<L_w<c\}]\,dw$ (Tonelli).
2. [x] **Symmetrization.** Prove $\int_0^\infty\mathbb E[\partial_wL_w\mathbf 1\{e^2<L_w<c\}]\,dw=0$. The integrand is $\frac{d}{dw}\mathbb E(\min\{L_w,c\}-e^2)_+$, with differentiation under $\mathbb E$ justified by $|\partial_wL_w|\leq\sum_i\omega_i|Z_i-w|e_i$, locally uniformly integrable. Since $\partial_w^2L=L(D^2-\kappa)$, we have $\frac{d}{dw}\mathbb E[\partial_wL\,\chi(w,F_w)]=\mathbb E_{Q_w}[(D^2-\kappa)\chi+D^2\partial_x\chi+D\,\partial_w\chi]$. ## Verification

Steps 1 and 2 give $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}\leq e^2+\frac12A\leq e^2+\frac12A_\eta$, because $\eta\geq\mathbf 1_{(2,\ell)}$. Step 5 gives $A_\eta\leq K+4S$. Step 6 gives $K\leq3\sqrt\ell+R_1$. Step 7 gives $R_1+4S\leq C\sqrt\ell$, with step 8 covering $w\leq1$. Hence $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}\leq e^2+C'\sqrt{\log c}\leq C_{\mathrm{FR}}\sqrt{\log c}$ for $c\geq e^8$. The case $2\leq c<e^8$ is trivial. All constants are absolute. The identities use only Gaussian integration by parts for $Z\sim\mathcal N_n(0,\Sigma)$, which holds for singular $\Sigma$, and finite $n$. No bound depends on $n$, $\Sigma$, the weights or $|\omega|$: every identity is linear in $\omega$, and (T) carries the factor $|\omega|\leq1$.

## Difference from failed plans

- plan_gaussian_frozen_ray_order_bound_planted_line_tracking: that plan placed a unit window at the random maximizer $\hat w$. It then had to bound the tracking event $\{|\hat w(Z^{i,a})-a|\leq1\}$, which has probability of order one at alignment $O(1/n)$ in the independent family, so the alignment-weighted length bound diverged. This plan has no maximizer, window or tracking event. The co-area bound (step 1) weights each scale by the Fisher score $|D_w|$. In the spread regimes that broke step 7 there, $|D_w|$ and $\kappa_w$ are small, because the profile is flat. This is the "mobility of $\hat w$" information that the failure section asked for, in integrated form: $D_w$ is $\partial_w\log L_w$ and $\kappa_w$ is the denominator of $\partial_a\hat w$. Multimodal profiles are not excluded. Their extra variation is paid for by the exact second-moment identity of step 4, not by a unimodality assumption. The proved steps 1 to 6 of that plan are not reused, except the shift identity (its step 1) for interpreting $Q_w$.

## Progress

Steps 1 to 6, 8 and 9 are established below; step 7 is not (see Failure). Notation is as in the Notation section. Throughout, $n$ is finite and $\omega_i>0$, so every quantity $\mathbb E[\mathrm{poly}(Z)\,L_w^{a}]$ is finite and locally bounded in $w$; this justifies each use of Fubini and of differentiation under $\mathbb E$ below (the bounds obtained do not depend on these finiteness constants).

**Tools.** Write $\Sigma=AA^{\mathsf T}$ and $Z=AG$ with $G$ standard Gaussian; this covers singular $\Sigma$. (i) *Shift identity.* Since $|A^{\mathsf T}e_i|^2=\Sigma_{ii}=1$, $e_i=\exp(w\langle A^{\mathsf T}e_i,G\rangle-w^2/2)$, and the Cameron-Martin formula gives $\mathbb E[e_i\,g(Z)]=\mathbb E[g(Z+w\Sigma_{\cdot i})]$; hence $\mathbb E_{Q_w}[g]=\sum_i\omega_i\mathbb E[g(Z+w\Sigma_{\cdot i})]$. Under the $i$-th shift, $F_w\geq\log\omega_i+wZ_i+w^2/2=:\lambda_i(w)$, which tends to $+\infty$ a.s. as $w\to\infty$. (ii) *Gaussian integration by parts.* $\mathbb E[Z_i\Phi(Z)]=\sum_k\Sigma_{ik}\mathbb E[\partial_k\Phi(Z)]$ for $C^1$ functions $\Phi$ of at most exponential growth with such derivatives (standard IBP in $G$). (iii) Derivatives: $\partial_ke_i=w\delta_{ik}e_i$, $\partial_kF_w=wp_k$, $\partial_kp_j=wp_j(\delta_{jk}-p_k)$, $\partial_wp_j=p_j(Z_j-S)$, $\partial_wL=LD$, $\partial_w^2L=L(D^2-\kappa)$.

**Step 1 (settled).** Fix $z$. The map $y\mapsto\min\{c,\max\{e^2,y\}\}$ is $1$-Lipschitz, so $v(w)=\min\{c,\max\{e^2,L_w\}\}$ is locally Lipschitz with $v'=\partial_wL_w\,\mathbf 1\{e^2<L_w<c\}$ for a.e. $w$ (chain rule for Lipschitz $\circ$ smooth; on $\{L_w\in\{e^2,c\}\}$ one has $\partial_wL_w=0$ a.e.). As $L_0=|\omega|\leq1<e^2$, $v(0)=e^2$, and for each $W$, $v(W)-e^2=\int_0^Wv'\leq\int_0^\infty(\partial_wL_w)_+\mathbf 1\{e^2<L_w<c\}\,dw$. Taking the supremum over $W$: $\min\{c,\Lambda\}\leq\min\{c,\max\{e^2,\Lambda\}\}=\sup_Wv(W)\leq e^2+\int_0^\infty(\partial_wL_w)_+\mathbf 1\{e^2<L_w<c\}\,dw$. Take expectations (Tonelli).

**Step 2 (settled).** Let $\psi(y)=\min\{(y-e^2)_+,c-e^2\}$ and $g(w)=\mathbb E\psi(L_w)$. Pathwise $\psi(L_W)=\int_0^W\partial_wL\,\mathbf 1\{e^2<L<c\}\,dw$, and $\mathbb E\int_0^W|\partial_wL|\,dw\leq\int_0^W\sum_i\omega_i\mathbb E[|Z_i-w|e_i]\,dw<\infty$, so $g(W)=\int_0^W\mathbb E[\partial_wL\,\mathbf 1\{e^2<L<c\}]\,dw$ and $g(0)=0$. Also $\psi(L_W)\leq(c-e^2)\mathbf 1\{L_W>e^2\}$ and $L_W\leq|\omega|e^{W\max_jZ_j-W^2/2}\to0$ a.s., so $g(W)\to0$ by dominated convergence. If $A^{\flat}:=\int_0^\infty\mathbb E[|\partial_wL|\mathbf 1\{e^2<L<c\}]\,dw<\infty$ (shown in step 5: $A^\flat\leq A_\eta<\infty$), then $\int_0^\infty\mathbb E[\partial_wL\mathbf 1\{\cdot\}]\,dw=\lim_Wg(W)=0$, hence $\int_0^\infty\mathbb E[(\partial_wL)_+\mathbf 1\{\cdot\}]=\tfrac12A^\flat$. Since $|\partial_wL|=L|D_w|$, $A^\flat=A=\int_0^\infty\mathbb E_{Q_w}[|D_w|\mathbf 1\{2<F_w<\ell\}]\,dw$ and $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}\leq e^2+\tfrac12A$.

**Step 3 (settled).** Let $\varphi$ be $C^1$, bounded, with bounded derivative.
- (I1) $\mathbb E[(Z_i-w)e_i\varphi(F_w)]$: IBP gives $\sum_k\Sigma_{ik}\partial_k(e_i\varphi)=we_i\varphi+we_i\varphi'(F_w)u_i$, so $\mathbb E[(Z_i-w)e_i\varphi]=w\mathbb E[e_iu_i\varphi']$. Multiply by $\omega_i$ and sum, using $\sum_i\omega_ie_iu_i=L|m|^2$: $\mathbb E[\partial_wL\,\varphi(F_w)]=w\,\mathbb E_{Q_w}[|m|^2\varphi'(F_w)]$.
- (I2) (for $\varphi$ as above). IBP of $Z_i$ against $Z_ie_i\varphi$ gives $\mathbb E[Z_i^2e_i\varphi]=\mathbb E[e_i\varphi+wZ_ie_i\varphi+wZ_ie_iu_i\varphi']$. IBP of $Z_i$ against $e_iS\varphi$, using $\partial_kS=p_k+wp_k(Z_k-S)$, hence $\sum_k\Sigma_{ik}\partial_kS=u_i+w\sum_k\Sigma_{ik}p_k(Z_k-S)$, gives $\mathbb E[Z_ie_iS\varphi]=\mathbb E[we_iS\varphi+e_i\varphi u_i+we_i\varphi\sum_k\Sigma_{ik}p_k(Z_k-S)+we_iSu_i\varphi']$. Summing with $\omega_i$ ($\sum_i\omega_ie_i\Sigma_{ik}=Lu_k$) and subtracting: $\mathbb E_{Q_w}[\operatorname{Var}_p(Z_J)\varphi]=\mathbb E_{Q_w}[(1-|m|^2)\varphi]+w\,\mathbb E_{Q_w}[(\varphi'-\varphi)\operatorname{Cov}_p(u_J,Z_J)]$, i.e. $\mathbb E_{Q_w}[\kappa_w\varphi]=\mathbb E_{Q_w}[|m|^2\varphi]+w\,\mathbb E_{Q_w}[(\varphi-\varphi')(F_w)\operatorname{Cov}_p(u_J,Z_J)]$. Also $\partial_w|m|^2=2\sum_jp_j(Z_j-S)u_j=2\operatorname{Cov}_p(u_J,Z_J)$ at fixed $Z$.
- (I3) By Cauchy-Schwarz, $(\partial_wL)^2=(\sum_i\omega_i(Z_i-w)e_i)^2\leq L\sum_i\omega_i(Z_i-w)^2e_i$, so $\mathbb E_{Q_w}[D_w^2]\leq\sum_i\omega_i\mathbb E[(Z_i-w)^2e_i]=\sum_i\omega_i\mathbb E[Z_i^2]=|\omega|$ by the shift identity.
- (I4) IBP of $Z_i$ against $e_i|m|^2\varphi$, with $\partial_k|m|^2=2wp_k(u_k-|m|^2)$ and $\sum_i p_i\Sigma_{ik}=u_k$, gives $\mathbb E_{Q_w}[D_w|m|^2\varphi]=w\,\mathbb E_{Q_w}[|m|^4\varphi']+2w\,\mathbb E_{Q_w}[\operatorname{Var}_p(u_J)\varphi]$.
- (I5) (new, recorded for later use; $\psi\in C^1$ bounded with bounded derivative). IBP of $Z_i$ against $e_iu_i\psi$, with $\sum_k\Sigma_{ik}\partial_ku_i=w(\sum_k\Sigma_{ik}^2p_k-u_i^2)$, minus (I4)'s computation, gives $\mathbb E_{Q_w}[\operatorname{Cov}_p(u_J,Z_J)\psi(F_w)]=w\,\mathbb E_{Q_w}[(V_2-3V_1)\psi(F_w)+V_1\psi'(F_w)]$, where $V_1=\operatorname{Var}_p(u_J)$ and $V_2=\sum_{j,k}p_jp_k\Sigma_{jk}^2-|m|^4=\operatorname{Var}_{p\otimes p}(\Sigma_{JJ'})\geq0$ (the overlap variance of two replicas).

**Step 4 (settled).** Let $h(w)=\mathbb E[\partial_wL\,\chi(w,F_w)]=\mathbb E_{Q_w}[D_w\chi]$. Then $h'(w)=\mathbb E_{Q_w}[(D^2-\kappa)\chi+D^2\partial_x\chi+D\partial_w\chi]$, and $h(W)-h(0)=\int_0^Wh'$ by Fubini. $h(0)=0$ since $F_0=\log|\omega|\leq0<1$. With $\chi=w\varphi(x)$, $\varphi=\eta/\sqrt x$: $-\partial_x\chi=\chi/(2x)-w\eta'/\sqrt x$, which is $\leq\chi/2$ on $[1,2]$ ($\eta'\geq0$), $\leq\chi/4$ on $[2,\ell]$, and $\leq\chi/(2\ell)+\chi/\ell\leq\chi/4$ on $[\ell,\infty)$. And $|\partial_w\chi|=\varphi\leq\eta$. Since $\chi\geq0$, step 4 on $[0,W]$ gives $\frac12\int_0^W\mathbb E_Q[D^2\chi]\leq\int_0^W\mathbb E_Q[\kappa\chi]+\int_0^W\mathbb E_Q[|D|\eta]+h(W)$. Finiteness (finite $n$): $|\kappa|\leq1+\sum_jp_jZ_j^2$, so $\mathbb E_{Q_w}[|\kappa|\chi]\leq w\,\mathbb E[(L_w+\sum_j\omega_je_jZ_j^2)\eta(F_w)]=w\sum_j\omega_j\mathbb E[(1+(Z_j+w)^2)\,\eta(F_w)(Z+w\Sigma_{\cdot j})]$ decays super-exponentially at large $w$ (as in step 4) and is $\leq C(1+w^2)^{1/2}e^{-(1-w^2/2)^2/(4w^2)}$ for $w\leq1$ (step 8); likewise $\mathbb E_Q[|D|\eta]\leq|\omega|^{1/2}Q_w(F_w\geq1)^{1/2}$. Hence $K=\int_0^\infty\mathbb E_{Q_w}[\kappa_ww\varphi(F_w)]dw$ converges absolutely, $A_\eta<\infty$, and letting $W\to\infty$: $\int_0^\infty\mathbb E_Q[D^2\chi]\leq2K+2A_\eta$ (so $K+A_\eta\geq0$). With $\beta=w/\sqrt{F}>0$ on $\operatorname{supp}\eta$, $|D|\eta\leq\frac\varepsilon2D^2\chi+\frac1{2\varepsilon}\eta\sqrt F/w$; with $\varepsilon=\frac12$: $A_\eta\leq\frac12(K+A_\eta)+S$, so $A_\eta\leq K+2S$, where $S=\int_0^\infty w^{-1}\mathbb E_{Q_w}[\sqrt{F_w}\,\eta(F_w)]dw$ (finite for finite $n$, by step 8 and the large-$w$ decay).

**Step 6 (settled).** For continuous integrable $k$ on $\mathbb R$ vanishing on $(-\infty,1]$, define $G'(x)=\int_1^xe^{-(x-y)}k(y)dy$, $G(x)=\int_1^xG'$. Then $G\in C^2$, $G'+G''=k$, $|G|,|G'|\leq\int|k|$, $G(\infty)=\int_1^\infty k$ (Fubini). By (I1) with $\varphi=G+G'$: $\frac d{dw}\mathbb E_{Q_w}[G(F_w)]=\mathbb E[\partial_wL\,(G+G')(F_w)]=w\mathbb E_{Q_w}[|m|^2k(F_w)]$ (differentiation under $\mathbb E$ by Fubini with $|\partial_w(LG(F))|\leq|\partial_wL|(|G|+|G'|)$). At $w=0$, $G(F_0)=0$. As $W\to\infty$, $\mathbb E_{Q_W}G(F_W)=\sum_i\omega_i\mathbb E G(F_W(Z+W\Sigma_{\cdot i}))\to|\omega|G(\infty)$ by bounded convergence, since $F_W\geq\lambda_i(W)\to\infty$ under each shift. For $k\geq0$ monotone convergence then gives (T): $\int_0^\infty w\,\mathbb E_{Q_w}[|m|^2k(F_w)]dw=|\omega|\int_1^\infty k$; for signed $k$ apply it to $k_\pm$. With $k=\varphi=\eta/\sqrt x$: $\int\varphi\leq1+2\sqrt\ell+e^{1/16}\sqrt\ell\leq4\sqrt\ell$. By (I2) with $\varphi$ and (T): $K=|\omega|\int\varphi+R_1\leq4\sqrt\ell+R_1$, with $R_1=\int_0^\infty w^2\mathbb E_{Q_w}[(\varphi-\varphi')(F_w)\operatorname{Cov}_p(u_J,Z_J)]dw=K-|\omega|\int\varphi$ finite.

**Step 8 (settled).** For real $k\geq1$, Jensen gives $L_w^k\leq|\omega|^{k-1}\sum_i\omega_ie_i^k$, so $\mathbb EL_w^k\leq e^{k(k-1)w^2/2}$, and $Q_w(F_w\geq x)=\mathbb E[L_w\mathbf 1\{L_w\geq e^x\}]\leq e^{k(k-1)w^2/2-(k-1)x}$. Optimizing ($k-1=x/w^2-\frac12$) gives, for $x\geq w^2/2$, the upper-tail bound $Q_w(F_w\geq x)\leq\exp(-(x-w^2/2)^2/(2w^2))$ (sharp for the single test). For $w\leq1$: $\mathbb E_{Q_w}[\sqrt F\eta(F)]\leq\sum_{j\geq1}\sqrt{j+1}\,Q_w(F\geq j)\leq Ce^{-1/(8w^2)}$, so $\int_0^1w^{-1}\mathbb E_Q[\sqrt F\eta]\,dw\leq C$; and $|\operatorname{Cov}_p(u_J,Z_J)|\leq\operatorname{Var}_p(Z_J)^{1/2}$ (as $|u_j|\leq1$) gives $\mathbb E_{Q_w}[|\varphi-\varphi'|(F)|\operatorname{Cov}_p|]\leq\|\varphi-\varphi'\|_\infty Q_w(F\geq1)^{1/2}(1+w^2)^{1/2}$, so the $w\leq1$ part of $R_1$ is $O(1)$. All constants absolute.

**Step 9 (settled: the proved reduction).** Combining steps 1, 2, 5, 6: for every $c\geq e^8$, uniformly in $n,\Sigma,\omega$,
$$
\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda(Z)\}\leq e^2+\tfrac12A\leq e^2+\tfrac12(K+2S)\leq e^2+2\sqrt\ell+\tfrac12R_1+S,
$$
with $R_1$ and $S$ as above; the $w\leq1$ parts of $R_1$ and $S$ are $O(1)$. The exact identities (I1) to (I5) and (T) hold as stated.

**Additional proved reduction of $S$ (D-cancellation).** Pathwise, $F_w=F_0+wD_w+I_w$ with $I_w=\int_0^wr\kappa_r\,dr$ (since $\frac d{dw}(wD_w)=D_w-w\kappa_w$). As $\sqrt F\eta=F\varphi$, $F_0\leq0$, $\varphi\geq0$ and by (I1) $w^{-1}\mathbb E_Q[wD_w\varphi(F_w)]=w\mathbb E_Q[|m|^2\varphi'(F_w)]$, whose integral over $w$ is $|\omega|\int_1^\infty\varphi'=0$ by (T) (absolutely convergent, $\varphi'$ continuous as $\eta'(1)=0$), we get $S\leq\int_0^\infty w^{-1}\mathbb E_{Q_w}[I_w\,\varphi(F_w)]\,dw$. So the score part of $F_w$ costs nothing; what remains of $S$ is the accumulated past curvature $I_w$ at fixed $Z$, weighted by the present tilt $Q_w$.

## Failure

Step 7 could not be completed: neither $S\leq C\sqrt\ell$ nor $R_1\leq C\sqrt\ell$ was proved. Steps 1 to 6, 8 and 9 are proved (see Progress), so the target follows from the single estimate $S+\frac12R_1\leq C\sqrt{\log c}$.

Why the planned inputs do not give $S\leq C\sqrt\ell$.
- (a) is not a valid principle as stated. Gaussian concentration of $F_w$ under a shift works at the scale of the Lipschitz constant, $\sup|\nabla F_w|=\sup w|m|$, which can be of order $w$. It does not work at the local value $w|m|$. The log-Sobolev/Herbst form $\operatorname{Ent}(e^{\lambda F})\leq\frac{\lambda^2}2\mathbb E[w^2|m|^2e^{\lambda F}]$ again needs a uniform bound on $|m|^2$. So small local overlap does not keep $F_w$ near its mean.
- (b) bounds only averages. $\rho(w)=\mathbb E_{Q_w}|m|^2/|\omega|=1-\mathrm{mmse}(w^2)$ is indeed nondecreasing (I-MMSE for the channel $G+wX_J$ with $|X_J|=1$), so $\mathrm{KL}_w\leq\frac{w^2}2\rho(w)$ and $\mathrm{KL}_w/w^2$ is nondecreasing. But $S$ needs the comparison at each level $x\in[2,\ell]$: $\mathbb E_{Q_w}[|m|^2\mid F_w=x]\gtrsim x/w^2$, or an equivalent statement integrated against $dw/w$. A bound on the mean $\mathrm{KL}_w$ does not control how the $Q_w$-mass at level $x$ is shared between large and small $|m|^2$.
- (c) is circular. After the D-cancellation above, $S\leq\int w^{-1}\mathbb E_{Q_w}[I_w\varphi(F_w)]dw$, which needs a two-time comparison: $\int_0^wr\kappa_r\,dr$ (curvature at earlier scales $r<w$, same $Z$) against $w^2|m_w|^2$, under the later tilt $Q_w$. Every exact identity available ((I1)-(I5), (T)) is single-time: it relates quantities at one scale $w$ under $Q_w$. The bound $\kappa\leq1$ is too lossy. In the one-factor family with small $q$ it gives $S\approx\frac1q\sqrt\ell$, while the truth is $\approx\sqrt\ell$, because there $\kappa_r\approx|m_r|^2\approx q$.
- The loss is structural. The Cauchy-Schwarz weight in step 5 depends only on $(w,F_w)$. So $S$ charges every bit of $Q_w$-mass in the band $\{2\leq F_w\lesssim\ell\}$ with $\sqrt{F_w}/w$, whatever $D_w$ is. Hence $S\leq C\sqrt\ell$ is equivalent, up to constants, to a log-scale occupation bound: $\int_0^\infty w^{-1}Q_w(F_w\in[x,x+1])\,dw\lesssim1/x$ for $x\in[2,\ell]$, uniformly in $n,\Sigma,\omega$. This bound holds in the four test families: single test, one-factor (scale-invariant in $q$), independent equal-weight family (where it is $O(1/\log n)$), and orthogonal mixtures. But it says that the tilted log-likelihood cannot stay in a band for many dyadic scales of $w$, a statement about the model that is not implied by the identities. A model with $F_w$ nearly frozen in the band over a long range of $\log w$ under $Q_w$ (flat profile, spread posterior) would defeat this plan even if the target is true. No such model was found.

Why $R_1$ is open. $R_1=K-|\omega|\int\varphi$ exactly, and by (I5) (for $C^2$ level weights)
$$
R_1=\int_0^\infty w^3\,\mathbb E_{Q_w}\big[(\varphi-\varphi')(F_w)(V_2-3V_1)+(\varphi'-\varphi'')(F_w)V_1\big]\,dw,
$$
a weighted integral of the replica overlap variances $V_2=\operatorname{Var}_{p\otimes p}(\Sigma_{JJ'})$ and $V_1=\operatorname{Var}_p(u_J)$. Bounding it by $C\sqrt\ell$ is an integrated Ghirlanda-Guerra-type estimate with weight $w^3$. In the independent family near $w=\sqrt{2\log n}$ the individual terms are of order $w^2$, so any bound must use cancellation between the $V_2-3V_1$ and $V_1$ terms (which there come from the one dominant atom). The route the plan suggested, differentiating $\mathbb E_{Q_w}[|m|^2\psi(F_w)]$ and using (I4), brings $\operatorname{Cov}_p(u_J,Z_J)$ back through $\partial_w|m|^2$. With (I5) this closes into the same overlap-variance integrals, with no sign that would give the bound.

What would be needed: (1) the log-scale occupation bound above, or the conditional comparison $\int_0^\infty w^{-1}\mathbb E_{Q_w}[(F_w-Cw^2|m_w|^2)_+\varphi(F_w)]dw\leq C\sqrt\ell$; with it, $S\leq C\sqrt\ell$ follows by (T). (2) The integrated overlap-variance bound for $R_1$. Equivalently, the bound $K\leq C\sqrt\ell$ for $K=\int_0^\infty w\,\mathbb E_{Q_w}[\kappa_w\varphi(F_w)]dw$, the level-weighted integrated curvature. A successor plan could also replace step 5's $(w,F)$-weight by one involving $|m|^2$. Step 4 then produces $\partial_w|m|^2=2\operatorname{Cov}_p(u_J,Z_J)$ terms, controlled by (I5).
