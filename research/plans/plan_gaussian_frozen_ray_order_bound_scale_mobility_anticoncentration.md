## Goal

Target: [asset](../assets/gaussian_frozen_ray_order_bound.md).

Prove $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda(Z)\}\leq C_{\mathrm{FR}}\sqrt{\log c}$ for every $c\geq2$, uniformly in $n$, $\Sigma\in\mathcal C_n$ (singular allowed, either sign) and subprobability weights. The statement is not changed.

Idea. Both failed plans lose at the same place: the planted line $a\mapsto Z+a\Sigma_{\cdot i}$ may track the maximizing scale ($\hat w\approx a$) for a long stretch while the alignment is small. The failed planted-line plan records the two mechanisms that keep this harmless in the test families. (i) Immobile lines (independent equal-weight family): $\hat w$ does not move with $a$, so the line crosses the tracking band once, in length $O(1)$. (ii) Mobile lines (one-factor family $\sqrt q\,u+\sqrt{1-q}\,e_j$): $\hat w$ moves with $a$ at speed $1$, so tracking is long, of length about $\sqrt{\log c/q}$. But it happens only with probability about $\sqrt q$, because $\hat w$ is spread at scale $1/\sqrt q$ in the factor direction. Convexity sees neither mechanism. This plan uses both. The mobility $\mu_i=\partial_a\hat w$ along the line comes from the implicit-function formula. Shrinking the window to width $\varepsilon\to0$ gives an exact fixed-point (Kac-Rice type) identity. In it, the planted index is distributed exactly by the posterior at $\hat w$ (no window mismatch as in step 6 of the planted-line plan), so atypical indices can be absorbed. Immobile lines are handled by counting crossings along the line. Mobile lines are handled by Gaussian integration by parts in $G$-space along the gradient of $\hat w$. Its size $|\nabla\hat w|=|W|/\kappa$ gives a density factor equal to the alignment $\beta_i$ of the planted direction with $\nabla\hat w$. In the one-factor family, $\beta_i\approx\sqrt q\approx\sqrt{\alpha_i}$, which exactly pays for the convexity length $\sqrt{\log c/\alpha_i}$. Everything is evaluated at the shared maximizing scale $\hat w$ (idea_6d87d51a889d).

## Notation

As in plan_gaussian_frozen_ray_order_bound_planted_line_tracking: $|\omega|\in(0,1]$, $L_a$, $p^{(a)}$, $\hat w$ (largest maximizer), $p=p^{(\hat w)}$, $\alpha_i=\sum_jp_j\Sigma_{ji}$, $Z^{i,a}=Z+a\Sigma_{\cdot i}$, $h=\min\{1,c/\Lambda\}\mathbf 1\{\Lambda>1\}$. Also put $\tilde h=\min\{1,c/\Lambda\}$. Work in $G$-space: $Z=AG$ with $A=\Sigma^{1/2}$, $v_i=Ae_i$, $|v_i|=1$, $Z_i=\langle v_i,G\rangle$. The planted line is $G'+av_i$ with $G'$ fixed, and $x(a)=AG'+a\Sigma_{\cdot i}$. Posterior quantities at $\hat w$:
$$
m=\sum_jp_jv_j,\qquad \kappa=1-\operatorname{Var}_p(Z_J),\qquad C=\operatorname{Cov}_p(v_J,Z_J)=\sum_jp_j(v_j-m)(Z_j-\bar Z),\qquad W=m+\hat w\,C .
$$
Then $|m|^2=p^{\mathsf T}\Sigma p$ and $\alpha_i=\langle v_i,m\rangle$. Mobility: $\mu_i=\langle W,v_i\rangle/\kappa$. Gradient alignment: $\beta_i=|\langle W,v_i\rangle|/|W|$. Tracking offset on line $i$: $u_i(a)=\hat w(x(a))-a$.

## Plan

1. [ ] **Reused facts.** Steps 1 (shift identity), 2 (scale profile, Borel measurability of $\hat w$, $g''\geq-1$, interior maximizer when $\Lambda>1$) and 4 (convexity of $\varphi(a)=\log\Lambda(x(a))$ with $\varphi'_+(a)\geq\hat w(x(a))\,\alpha_i(x(a))$) of plan_gaussian_frozen_ray_order_bound_planted_line_tracking are proved. Copy them with that reference. Also record $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}\leq1+\mathbb E[\Lambda h]$ and $\mathbb E[\min\{c,\Lambda\}\mathbf 1\{\Lambda\leq1\}]\leq1$.
2. [ ] **Regularity and gradient formulas.** Let $N$ be the set of $G$ with $\Lambda>1$, a unique global maximizer $\hat w$ and $\kappa>0$. Claims. (a) On $N$, $\hat w$ is real-analytic near $G$, with $\nabla_G\hat w=W/\kappa$, $\nabla_G\log\Lambda=\hat w\,m$, and $\partial_a\hat w(G'+av_i)=\mu_i$. This agrees with the planted-line plan's $(\alpha_i+\hat w\operatorname{Cov}_p(x_J,\Sigma_{Ji}))/\kappa$, since $\langle C,v_i\rangle=\operatorname{Cov}_p(Z_J,\Sigma_{Ji})$. (b) $\{\Lambda>1\}\setminus N$ is $\gamma$-null. (c) For each $i$ and a.e. $G'$, the line meets $\{\Lambda>1\}\setminus N$ in a locally finite set (ties, where $\hat w$ jumps), and every zero of $u_i$ in $N$ has $\mu_i\neq1$. For (c), use Sard's theorem for $(G',a)\mapsto u_i$, together with the fact that ties are zero sets of differences of analytic local-maximum values. If (b) or (c) fails on a positive-measure set, isolate that set as an extra term handled in step 9.
3. [ ] **Exact fixed-point identity (window $\varepsilon\downarrow0$).** For Borel $f_j(x)\geq0$ with $\Lambda\tilde h\sum_jp_jf_j$ integrable, prove
$$
\mathbb E\Big[\Lambda\tilde h\sum_jp_jf_j\Big]=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\sum_i\omega_i\int_0^\infty\varepsilon^{-1}\,\mathbb E\big[\mathbf 1\{u_i(a)\in[-\varepsilon,0]\}f_i\tilde h\big](Z^{i,a})\,da .
$$
Proof: by step 1, the right side before the limit is $\mathbb E[\tilde h\,\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{\hat w}^{\hat w+\varepsilon}\sum_i\omega_ie^{aZ_i-a^2/2}f_i\,da]$. As $\varepsilon\to0$ this tends pointwise to $\Lambda\tilde h\sum_jp_jf_j$, because $a\mapsto p^{(a)}$ is continuous. Dominated convergence applies for bounded $f$ (the bound is $\Lambda\tilde h\sup f\leq c\sup f$), and monotone truncation handles the general case. Corollaries. (a) Pathwise Fatou: $\sum_i\omega_i\mathbb E\sum_{a:\,u_i(a)=0}f_i\tilde h/|1-\mu_i|\leq\mathbb E[\Lambda\tilde h\sum_jp_jf_j]$, using step 2(c). (b) Nishimori absorption: if $p(S(x))\leq\eta$ on $\{\Lambda>1\}$, then $\mathbb E[\Lambda h\,p(S)]\leq\eta\,\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}$.
4. [ ] **Mobility split.** With $S_{\mathrm{imm}}=\{j:\mu_j<1/4\}$ and $S_{\mathrm{mob}}=\{j:\mu_j\geq1/4\}$ (defined on $N$), $\mathbb E[\Lambda h]=\mathbb E[\Lambda h\,p(S_{\mathrm{imm}})]+\mathbb E[\Lambda h\,p(S_{\mathrm{mob}})]$. Put
$$
M=\mathbb E\Big[\Lambda\tilde h\sum_jp_j\max\{1,\mu_j\}\mathbf 1\{\mu_j\geq1/4\}\Big]\geq\mathbb E[\Lambda h\,p(S_{\mathrm{mob}})].
$$
5. [ ] **Immobile lines: crossing count.** Fix $i$ and $G'$, and work at fixed small $\varepsilon$ so that no limits need exchanging. (a) On a maximal interval of $N$-points with $\mu_i<1/4$, $u_i'\leq-3/4$, so the interval meets $\{u_i\in[-\varepsilon,0]\}$ in length at most $4\varepsilon/3$. (b) Two consecutive downward passages of $u_i$ through the band are separated either by an upward passage with $\mu_i>1$ or by an upward jump of $u_i$ across the band at a tie. (c) Charging. $\varphi$ is convex, so $\tilde h(x(a))=\min\{1,ce^{-\varphi(a)}\}$ is monotone on each side of $\arg\min\varphi$. Charge each downward passage on the increasing side of $\varphi$ to the preceding upward event, and each on the decreasing side to the following one. Each upward event is charged at most twice, and at most two passages are left uncharged. (d) Count the upward passages with $\mu_i>1$ by step 3(a) with $f_j=\max\{1,\mu_j\}\mathbf 1\{\mu_j>1\}$: indeed $1\leq\max\{1,\mu\}/|1-\mu|$ for $\mu>1$. Claim: $\mathbb E[\Lambda h\,p(S_{\mathrm{imm}})]\leq\frac43(2+2M+2J)$. Here $J=\sum_i\omega_i\mathbb E\sum_{\text{upward tie jumps of }u_i\text{ across }[-1,0]}\tilde h$.
6. [ ] **Key step A: anti-concentration of $\hat w$ on mobile lines.** Fix $(i,a)$ and let $\gamma_{i,a}=\mathcal N(av_i,I)$, the law of $G'+av_i$. Let $\chi$ be a smooth cutoff, equal to $1$ on $[1/4,\infty)$ and to $0$ on $(-\infty,1/8]$. On $N$ put $V=\nabla\hat w/|\nabla\hat w|^2=\kappa W/|W|^2$ and $F_i=\tilde h\max\{1,\mu_i\}\chi(\mu_i)\,V$. With $\Psi_\varepsilon(y)=\min\{1,(y+\varepsilon)_+/\varepsilon\}$, Gaussian integration by parts under $\gamma_{i,a}$ gives
$$
\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbb E_{\gamma_{i,a}}\big[\mathbf 1\{\hat w-a\in[-\varepsilon,0]\}\tilde h\max\{1,\mu_i\}\chi(\mu_i)\big]=\mathbb E_{\gamma_{i,a}}\big[\Psi_\varepsilon(\hat w-a)\,\delta_{i,a}(F_i)\big]+\mathcal T_{i,a},
$$
where $\delta_{i,a}(F)=\langle G-av_i,F\rangle-\operatorname{div}F$ is the Gaussian divergence and $\mathcal T_{i,a}$ is the flux of $F_i$ through the tie set, weighted by the jump of $\Psi_\varepsilon(\hat w-a)$. Claims. (a) Pointwise, $|F_i|\leq4\tilde h\,\beta_i$: for $\mu_i\geq1/8$ we have $\kappa\leq8\langle W,v_i\rangle$, and $\max\{1,\mu_i\}\kappa/|W|\leq8|\langle W,v_i\rangle|/|W|$. (b) By the Skorokhod isometry, $\mathbb E\,\delta(F)^2\leq\mathbb E|F|^2+\mathbb E\|\nabla F\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$, which is dimension-free. Compute $\nabla F_i$ from step 2(a). The derivatives of $\kappa,W,\mu_i$ bring third posterior cumulants at $\hat w$ and one factor $\nabla\hat w=W/\kappa$, which the prefactor $\kappa/|W|^2$ of $V$ compensates. Claim: $\|\nabla F_i\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leq C\tilde h(\beta_i+R_i)+|\nabla\tilde h|\,|V|$, with an explicit single-scale remainder $R_i$. Note $|\nabla\tilde h|=\tilde h\hat w|m|\mathbf 1\{\Lambda>c\}$. (c) Combining with step 3, $M\leq\sum_i\omega_i\int_0^\infty\mathbb E_{\gamma_{i,a}}[\mathbf 1\{\hat w\geq a-1\}|\delta_{i,a}(F_i)|]\,da+\sum_i\omega_i\int|\mathcal T_{i,a}|\,da$.
7. [ ] **Key step B: single-scale comparison on typical indices.** Call $i$ typical at $x$ if $(\alpha_i)_+\leq K|m|^2$ and $|\langle C,v_i\rangle|\leq K\sum_jp_j|\langle C,v_j\rangle|$. By Markov under $p$ (Nishimori: $\sum_jp_j\alpha_j=|m|^2$), atypical indices have $p$-mass at most $2/K$. They are absorbed by step 3(b) once $K\geq8$. Claim: for typical $i$ with $\mu_i\geq1/8$, $\beta_i^2\leq C_K\big((\alpha_i)_++\rho\big)$, where $\rho\geq0$ is a single-scale remainder built from $\hat w|C|/|m|$ that vanishes when $C=0$. Model computation: if $C=0$, then $\beta_i=\alpha_i/|m|$ and $\alpha_i\leq K|m|^2$ give $\beta_i^2\leq K\alpha_i$. Check the claim, and the size of $\rho$ and $R_i$, in four families: single test ($\beta=\alpha=\mu=1$), one-factor ($\beta\approx\sqrt q$, $\alpha\approx q$, $\mu\approx1$, $C\approx0$), independent equal-weight (mobile indices rare), and orthogonal mixtures. Also check numerically, in the independent family and in a two-level hierarchical family, that $\sum_i\omega_i\int\mathbb E[\mathbf 1\{\hat w\geq a-1\}|\delta(F_i)|]\,da$ stays bounded as $n$ grows.
8. [ ] **Key step C: one-sided weighted length along the line.** Step 5 of the planted-line plan uses only the one-sided event $\hat w(x(a))\geq a-1$. Redo it with the weight $\sqrt{(\alpha_i)_+}$ instead of a fixed alignment layer. On $\{\hat w\geq a-1,\ a\geq2\}$ we have $\varphi'_+\geq\frac a2\alpha_i$. Claim: $\sum_i\omega_i\int_0^\infty\mathbb E_{\gamma_{i,a}}\big[\mathbf 1\{\hat w\geq a-1\}\tilde h\,(\sqrt{(\alpha_i)_+}+\sqrt\rho+R_i+|V|\hat w|m|\mathbf 1\{\Lambda>c\})\,(1+|\langle G-av_i,\hat V\rangle|)\big]\,da\leq C(1+\sqrt{\log c})$, with $\hat V=V/|V|$, or the same bound with the $L^2$ form of step 6(b) after Cauchy-Schwarz in $G'$. Pathwise, $\int\sqrt{\alpha}\,da$ with $\int a\,\alpha\,da\leq2\log c$ is uniformly $O(\sqrt{\log c})$ when $\alpha$ is roughly constant, but it loses $\sqrt{\log(R/a_0)}$ when $\alpha$ decays like $a^{-2}$ along a long tracking stretch $[a_0,R]$. Rule this profile out in expectation with the probability factor of step 6, or show that it forces $\mu_i<1/8$, so that the line belongs to step 5.
9. [ ] **Ties.** Bound $J$ and the flux terms $\mathcal T_{i,a}$ by $C(1+\sqrt{\log c})+\frac18\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}$. Route: by step 1 backwards, both are Gaussian surface integrals over the tie set $\{\log L_{b_1}=\log L_{b_2}\}$, where $b_1<b_2$ are two global maximizers. Their weight is at most $\min\{c,\Lambda\}(b_2-b_1)$ times the normal component, where the normal is proportional to $b_2m^{(b_2)}-b_1m^{(b_1)}$. Bound the surface measure by co-area for the difference of the two local-maximum values, again in a band. Since $g''\geq-1$, $\log L$ has a dip of depth at least $(b_2-b_1)^2/8$ between two equal maxima, so the posterior must change substantially across a tie.
10. [ ] **Fallback record.** If a key step fails, record the proved reductions: step 3 (exact fixed-point identity and Nishimori absorption), step 5 (immobile part $\leq\frac43(2+2M+2J)$), and whichever of steps 6 to 9 hold. State the failing inequality exactly, and the family on which it fails.

## Verification

Steps 1 and 4 give $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}\leq1+\mathbb E[\Lambda h\,p(S_{\mathrm{imm}})]+\mathbb E[\Lambda h\,p(S_{\mathrm{mob}})]$. The degenerate set adds nothing, by step 2(b). Step 5 bounds the immobile part by $\frac43(2+2M+2J)$, and the mobile part is at most $M$. Steps 6 to 8, with atypical indices absorbed by step 3(b), give $M\leq C(1+\sqrt{\log c})+\frac14\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}+\sum\int|\mathcal T|$. Step 9 bounds $J$ and the flux terms. Hence $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}\leq C'(1+\sqrt{\log c})+\theta\,\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}$ with a numerical $\theta<1$ (choose $K$ and the absorption fractions so that $\theta\leq1/2$). Since $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}\leq c<\infty$, absorption gives $\mathbb E\min\{c,\Lambda\}\leq2C'(1+\sqrt{\log c})\leq C_{\mathrm{FR}}\sqrt{\log c}$, using $1\leq\sqrt{\log c/\log2}$ for $c\geq2$. All constants are numerical; nothing depends on $n$, $\Sigma$, $|\omega|$ or $c$. The Skorokhod isometry and the Hilbert-Schmidt norms are dimension-free. Sanity checks that execution must confirm: in the single-test case the bound reduces to $\int\min\{1,ce^{-a^2/2}\}\,da\asymp\sqrt{\log c}$. In the one-factor case the density factor $\sqrt q$ times the length $\sqrt{\log c/q}$ gives $\sqrt{\log c}$. In the independent family the immobile count gives $O(1)$.

## Difference from failed plans

- plan_gaussian_frozen_ray_order_bound_planted_line_tracking: that plan bounded tracking length by convexity and alignment alone, which diverges as the alignment goes to $0$. This plan adds three new ingredients. First, the mobility $\mu_i=\partial_a\hat w$ along the line, which separates immobile lines (bounded by a crossing count, as in the independent family) from mobile ones. Second, Gaussian anti-concentration of $\hat w$ in $G$-space along $\nabla\hat w=W/\kappa$, which supplies the missing probability factor $\beta_i\approx\sqrt{\alpha_i}$. Third, an $\varepsilon\to0$ window that makes the planted index exactly posterior-distributed. That last point removes the window mismatch $p^{(a)}$ versus $p$ of its step 6 and allows Nishimori absorption of atypical indices.
- plan_gaussian_frozen_ray_order_bound_coarea_fisher_overlap: that plan stayed at fixed scales $w$ under the tilts $Q_w$. It was stopped by a two-scale comparison (log-scale occupation, or curvature at earlier scales under a later tilt) that no single-time identity supplies. This plan never integrates over scales under a tilt. It follows the random maximizer $\hat w$ itself: its gradient, its mobility along planted lines, and its density. So the two-scale information enters through how $\hat w$ moves with $G$, not through any occupation bound. The open inputs here are single-scale inequalities at $\hat w$ (steps 7 and 8) plus a tie estimate (step 9), not an overlap-variance integral.
