## Goal

Target: [Sharp uniform upper-bound target for two-sided Gaussian BH](../assets/gaussian_bh_two_sided_sharp_upper.md).

**The only conclusion.** For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $q_\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ such that for every $q\in(0,q_\varepsilon)$, every finite $N$, every $\theta\in\mathbb R^N$ and every unit-diagonal positive-semidefinite $\Sigma$ (singular allowed; $N,\theta,\Sigma$ may depend on $q$), ordinary BH with the largest qualifying rank applied to $p_i=2\overline\Phi(|T_i|)$ with true nulls $\{\theta_i=0\}$ satisfies
$$
\operatorname{FDR}\le\Big(\frac1{2\sqrt\pi}+\varepsilon\Big)q\sqrt{\log(1/q)} .
$$
Block classes, deterministic curves, realisable-background families and fixed-$q$ numerics below are gates and tests only; none of them is the conclusion. No fixed-dimension, one-factor, spherical or $N\to\infty$-first substitute is used.

## Mechanism (chosen at planning time)

1. **Exact reduction (re-included, proved in earlier Progress).** $\operatorname{FDR}=q\pi_0+\frac qN\sum_i\Delta_i$; in the block class the background acts on a null only through the monotone deficit curve $D^*$ (curve reduction); the shell is paid by the group cap.
2. **New deterministic fact (proved at planning time, step 7): the two-sign excess is one quarter-distance.** For every curve and every mates family, Lei's reflected-sign exclusion holds with the BH line replaced by $D^*$, *exactly* (no good event), provided the separation exceeds $B=\max\{(2K+2r^2)/(1-r^2/U),\ \sqrt U\,\widetilde\Lambda\}$, where $\widetilde\Lambda$ is the largest dimensionless *flat stretch* $vQ(v)$ of the curve ($Q(v)$ = level distance over which $D^*$ drops by a factor $4$). For Lei's line $\widetilde\Lambda\approx\log4$. The DCL counterexample of the failed deficit-curve plan has $\widetilde\Lambda=\infty$; that is exactly why it doubles.
3. **New averaged statement (FIRST KEY gate, step 6).** For every block background, $\mathbb E[\min\{\widetilde\Lambda,\sqrt U\}]\le\eta(q)\sqrt U$. Why it should hold: (a) by MLR the *mean* background count relative to the BH line is monotone in the level (proved at planning time, 6b), so the *mean* deficit grows at least linearly in the rank and is never flat; (b) a flat stretch therefore needs an upward fluctuation of the realised count to within relative $4e^{-vh}$ of the line, and below the critical level this is Markov-small (6d, proved at planning time); (c) at and above the critical level the mean is useless (glassy heavy blocks sit far below their mean), and a quenched bound is needed: **this is the hardest sub-lemma (6e)**. Neither (b) nor (c) is a per-level anti-concentration bound, so the sibling counterexample is not contradicted (6e states its scope).
4. With 2 and 3: averaged DCL with slope $\hat g_r=\gamma_r=\sqrt{(1-r)/(1+r)}$ for the large-carried part; the small-loading part is Lei's Lemma 4.6 for curves (KEY-2, step 8).

### Step 6b - MLR monotonicity, re-proved (sub-step of the KEY step 6; settled)

For $\theta\in\mathbb R$ and $x>0$ the density of $|\theta+\xi|$ is $\phi(x-\theta)+\phi(x+\theta)=2\phi(x)e^{-\theta^2/2}\cosh(\theta x)$, whose ratio to the density $2\phi(x)$ of $|\xi|$ is $e^{-\theta^2/2}\cosh(\theta x)$, nondecreasing in $x>0$. Hence $L_\theta(w):=\mathbb P(|\theta+\xi|\ge w)/p(w)=\mathbb E[e^{-\theta^2/2}\cosh(\theta|\xi|)\mid|\xi|\ge w]$, and since the conditional law of $|\xi|$ given $|\xi|\ge w$ is stochastically nondecreasing in $w$ and the integrand is nondecreasing, $L_\theta$ is nondecreasing on $[0,\infty)$, $L_\theta(0)=1$. Each background coordinate is marginally $N(\theta_\ell,1)$ (unit diagonal; any loading, also $\pm1$), so $m(w)=p(w)\sum_\ell L_{\theta_\ell}(w)$ and $\psi(w)=\frac qN\sum_{\ell\notin B_g}L_{\theta_\ell}(w)$ is nondecreasing. ### Step 6c - rigidity rate, proved WITHOUT the convexity claim 6c(i) (settled; 6c(i) itself is not proved and not used)
