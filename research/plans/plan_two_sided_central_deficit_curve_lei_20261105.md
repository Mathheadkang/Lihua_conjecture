## Goal

Target: [Sharp uniform upper-bound target for two-sided Gaussian BH](../assets/gaussian_bh_two_sided_sharp_upper.md).

**The only conclusion.** For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $q_\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ such that for every $q\in(0,q_\varepsilon)$, every finite $N$, every $\theta\in\mathbb R^N$ and every unit-diagonal positive-semidefinite $\Sigma$ (singular allowed; $N,\theta,\Sigma$ may depend on $q$), ordinary BH with the largest qualifying rank applied to $p_i=2\overline\Phi(|T_i|)$ with true nulls $\{\theta_i=0\}$ satisfies
$$
\operatorname{FDR}\le\Big(\frac1{2\sqrt\pi}+\varepsilon\Big)q\sqrt{\log(1/q)} .
$$
Block classes, frozen deficit curves, multiplicities and fixed-$q$ families below are gates and tests only; none of them is the conclusion.

## Mechanism (chosen at planning time)

1. **Exact reduction (proved in earlier executions, re-included).** For a null $i$ in block $g$ of independent one-factor blocks, $\operatorname{FDR}=q\pi_0+\frac qN\sum_i\Delta_i$ and the baseline-subtracted pivotal identity (BSP) $\Delta_i=\mathbb E[\rho_r(u_R,Z_g)-\rho_r(u_{R^\flat},Z_g)]$, $R\ge R^\flat$, $R^\flat$ independent of $Z_g$ and of the block's noise.
2. **New exact structural fact (proved here at planning time).** Given the background path $\beta(k)=\#\{\ell\notin B_g:P_\ell\le t_k\}$ (independent of the block), put $D(k)=k-1-\beta(k)$ and $D^*(k)=\min_{k\le j\le N}D(j)$. Then $D^*$ is nondecreasing and
$$
R=\max\{k:\ M_Z(k)\ge D^*(k)\},\qquad R^\flat=\max\{k:D^*(k)\le0\},
$$
where $M_z(k)$ is the mates' count at level $t_k$. So the whole influence of an arbitrary background on a null is one **nondecreasing deficit curve** $D^*\le k-1$. Lei's setting is the steepest curve $D^*(k)=k-1$ (empty background). The central statement needed is a "Lei lemma for every monotone curve" (DCL). Because the background is independent of the block, a bound that holds **for every deterministic curve** passes to every random background by Fubini: no per-level anti-concentration, no restriction on the number of background blocks, no use of the realised-count law. The sibling counterexample cx_block_extreme_window_lemma_r1_s2_critical_iid_background only says that critical i.i.d. backgrounds realise hugging curves $D^*\approx\sqrt k$ with probability of order one; those are curves, and DCL must hold for them.
3. **Central form only.** The full-expectation per-null inequality is false (shell $|Z|\in(U,(2+r)U]$ gives twice the bound, idea_4bf520f014e0). The gate is the central form $|Z_g|\le U$. The shell $U<|Z_g|\le U_g$, $p(U_g)=qw_g$, is paid by the group cap with a sharpened shell lemma (step 4).
4. **First KEY gate = mates only, $d=0$, central form.** It is a sub-class of Lei's one-common-factor model (null $r$, mates of loading $\pm1$, arbitrary means), and is **Lei's Lemma 4.7 verbatim** (decision recorded in `## Adapted from`). The first genuinely new KEY is the monotone-curve lemma for $d\ge1$ (step 6).

The mixed near/far case gives only $n_1\le D^*(w_2)-1$, $\varphi_1\le D^*(w_3)-1$, i.e. $D^*(u_1)\le D^*(w_2)+D^*(w_3)-2$, which contradicts nothing for a flat curve. This is where DCL breaks (next paragraph).

**6d/6b: DCL is false for a deterministic flat staircase curve with two-sign loading-one mates (complete analytic proof + exact numerics).**

*Configuration $\mathcal C(q,r,n,\varepsilon)$.* $\sqrt2-1<r<3/5$ (e.g. $r=0.5$), $q$ small, $U=p^{-1}(q)$, $\varepsilon>0$ small ($\varepsilon<U(r^2+2r-1)/r$), $n\ge1$ with $W:=U+\varepsilon(n-1)\ge U/r$. Ranks $k_1<\dots<k_n=N$ with $t_{k_m}=p(w_m)$, i.e. $u_{k_m}=w_m$ (integer rounding of $Np(w_m)/q$; $N$ so large that they are distinct, $k_1\ge n+2$; the rounded levels differ from $w_m$ by $O(1/k_1)$, below any tolerance used). Curve: $D^*(k)=\min\{k-1,\,n+m(k)\}$, $m(k)=\min\{m:k_m\ge k\}$ ($k\le N$). Then $D^*$ is integer, nondecreasing, increments in $\{0,1\}$ (it increases by $1$ exactly at $k=k_m\to k_m+1$ and along $k-1$ for $k\le n+2$), $D^*\le k-1$, $k_f=1$ ($D^*(1)=0$, $D^*(2)=1$), so $D^*\in\mathcal D_N$; $D^*(k_m)=n+m$. It is realised exactly by the deterministic background $\beta(k)=k-1-D^*(k)$ (step 3: $D=D^*$), so it is a legitimate frozen curve.

*Crossing rule (proved).* By the symmetry $\theta\to-\theta$ of the mates, $F_z=F_{-z}$; take $z\in[0,U]$, $F_z(w)=\#\{j:|\theta_j+z|\ge w\}$. For $w\le z/r+\varepsilon$ every positive mate is counted: $(1+r)w_j+z\ge(1+r)U+z\ge z/r+\varepsilon$ because $(1+r)U-z(1-r)/r-\varepsilon\ge U(r^2+2r-1)/r-\varepsilon>0$. A negative mate has $|\theta^-_j+z|=(1+r)w_j-z$ (as $(1+r)w_j>z$), counted iff $w_j\ge(w+z)/(1+r)$. Since $w_j$ decreases in $j$, $\#\{j:w_j\ge a\}\ge m$ iff $w_m\ge a$. Hence $F_z(w_m)\ge n+m=D^*(k_m)$ iff $w_m\ge(w_m+z)/(1+r)$ iff $w_m\ge z/r$ (for $w_m\le z/r+\varepsilon$). By (R1), $R(z)=k_m$ for the largest $m$ with $F_z(w_m)\ge n+m$. Therefore for $rU\le|z|\le U$: $R(z)=k_{m^*}$, $u(z)=w_{m^*}=\min\{w_m:w_m\ge|z|/r\}\in[|z|/r,|z|/r+\varepsilon)$ (qualifies at $m^*$, fails at every deeper $m$); for $|z|<rU$: level $U$ itself qualifies (all positive mates counted, $(U+|z|)/(1+r)\le U$) so $R=N$. Both signs of $z$ are tilt-matched simultaneously.

*Value.* With $\delta=u(z)-|z|/r\in[0,\varepsilon)$ and the exact identity $(1-r^2)z^2+(u-rz)^2-(1-r^2)u^2=(z-ru)^2$, the Mills bounds $\overline\Phi(x)\ge\frac x{1+x^2}\phi(x)$, $\overline\Phi(u)\le\phi(u)/u$ give $\phi(z)\rho_r(u,z)\ge\frac{1+O(U^{-2})}{2\sqrt{2\pi}\,s}\,e^{-r^2\delta^2/(2s^2)}$ on $rU\le|z|\le U$. The baseline $\int_{|z|\le U}\phi\rho_r(u_{k_f},z)\,dz\to0$ as $N\to\infty$ ($u_{k_f}=u_1\to\infty$). Hence as $\varepsilon\to0$, $N\to\infty$,
$$\Delta^{\rm c}(D^*)\ \ge\ 2\,\frac{(1-r)U}{2\sqrt{2\pi}\,s}\,(1-O(U^{-2}))=2\gamma_r\,\frac{U}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}(1-o(1)),\qquad\gamma_r=\sqrt{\tfrac{1-r}{1+r}}.$$
So $\Delta^{\rm c}/(\gamma_rU/(2\sqrt{2\pi}))\to2$ and $\Delta^{\rm c}/(U/(2\sqrt{2\pi}))\to2\gamma_r\in(1,1.286)$ for $r\in(\sqrt2-1,3/5)$ ($2\gamma_r=1.278$ at $r=0.42$, $1.155$ at $r=0.5$). The displayed DCL inequality (with $\hat g_r=\gamma_r$) is violated by a factor $\to2$, and the weaker target form $\le U/(2\sqrt{2\pi})+\eta U$ is violated for every $r<3/5$ in this range.
